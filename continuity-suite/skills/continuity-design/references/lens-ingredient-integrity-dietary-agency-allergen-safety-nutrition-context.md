# Ingredient integrity, dietary agency, allergen safety, and nutrition context

## Generalized principles

- Test dietary semantics before interface convenience: for every preference, icon, filter, claim and profile field, identify whether it means taste, avoidance, ethical or religious practice, intolerance, immune-mediated allergy, celiac requirement, clinician-directed diet or nutritional goal; define scope and responsible authority; preserve recipient and consequence without diagnosing; distinguish contains, may contain, free-from, unknown and not assessed; and never use lifestyle language, color or a broad vegetarian or healthy label as a proxy for ingredient-level safety.
- Audit ingredient evidence as a versioned safety record: require exact food and variant, responsible manufacturer or food business, location where relevant, complete current ingredient list, regulated allergen source names, precautionary statement, formulation or recipe date, lot or menu version, preparation and contact route; preserve changes and source disagreements; make written evidence available before commitment and at handoff where applicable; and never let a product image, abbreviated contains list, prior order or merchant category overwrite the current physical label and recipe.
- Represent cross-contact as a managed risk, not an ingredient toggle: identify shared equipment, surfaces, utensils, oil, storage, transport and last-mile substitution where material; separate intentional ingredients from unintended possible presence; show the basis and limits of precautionary statements and free-from controls; let the responsible business state when it cannot safely accommodate; avoid blanket warnings that replace risk management; and never claim that removing an ingredient, adding a note or selecting a filter guarantees elimination of allergen exposure.
- Make discovery uncertainty explicit and conservative for safety-sensitive use: disclose filter definition, evidence source, last update, item coverage, excluded fields, customization effects and treatment of missing data; show why each result matched; keep promoted and personalized ranking separate from constraint satisfaction; default unknown items out of strict safety views or label them unmistakably; support accessible text and correction; and never translate a preference match, cuisine label, machine inference or merchant silence into safe-for-you.
- Reassess every material customization and substitution as a new composition: retain base food, additions, removals, replacements, portion, preparation and original constraint set; recompute known ingredients, allergens, nutrition, price and claims; state unresolved cross-contact; compare a replacement by exact package and label rather than category; block known conflicts and require informed approval for unknown or changed evidence; preserve refusal and refund; and never infer equivalence from brand, appearance, popularity, nutrition score or a shopper's judgment.
- Keep nutrition contextual, source-bound and free from moral judgment: show serving size, servings per package, customized quantity, nutrient units, daily-value basis, calculation or source, variability and relevant date; distinguish mandated label values, restaurant estimates and generated recommendations; qualify individual needs; require substantiation for health and safety claims; avoid good-bad food scoring, body ranking, shame and restrictive-engagement loops; and never present calories alone, a wellness aesthetic or personalized target as clinical appropriateness.
- Connect digital information to accountable human food preparation: identify the manufacturer, kitchen, picker or other business responsible for the exact food; provide a trained, accessible and language-appropriate route for questions; transmit the exact recipient, item, restriction and confirmed response into preparation and handoff; record acknowledgment without implying certainty beyond evidence; support an honest cannot-accommodate outcome; and never send allergy questions to a platform generalist or treat unacknowledged free text as operational consent.
- Protect dietary and health agency across people and contexts: collect only needed constraints, keep multiple recipients distinct, support temporary and order-specific settings, disclose sharing with restaurants, shoppers or recommenders, prevent sensitive-health inference and unrelated marketing, allow correction and deletion, and preserve cultural and affordable options; do not pressure people to reveal diagnosis or severity to receive basic information; and never let one household profile silently apply to every diner or turn restriction data into moralized personalization.
- Design handoff and incident response around exact evidence and appropriate urgency: label each item and recipient, preserve final composition, substitutions, preparation acknowledgment, package and lot, receipt and custody; allow inspection before acceptance; support reports of wrong food, missing disclosure, unexpected ingredient, cross-contact concern or reaction; clearly direct urgent medical situations outside ordinary support without delaying action; retain evidence, notify responsible actors and correct affected catalog records; and never collapse a potential safety event into a refund or review prompt.
- Audit food-choice success against safety, agency and inclusion rather than conversion alone: measure ingredient and allergen completeness, data freshness, unknown-item treatment, filter false assurance, customization recalculation, substitution conflicts, acknowledgment, cannot-accommodate clarity, recipient and handoff accuracy, label mismatch, incidents, correction time, accessibility, language parity, food access and health-claim comprehension; include people who abandon, refuse replacements or cannot be accommodated; and never optimize order completion, recommendation clicks, calorie reduction or substitution acceptance without these outcomes.

## Method

- Trace one food from catalog or recipe source through discovery, detail, customization, preparation, substitution, fulfillment, handoff and incident correction.
- Separate consumer, recipient, caregiver, platform, merchant, manufacturer, kitchen, picker, courier, certifier and clinician.
- Reconstruct food identity, ingredients, intentional allergens, possible cross-contact, claims, serving-based nutrition and every composition change.
- Walk severe allergy, intolerance, celiac, clinical diet, religious, ethical, cultural, low-literacy, disabled, multilingual and multi-person scenarios.
- Test stale label, changed recipe, unknown data, customization, shared oil, replacement, mixed-up handoff, missing acknowledgment, wrong package and adverse reaction.
- Compare displayed confidence with the evidence, responsible actor and actual operational controls supporting it.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not describe preference filtering as allergen or clinical safety.
- Do not treat missing ingredient or cross-contact data as absence.
- Do not claim ingredient removal eliminates derivatives or shared-preparation exposure.
- Do not use icons, color or images as the only dietary or allergen evidence.
- Do not substitute a food under safety-relevant constraints without exact evidence and appropriate approval.
- Do not route food-safety questions only to generic marketplace support.
- Do not moralize food, body, calories or adherence in recommendations.
- Do not delay urgent-care direction while completing platform support or refund steps.

## Variation levers

- Increase exact label, lot and supplier provenance for packaged foods and changed formulations.
- Increase recipe, kitchen, staff and cross-contact controls for prepared and non-prepacked foods.
- Increase physical-item verification and replacement constraints for shopper-mediated grocery fulfillment.
- Increase certification identity and scope for regulated free-from, religious and ethical claims.
- Increase recipient separation, caregiver controls, language support and emergency evidence for severe or dependent-care contexts.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Complete evidence versus rapid menu scanning and ordering.
- Personalized convenience versus sensitive dietary-data privacy.
- Flexible customization versus reproducible preparation and current calculations.
- Written labels versus the need for responsible human confirmation.
- Precautionary warnings versus meaningful availability and non-exclusion.
- Nutrition guidance versus individual variation, clinical limits and eating-disorder risk.

## Failure modes

- A lifestyle tag is interpreted as medical safety.
- Old or incomplete catalog data overrides the current package.
- A removal control hides preparation cross-contact.
- Unknown items remain in strict dietary-filter results.
- A replacement inherits approval despite changed ingredients.
- Calories or wellness language become moralized health advice.
- An allergy note never reaches a responsible food operator.
- A potential reaction is handled as customer dissatisfaction.

## Anti-patterns

- Lifestyle-to-safety leap.
- Catalog-over-label.
- Cross-contact erasure.
- Unknown-safe default.
- Inherited substitution consent.
- Nutrition morality score.
- Marketplace allergy mailbox.
- Food incident as support sentiment.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are preference, ethics, religion, intolerance, allergy, celiac, clinical diet and nutrition goals explicitly distinguished?
- Can current food identity, ingredients, allergen sources, precautionary information and responsible business be verified?
- Does the experience explain cross-contact controls and uncertainty without offering a false guarantee?
- Do discovery filters expose evidence, freshness, unknown treatment, match rationale and customization limits?
- Are customized and substituted foods reassessed as new compositions under inherited constraints?
- Is nutrition serving-based, source-bound, qualified and free from moral or clinical overreach?
- Can a trained responsible operator acknowledge the exact recipient, food question and response accessibly?
- Are dietary profiles minimal, revisable, recipient-specific and protected from unrelated use?
- Does handoff preserve exact evidence and does incident response distinguish urgent care from platform remedy?
- Do outcome measures cover safety, uncertainty, correction, inclusion and access beyond conversion?
