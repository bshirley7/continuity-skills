# Cinematic

## Generalized principles

- Treat immersion as a bounded layer around a legible task or story: retain title, current unit, progress, playback state, navigation, next action, and an immediate exit.
- Bind every cinematic hero to decision context through identity, source, metadata, suitability, scope, and a concrete action rather than relying on atmosphere alone.
- Keep media controllable through visible play or pause, captions or text alternatives, volume where relevant, skip or close, and reduced dependence on timed transitions.
- Use staged scale, depth, and sequence to create pacing while preserving neighboring context, carousel or step position, stable navigation, and direct movement through the narrative.
- For consequential or emotional experiences, connect imagery to provenance, measurable state, safeguards, exact commitment, and recovery so emotion cannot substitute for evidence.
- In creative and media tools, preserve exact content identity and workflow actions—play, save, download, share, organize, or continue—inside the immersive frame.

## Variation levers

- Use full-screen video for guided demonstration and full-bleed stills for discovery or content identity.
- Use captions and task sheets when media explains an action.
- Use restrained editorial scale when motion is unnecessary.
- Use carousels only when position and direct navigation are visible.
- Reduce cinematic intensity at commitment, configuration, and recovery boundaries.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Immersion increases emotional focus while reducing orientation.
- Large media strengthens identity while consuming decision space.
- Timed pacing supports narrative while restricting user control.
- Dark framing improves image impact while weakening text contrast.
- Emotional imagery motivates action while risking evidence displacement.

## Failure modes

- Media starts without visible control or escape.
- A hero omits identity, metadata, or concrete action.
- Narrative sequence hides progress or neighboring context.
- Spoken content lacks captions or text equivalent.
- Cinematic transitions delay repeat work.
- Emotional imagery outranks provenance, safeguards, or commitment details.
- Playback is not bound to the exact content unit.

## Anti-patterns

- Unskippable introductions.
- Autoplay with hidden audio control.
- Full-screen media with no title.
- Mystery-meat carousels.
- Scroll-jacking narrative.
- Low-contrast text over imagery.
- Emotion-only fundraising appeals.
- Ambient video at transactional boundaries.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can users identify the current task or story unit, progress, next action, and exit?
- Does each hero retain identity, source, metadata, scope, and action?
- Are playback, captions, volume, skip, close, and reduced-motion needs addressed?
- Is narrative position and direct movement visible?
- Do emotional experiences preserve provenance, safeguards, measurable state, and commitment?
- Are exact content identity and workflow actions available inside the frame?
