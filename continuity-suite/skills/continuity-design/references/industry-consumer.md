# Consumer

## Generalized principles

- Deliver immediate value before demanding extensive setup by combining recent activity, starter content, clear categories, search, and saved or owned-library routes, then layer personalization as evidence accumulates.
- Make personalization inspectable and controllable by naming the signal or basis, separating recommendations from recent and saved content, and retaining direct search, category, history, notification, and library controls.
- At transaction completion, preserve order identity, date, item or service, contact, destination, payment method, full amount, every fee, authorization-versus-charge state, fulfillment timing, and the route to track, change, cancel, or get help.
- Treat the receipt as the start of fulfillment rather than an endpoint: explain readiness, delivery or pickup conditions, notifications, identity requirements, hold periods, support, and any pending payment or loading state.
- Make subscription state self-contained through plan type, price, billing cadence, renewal or pause date, payment method, history, included capabilities, usage or retained value, and direct pause, resume, change, and cancellation actions.
- Offer retention alternatives only when they genuinely map to a user need and disclose their effect on charges, access, holdings, orders, progress, and future cancellation; keep cancellation visible and independently available.
- Preserve creative work as a draft and present preview, cover, metadata, audience, privacy, comments, reuse, moderation, saving, and external distribution as separately understandable choices before publication.
- Name audience reach in concrete terms and explain moderation or participation consequences, distinguishing who can view, who can comment, who can reuse, where copies are saved, and where content is distributed.
- Keep primary consumer tasks reachable through stable navigation and one clear contextual action, while moving infrequent preferences and advanced controls into discoverable secondary layers without hiding consequential choices.
- Design repeat engagement around user-controlled continuity—recent activity, saved content, owned collections, history, notification preferences, and resumable drafts—rather than artificial urgency or blocked exit.

## Variation levers

- Use starter collections when history is sparse, recent activity for continuity, and explained recommendations when preference evidence is mature.
- Use bottom navigation for a small set of frequent destinations and contextual sheets for bounded audience, privacy, or settings choices.
- Use a concise confirmation for low-risk digital delivery and a detailed receipt for physical fulfillment, fees, pickup, or delayed charging.
- Use direct cancellation for simple memberships and separate pause, order snooze, care, or support routes when they solve materially different needs.
- Use automatic drafts for creation, explicit publish for distribution, and per-capability switches for comments, reuse, saving, and external sharing.
- Use named communities or contacts for bounded audiences and an explicit global label for broad publication.
- Use notification and history controls near personalized content when those systems materially shape repeat engagement.
- Use benefit lists for entitlement clarity and usage or retained-value evidence only when it is current and decision-relevant.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Personalization can reduce discovery effort while narrowing exposure and obscuring ranking logic.
- Immediate value reduces setup friction while producing less relevant first-use content.
- Detailed receipts improve trust while increasing post-purchase density.
- Persistent fulfillment messaging supports recovery while prolonging transactional anxiety.
- Pause and snooze options preserve flexibility while complicating lifecycle understanding.
- Benefit and retained-value reminders clarify consequences while becoming coercive if cancellation is de-emphasized.
- Rich publishing controls protect agency while increasing the burden before sharing.
- Repeat-engagement features support continuity while encouraging excessive attention when not user-controlled.

## Failure modes

- A first-use home withholds useful content until extensive preference or account setup is complete.
- Recommendations have no visible basis, search, history, saved-content, or preference controls.
- A confirmation omits order identity, full amount, fees, payment state, destination, timing, or support.
- A receipt declares success while item or fulfillment detail is still unverifiable.
- Subscription management hides price, cadence, renewal, payment, history, entitlements, or cancellation.
- A retention alternative changes charges or access without explaining the effect.
- Publishing lacks draft preservation, preview, audience, privacy, moderation, reuse, or distribution review.
- Audience and participation settings use vague labels that do not explain actual reach.
- Consequential consumer controls are buried with infrequent preferences.
- Engagement relies on urgency, blocked exit, or uncontrolled notifications instead of resumable value.

## Anti-patterns

- Requiring many interests before showing any useful content.
- Presenting a personalized feed with no search or library route.
- Showing an order total without itemized fees.
- Calling checkout complete while order details remain placeholders.
- Hiding cancellation behind support contact.
- Offering pause without stating when charging resumes.
- Publishing immediately with no draft or audience review.
- Using public or friends without explaining reach and reuse.
- Placing cancellation or privacy only in an advanced menu.
- Using countdowns or repeated prompts to manufacture return behavior.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can users receive useful value before extensive setup, with recent, search, category, and saved-content paths?
- Is personalization basis visible and controllable through history, preferences, notifications, search, and library?
- Does transaction completion preserve identity, item, contact, destination, payment, amount, fees, timing, and recovery?
- Does the receipt explain fulfillment, notification, identity, hold, support, and pending payment conditions?
- Are plan, price, cadence, renewal or pause, payment, history, capabilities, and lifecycle actions visible together?
- Do retention alternatives disclose their exact effect while preserving direct cancellation?
- Are draft, preview, metadata, audience, privacy, comments, reuse, moderation, saving, and distribution reviewed before publish?
- Do audience and participation controls explain concrete reach and downstream use?
- Are frequent tasks and consequential choices reachable without overloading the primary interface?
- Does repeat engagement come from user-controlled continuity rather than urgency, blocked exit, or uncontrolled notifications?
