# Procedural fairness and service recovery

## Generalized principles

- Give the affected person meaningful voice before deciding: make the route easy to find and use, support suitable channels and representation, let them describe what happened, impact, evidence, access needs, and sought outcome in their own terms, acknowledge every material issue, and provide a fair opportunity to correct the record or respond to adverse information.
- Make handling neutral, consistent, proportionate, and accountable through published scope and standards, disclosed roles and authority, conflict-aware assignment, criteria grounded in applicable policy and evidence, a complete retrievable record, privacy protection, and review by someone sufficiently independent when the original handling or decision is contested.
- Keep the person oriented through an accessible receipt, stable reference, captured issues, owner or responsible team, current state and next actor, response expectation, material delay or transfer, requests for information, and concise programmatically available updates; do not make repeated contact the mechanism for discovering progress.
- Explain outcomes with respect and specificity: address each material issue, identify the facts and evidence considered, applicable policy or standard, finding and reasons, action already taken and still promised, timing, limitations, responsible owner, and available correction, complaint, appeal, escalation, or independent review route.
- Make recovery proportional to the actual harm and verify it: stop continuing harm, restore the person as nearly as practical, correct records or access, refund or compensate where appropriate, acknowledge and apologize without deflection, explain prevention work, confirm completion with evidence, preserve reopening, and measure both fair process and durable outcome rather than deflection or closure alone.

## Method

- Map entry routes, supported channels, representation, language and disability needs, identity and consent, issue capture, evidence, desired outcome, urgency, and safe alternatives.
- Define case receipt, reference, ownership, conflicts, lifecycle states, next actor, response standards, information requests, transfers, delay communication, records, privacy, and audit.
- Specify fact finding, adverse-information response, policy and evidence criteria, decision authority, reason writing, proportionality, and independent review conditions.
- Define remedies for stopping harm, restoration, correction, refund or compensation, acknowledgment, apology, prevention, completion evidence, verification, reopening, and appeal.
- Test with people facing low trust, disability, language barriers, distress, intermittent access, delegated representation, missing evidence, repeated transfer, adverse decisions, delay, and incomplete remedies.
- Measure successful access, first meaningful response, repeat explanation, transfers, abandonment, issue acknowledgment, expectation accuracy, remedy correctness and completion, reopen, review changes, accessibility, subgroup disparities, and learning applied.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not hide a complaint or human-support route behind automation, search, account closure, paid tiers, or repeated self-service loops.
- Do not penalize, deprioritize, threaten, shame, or retaliate against a person for complaining, escalating, requesting accommodation, using representation, or rejecting an inadequate remedy.
- Do not optimize for deflection, handle time, closure, satisfaction prompts, or low escalation while ignoring abandonment, repeated contact, unresolved harm, unfair outcomes, or subgroup disparities.
- Do not mark a case resolved from a sent response, offered article, user silence, expired timer, or internal task completion when the promised action or remedy is incomplete.
- Do not demand unnecessary credentials, diagnostic data, identity evidence, public disclosure, or repeated proof already held by the service.
- Do not make feedback, withdrawal, confidentiality beyond legitimate need, or waiver of review a condition of receiving an owed correction or remedy.

## Variation levers

- Increase formality, independence, written reasons, evidence controls, review rights, and remedy verification with consequence, vulnerability, dispute, or power imbalance.
- Use informal rapid recovery for simple reversible failures and a durable formal case when harm, recurrence, disagreement, delay, regulation, or requested review makes history important.
- Offer asynchronous, synchronous, assisted, represented, translated, and accessible channels according to need while preserving one case record.
- Use immediate operational correction, financial restoration, nonfinancial acknowledgment, preventive change, or a combined remedy according to the nature and duration of harm.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Fast informal recovery reduces burden while bypassing durable rights, reasons, or review.
- Consistent standards support fairness while rigid scripts ignore individual circumstances and access needs.
- Complete records support accountability while excessive collection increases privacy and security risk.
- Independent review strengthens legitimacy while adding time during continuing harm.
- Apology and explanation support repair while becoming hollow when corrective action is absent.

## Failure modes

- The service offers contact but no usable complaint, escalation, assisted, or represented route.
- The person's account, evidence, desired outcome, or response to adverse information is omitted from the decision.
- Ownership, state, next action, expectation, delay, and transfer are invisible or inaccessible.
- A decision provides a conclusion without the issues, facts, evidence, policy, reasons, authority, or review path.
- A remedy is promised without owner, timing, completion evidence, verification, or reopening.
- Success is reported from deflection or closure while repeat contacts, abandonment, unresolved harm, and disparities remain unmeasured.

## Anti-patterns

- Complaint route as maze.
- Automation as gatekeeper.
- No chance to be heard.
- Conclusion without reasons.
- Transfer erases accountability.
- Closure is the metric.
- Apology without repair.
- Review starts from zero.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can the person find and use an appropriate, accessible, represented route and explain the issue, impact, evidence, needs, and desired outcome?
- Does handling disclose scope, standards, roles, authority, conflicts, evidence criteria, records, privacy, and independent review where warranted?
- Can the person inspect receipt, reference, acknowledged issues, owner, state, next actor, expectation, delays, transfers, and requests in an accessible form?
- Does the outcome address every material issue with facts, evidence, policy, finding, reasons, action, timing, owner, limitations, and review routes?
- Does the remedy stop harm, restore or correct, compensate where appropriate, acknowledge, prevent recurrence, prove completion, verify outcome, and preserve reopening?
- Do measures cover access, meaningful response, repeated explanation, transfers, abandonment, fair process, remedy completion, reopen, review changes, accessibility, disparities, and learning?
