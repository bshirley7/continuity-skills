# Feedback validity, power asymmetry, and reputational remedy

## Generalized principles

- Define the construct and eligible population before collecting feedback: state whether the measure concerns satisfaction, recommendation, quality, fit, reliability, safety or a specific interaction; bind it to a genuine and relevant experience; preserve version, time and transaction context; distinguish absence of feedback from neutral judgment; and do not reuse a rating for a materially different product, provider, variant or decision.
- Treat solicitation as a source of measurement bias: document who is asked, when, how often, after which outcomes and through which channel; make decline and later response possible; avoid prompting only satisfied people, gating criticism, conditioning incentives on sentiment or interrupting people before meaningful use; compare respondents with the eligible population; and interpret increased review volume as changed sampling until evidence shows otherwise.
- Reduce retaliation and dependency effects in two-sided systems: delay or simultaneously reveal feedback when seeing the other party's judgment could change expression, separate public review from private safety and operational reports, minimize unnecessary identity exposure, prohibit threats and review extortion, prevent one side from conditioning service or remedy on favorable feedback, and provide protected reporting and human recourse when the reviewed party controls access, housing, care, work or income.
- Preserve authenticity without overstating verification: require genuine experience, disclose material connections and incentives, distinguish transaction-verified from identity-verified or fact-verified, detect coordinated, duplicated, synthetic, hijacked and insider content, apply comparable checks to positive and negative reviews, label imports and translations, and explain the limits and error modes of automated detection rather than presenting a verified badge as proof that every claim is true.
- Make aggregation statistically and semantically legible: show count, distribution, eligible denominator when known, recency, version and category context; disclose weighting, resets and exclusions; avoid false precision; use freshness without erasing historical change; flag sparse or polarized evidence; keep written reviews available; and ensure generated summaries identify their synthetic status, cover material negative and minority themes, refresh predictably and remain traceable to source evidence.
- Design rating and review controls for equivalent expression: give scales visible semantic labels, make selection perceivable beyond color and stars, support keyboard and assistive operation, announce errors and publication state, allow sufficient target size and text entry, localize scale direction and language without changing meaning, support correction, and ensure negative, mixed, not-applicable and private-safety paths are no harder to reach than praise.
- Separate moderation, response and adjudication powers: publish rules and collection methods, preserve the full picture of genuine relevant feedback, give authors specific removal or delay reasons and an edit or appeal path, let subjects respond or report policy violations without vetoing criticism, prevent responses from disclosing personal information or intimidating authors, record changes, and do not imply that content moderation has proven or disproven a contested real-world event.
- Apply procedural safeguards when reputation becomes an allocation mechanism: identify every use in ranking, badges, pricing, access, matching, deactivation or work distribution; evaluate comparability, bias, manipulation, sample sufficiency and temporal relevance; limit the effect of anomalous feedback; provide notice and an intelligible explanation; allow evidence correction and contextual response; require proportionate human review before serious loss; and audit error and outcome disparities for both reviewers and reviewed people.

## Method

- Define the feedback construct, object, eligible population, transaction or experience basis, timing, audience and downstream uses.
- Map solicitation and nonresponse, then test whether prompting, incentives, channel and timing distort who responds or what they say.
- Design protected public, private, safety and complaint paths with labeled scales, accessible composition and retaliation controls.
- Specify authenticity checks, incentive disclosures, aggregation, distributions, weighting, freshness, summaries and uncertainty.
- Define symmetric moderation, subject response, author correction, reporting, removal reasons, appeal and privacy boundaries.
- Audit downstream reputation decisions for manipulation, bias, sufficiency, proportionality, explanation, correction and human recourse.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not interpret nonresponse as satisfaction, neutrality or consent.
- Do not solicit only after favorable outcomes or make support contingent on withholding criticism.
- Do not expose reviewer identity where dependency or retaliation risk is material without a compelling disclosed need.
- Do not call a review fact-verified merely because a transaction or account was verified.
- Do not hide genuine negative evidence through moderation, ordering, excerpts or summaries.
- Do not let a provider response disclose personal information, threaten or pressure the author.
- Do not remove disputed criticism solely because the subject denies the experience.
- Do not use a sparse, stale, biased or incomparable score to impose serious loss without review and appeal.
- Do not optimize review count or average score while ignoring honesty, representativeness, correction and harm.

## Variation levers

- Increase delayed publication, privacy, protected reporting and independent review with retaliation, dependency and livelihood risk.
- Increase distribution and uncertainty disclosure as samples shrink, offerings change or decisions become consequential.
- Use verified-transaction eligibility for experience claims while preserving distinct channels for pre-transaction discrimination, accessibility and safety concerns.
- Require more contextual dimensions for heterogeneous services and fewer dimensions when reviewers cannot judge them reliably.
- Shorten the effect of old feedback when conditions change while retaining visible history and explaining the weighting.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- More prompts increase feedback supply while changing the respondent population and emotional timing.
- Verification reduces some fraud while excluding real experiences and creating false confidence.
- Public identity may deter abuse while also deterring criticism from dependent or vulnerable people.
- Recency weighting reflects improvement while allowing history to disappear without accountability.
- Automated summaries improve comprehension while concentrating selection and omission power.
- Provider response supports repair while increasing intimidation and privacy risk.

## Failure modes

- The metric name is clear but the underlying construct and eligible population are undefined.
- Only successful or satisfied users receive the prompt.
- A reciprocal system reveals one party's review before the other commits.
- A verified badge is read as validation of every factual claim.
- A high average hides a tiny, stale or polarized sample.
- A generated summary omits recurring negative or minority experiences.
- Moderation removes criticism while leaving equivalently unsupported praise.
- A public response exposes account, health, location or transaction details.
- A reputation score silently controls opportunity with no explanation or appeal.

## Anti-patterns

- Undefined feedback construct.
- Happy-path sampling.
- Retaliatory unveiling.
- Verification theater.
- Average without evidence shape.
- Synthetic consensus.
- Asymmetric moderation.
- Privacy-breaking response.
- Invisible reputation governor.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are the construct, object, eligible population, experience basis, timing, audience and downstream uses explicitly defined?
- Has solicitation bias been assessed across who is prompted, timing, outcome, incentives, channel, decline and nonresponse?
- Do delayed publication, privacy, protected reporting and recourse match retaliation, dependency and livelihood risk?
- Are genuine experience, material connections, transaction verification, claim truth and automated detection limits clearly distinguished?
- Do aggregates expose count, denominator, distribution, recency, version, weighting, sparsity, polarization and summary provenance?
- Can all people express positive, negative, mixed, not-applicable and safety feedback accessibly and correct it later?
- Are moderation, provider response, factual dispute, privacy, correction, removal and appeal powers separated and symmetric?
- Are reputation-driven allocation decisions proportionate, explainable, bias-audited, correctable and subject to human appeal?
