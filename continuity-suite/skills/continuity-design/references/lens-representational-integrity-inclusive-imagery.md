# Representational integrity and inclusive imagery

## Generalized principles

- Classify an image by purpose in its exact context before styling or describing it: decorative imagery contributes no required information and should be suppressible; informative imagery needs an equivalent for its relevant meaning; functional imagery needs an action or destination name; complex imagery needs an adequate extended representation; identity and evidence imagery need context, provenance and higher scrutiny; and the same asset may require a different classification when its use changes.
- Make the experience equivalent, not merely technically labeled: write alternatives from purpose and surrounding context, avoid repeating visible text or announcing decorative assets, expose data or structure behind complex visuals, preserve control names and state, support zoom, reflow, contrast and assistive settings, and test the image-hidden, failed, low-bandwidth, high-contrast and nonvisual paths for the same task outcome.
- Evaluate representation as a system rather than selecting isolated diverse assets: review who appears, who acts, who receives help, which bodies, disabilities, ages, families, occupations, cultures, places and economic settings recur, how subjects are cropped and captioned and whether difference is contextual or tokenized; avoid stereotype, deficit framing, exoticism and invented identity; and include intended communities in review when portrayal can affect dignity or access.
- Treat images of people as potentially sensitive data, not neutral decoration: establish a lawful and understandable purpose, consent or other valid basis, minimization, access, retention, removal and secondary-use boundaries; distinguish display from biometric inference or identity verification; do not infer protected, emotional or behavioral traits from appearance; and increase notice, choice, security, testing and human recourse as identifiability and consequence increase.
- Separate what an image depicts from what its history can support: label representative, illustrative, edited, composite, generated and documentary uses where confusion is material; preserve available creator, source, time and modification provenance; make inspection proportionate to consequence; treat credentials and signatures as evidence about origin and change rather than proof that the depicted claim is true; and communicate absent or broken provenance without declaring the content false.
- Design responsive art direction to preserve meaning: define focal point, safe area, acceptable crops, aspect ratios, overlay zones, theme variants, resolution, color profile and detail access; keep essential subjects, labels and evidence visible across viewport, zoom and display modes; provide sufficient text and non-text contrast; and replace or retune assets when a mechanical crop, tint or dark-mode treatment changes interpretation or legibility.
- Govern the full image lifecycle with accountable human review: record purpose, owner, source, creator, rights, consent, subjects, sensitivity, provenance, transformations, generation method, alternative representation, focal point, locale, moderation, retention and replacement status; review changes as content changes rather than cosmetic swaps; support correction, withdrawal and removal; and audit accessibility, representation, truth claims and privacy across the library and real interface contexts.

## Method

- Inventory each image use by contextual purpose, claim, subject, interaction, consequence and whether the task remains possible without vision or successful asset delivery.
- Specify the visible context, concise or extended alternative, state, provenance, disclosure and inspection needed for that purpose.
- Review representation across the complete set for inclusion, stereotypes, dignity, cropping, captions, localization and community impact.
- Assess privacy, identifiability, consent, biometric or trait inference, secondary use, retention and removal before collecting or displaying people imagery.
- Test responsive crops, themes, overlays, contrast, zoom, loading, failure, low bandwidth and assistive paths on representative devices and content.
- Record ownership and lifecycle metadata, then audit released imagery and replacements for accessibility, integrity, privacy and representational drift.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not use a visual-only alternative for information or function that must be perceivable nonvisually.
- Do not write alternative text as a literal inventory when the needed equivalent is purpose, action, state or data.
- Do not treat one visibly diverse campaign image as evidence that the broader representation system is inclusive.
- Do not infer identity, emotion, health, character, intent or protected traits from appearance.
- Do not reuse a person's image beyond the disclosed purpose or retention boundary.
- Do not present provenance credentials as proof that the depicted event or claim is true.
- Do not imply that missing provenance proves manipulation or falsehood.
- Do not allow automatic crops, overlays or theme treatments to hide subjects, evidence or readable labels.
- Do not replace or remove governed imagery without preserving correction, consent and audit history where appropriate.

## Variation levers

- Increase equivalent detail, provenance, inspection and human review with functional importance, complexity, sensitivity and consequence.
- Use concise alternatives for simple informative images and structured extended representations for charts, diagrams, maps and evidentiary media.
- Increase privacy controls and reduce collection, resolution, retention and reuse as people become more identifiable or biometric processing becomes possible.
- Use more literal and source-specific imagery when the task depends on material reality; use clearly labeled illustration when abstraction, privacy or explanation is safer.
- Require community or domain review when imagery concerns vulnerable populations, health, identity, public events, regulated claims or cultural meaning.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- A vivid image can improve comprehension for some people while creating a complete barrier for others without an equivalent.
- Specific human imagery can create recognition and trust while increasing privacy, bias and dignity risk.
- Aggressive cropping creates consistent layouts while changing evidence and representation.
- Provenance signals improve transparency while encouraging false certainty about truth or false suspicion when absent.
- A globally consistent visual system reduces production cost while flattening cultural and contextual meaning.

## Failure modes

- Every image receives generic alternative text regardless of purpose and context.
- Decorative assets create assistive noise while meaningful images remain undescribed.
- Representation is reviewed one asset at a time and systemic stereotypes remain invisible.
- A portrait is used for biometric inference, verification or reuse beyond the person's expectation.
- Generated, edited or illustrative imagery is allowed to read as documentary proof.
- A valid credential is interpreted as validation of the content's truth.
- A crop or overlay removes the only distinguishing or evidentiary feature.
- Rights, consent, alternatives, provenance and removal have no accountable owner.

## Anti-patterns

- Alt text by visual inventory.
- Decorative assistive noise.
- Diversity by single asset.
- Portrait as permission.
- Synthetic documentary ambiguity.
- Credential equals truth.
- Meaning-destroying crop.
- Ownerless image governance.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is each image classified by contextual purpose, with decorative, informative, functional, complex, identity and evidence obligations distinguished?
- Can people reach the same task outcome through appropriate alternatives, contrast, zoom, reflow, assistive and failure paths?
- Has representation been reviewed across the complete system for inclusion, stereotype, dignity, cropping, captioning and cultural context?
- Are people images governed as potentially identifiable or biometric data with purpose, consent, minimization, security, retention and recourse?
- Are depiction, illustration, editing, generation, provenance, verification and truth claims explicitly separated?
- Do responsive crops, safe areas, overlays, themes, resolution and detail access preserve meaning and legibility?
- Are purpose, rights, consent, provenance, transformation, alternatives, locale, moderation, retention, correction and ownership recorded and audited?
