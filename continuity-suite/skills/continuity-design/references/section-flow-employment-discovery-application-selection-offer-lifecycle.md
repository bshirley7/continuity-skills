# Employment discovery, application, selection, and offer lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Preserve one versioned opportunity identity from discovery through closure: keep employer, requisition or role ID, title, location, work arrangement, employment type, compensation basis, schedule, start expectations, essential qualifications, sponsorship or authorization constraints, posted and closing dates, and material changes inspectable; bind every application, assessment, message, decision and offer to that identity; notify consequential changes; and never silently evaluate a person against terms they did not see.
- Separate discovery, recommendation, eligibility, application, assessment, interview, selection, offer and employment states: explain what each state means, which evidence produced it, who owns the next action, expected timing and available correction or exit; do not represent a match score, completed form, assessment result, recruiter view, interview or verbal intent as an offer or final decision.
- Make applicant effort proportional and reusable without hidden substitution: group every requested field, document, profile import and question by purpose; show required versus optional information and who receives it; preserve drafts; let people inspect, update or replace reused evidence for this role; prevent stale profile data from silently overriding submitted material; and never require full account enrichment when a smaller role-specific submission is sufficient.
- Treat each assessment and interview as a declared selection instrument: state the competency or job task, format, duration, timing rule, technology, monitoring or recording, evaluator, scoring role, preparation, practice, accessibility and accommodation route, reschedule or interruption policy, data use, retention, result timing and next stage; preserve equivalent alternatives where possible; and never disguise a consequential evaluation as a casual chat, game or profile enhancement.
- Maintain a candidate-controlled status record after submission: show the exact material sent, submitted time, current stage, last meaningful update, responsible organization, expected next event, communication history, interview details, requested action, withdrawal and support routes; distinguish no update from rejection and employer review from automated processing; close stale or filled roles honestly; and do not force applicants to infer status from silence.
- Make verification and adverse evidence inspectable before it controls the outcome: identify the check, provider, scope, timing, consent, source and relationship to the role; separate identity, work authorization, credentials, references, criminal or financial reports and medical or post-offer processes; provide accessible correction and dispute routes; preserve interim state; and never ask for broader identity or sensitive evidence earlier than the applicable decision requires.
- Represent an offer as an exact, reviewable and revisable employment proposition: show employer and worker entities, role, manager or team where relevant, location, remote expectations, employment or contractor classification, compensation and currency, pay period, hours, benefits, leave, equity or variable-pay conditions, start date, contingencies, governing jurisdiction, documents, expiry and contacts; preserve negotiation and corrected versions; separate acceptance from onboarding; and issue a durable receipt of the exact accepted terms.
- Close every candidacy with agency and bounded retention: distinguish applicant withdrawal, employer rejection, role cancellation, filled role, expired application, offer decline and accepted offer; provide specific stage-appropriate notice and correction or review where consequential evidence may be wrong; explain whether the profile or application remains in a talent pool, for what purpose and duration; allow separate consent, deletion or future-contact control where applicable; and never convert rejection into indefinite marketing or opaque surveillance.

## Variation levers

- Increase requisition identity, auditability and structured stage controls for large or regulated employers.
- Increase portfolio, proposal and negotiated-term support for freelance and project work.
- Increase jurisdiction, currency, classification and document clarity for cross-border employment.
- Increase accommodation, assisted completion and alternative assessment routes for high-burden selection.
- Increase correction, human review and outcome monitoring when automation ranks, advances or rejects candidates.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Fast profile reuse versus stale or over-broad evidence.
- Recruiter flexibility versus consistent and job-related selection.
- Applicant visibility versus exposure of private reviewer deliberation.
- Fraud prevention versus premature identity and sensitive-data collection.
- Automation efficiency versus meaningful explanation, correction and human review.
- Talent-pool reuse versus purpose limitation and indefinite retention.

## Failure modes

- A role changes after submission without notice.
- A match score is presented as eligibility or selection.
- Profile reuse silently submits stale evidence.
- An assessment hides purpose, monitoring or accommodation.
- Applicants must infer rejection from prolonged silence.
- Background information controls a decision before correction.
- A verbal or changing offer lacks versioned exact terms.
- Rejected applications become an indefinite marketing database.

## Anti-patterns

- Mutable requisition trap.
- Match-as-merit score.
- Invisible resume substitution.
- Mystery assessment.
- Ghosted status tracker.
- Verify-first overcollection.
- Offer ambiguity theater.
- Perpetual candidate pool.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can the applicant inspect the exact opportunity and every material change across the lifecycle?
- Are recommendation, eligibility, application, assessment, selection and offer represented as distinct states?
- Is every data request proportional, purpose-bound, reviewable and correctable?
- Are assessment purpose, burden, monitoring, accommodation and decision role explicit?
- Does submission create a durable, current and candidate-controlled status record?
- Can verification evidence be inspected and corrected before it controls an outcome?
- Are offer terms exact, versioned, negotiable and durably receipted?
- Does closure explain state, remedy and retention without converting applicants into an indefinite audience?
