# Guided narrative

## Generalized principles

- Give the narrative a visible thesis and map—current context, stage labels, progress or chapter structure, and a clear destination—so readers always know where they are and why the next stage matters.
- Make every stage independently useful with a descriptive heading, concise claim, mechanism or example, and optional deeper path so scanning and skipping do not destroy comprehension.
- Sequence transformation through current state, cause, changed mechanism, adoption or handoffs, resulting state, evidence, and remaining constraints rather than presenting an effortless leap.
- Bind visuals, examples, and proof to the exact narrative stage and claim they support; preserve equivalent text and controls when motion, stickiness, or scroll effects are unavailable.
- Place consequential terms, safeguards, uncertainty, eligibility, fees, and limits before the action they govern, even when doing so interrupts the emotional arc.
- Offer multiple exits matched to reader intent—skip to proof, inspect details, compare approaches, view documentation, begin a bounded task, or contact an expert—without forcing completion of the entire story.

## Variation levers

- Use a compact chapter sequence for familiar products and deeper staged explanation for complex change.
- Use sticky visuals only when they materially demonstrate stage-to-stage change.
- Let technical readers branch into documentation while preserving a plain-language main path.
- Bring proof forward at the stage where skepticism naturally arises.
- Use a persistent or repeated action only when its meaning remains appropriate across stages.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Narrative control improves coherence while reducing reader agency.
- Sticky motion creates continuity while adding accessibility and performance risk.
- Progressive depth serves mixed audiences while duplicating explanation.
- Emotional storytelling increases relevance while risking evidentiary overreach.
- Repeated calls to action improve availability while becoming pressure.

## Failure modes

- The reader cannot identify the current stage.
- A stage depends on earlier copy that skimmers will miss.
- Visuals are decorative or lag the explanation.
- Proof is detached from the claim it supports.
- Scroll effects hide content or remove keyboard access.
- Consequential terms appear only after the emotional climax.
- The only exit is a sales commitment.

## Anti-patterns

- Scroll-jacking.
- Mystery chapter labels.
- One sentence split across many screens.
- Sticky visual with no semantic change.
- Evidence dump at the end.
- Emotional arc that hides limits.
- Progress indicator with no navigable chapters.
- Auto-advancing story.
- Repeated identical demo button after every paragraph.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is the thesis, current stage, chapter structure, and destination visible?
- Can each stage stand alone for a scanning or skipping reader?
- Does transformation include current state, cause, mechanism, adoption, result, evidence, and constraints?
- Are visuals and proof bound to the exact stage and available without motion or stickiness?
- Do terms, safeguards, uncertainty, eligibility, fees, and limits precede commitment?
- Can readers skip to proof, details, comparison, documentation, a bounded task, or expert contact?
- Is guidance based on visible narrative behavior rather than claiming unobserved outcomes?
