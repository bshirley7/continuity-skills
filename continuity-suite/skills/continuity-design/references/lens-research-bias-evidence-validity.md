# Research bias and evidence validity

## Generalized principles

- Define the research decision before collecting evidence: bind the question, population, context, artifact and revision, task, constructs, hypotheses and counter-hypotheses, measures, exclusions, analysis, uncertainty, stopping rule, and possible no-change conclusion; record later deviations and exploratory findings separately.
- Make coverage and selection visible: identify the target population, sampling frame, recruitment channel, eligibility, opt-in pressure, compensation, inclusion and exclusion, nonresponse, attrition, missingness, and who never reached the study; never generalize from responders, retained users, successful customers, or visible survivors without bounding the claim.
- Separate observation, participant account, instrument output, interpretation, and decision: preserve exact artifact anchors and context, use structured criteria, independent coding or blinding where feasible, record disagreement and negative cases, and do not let researcher expectation or stakeholder preference silently determine what is noticed, prompted, coded, or omitted.
- Treat the research encounter as an intervention: document moderator identity, script, question order, disclosure, observation method, incentives, audience, recording, task artificiality, demand cues, social desirability, and repeated exposure; compare behavior with other evidence and qualify conclusions when observation may have changed the phenomenon.
- Preserve complete outcome and analysis provenance: report all planned measures, denominators, exclusions, transformations, segments, windows, failed tasks, null and contradictory findings, instrument changes, and uncertainty; prevent favorable metric, quote, participant, task, or time-window selection from becoming the apparent result.
- Triangulate evidence without collapsing distinct questions: observed task behavior, explanation, survey response, analytics, accessibility inspection, expert judgment, support demand, and business outcome each have different validity limits; seek convergence, explain disagreement, and do not average unlike evidence into one confidence or usability score.
- Bind recommendations to strength and transfer limits of the evidence: distinguish exploratory, descriptive, comparative, causal, replicated, and externally validated claims; state population, context, artifact, date, confidence, contradictions, and unknowns; require replication or deliberate variation before treating a local pattern as a general design principle.

## Method

- Write the decision, question, population, context, artifact revision, hypotheses, counter-hypotheses, measures, exclusions, analysis, stopping rule, and no-change outcome.
- Map the sampling frame, recruitment, eligibility, compensation, nonresponse, attrition, missingness, excluded groups, and people absent from the observable journey.
- Separate source observations, participant explanations, instrument outputs, researcher interpretations, stakeholder preferences, and final decisions in the record.
- Standardize scripts and coding where useful, preserve moderator deviations and demand cues, and use independent review or blinding proportionate to consequence.
- Audit all planned and exploratory outcomes, tasks, quotes, segments, windows, exclusions, transformations, nulls, contradictions, and instrument changes.
- Triangulate distinct evidence types, investigate disagreement, test transfer under changed conditions, and bind every recommendation to its exact evidence scope.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not recruit only convenient, compliant, successful, retained, high-literacy, or digitally capable participants and claim user coverage.
- Do not prompt, observe, compensate, or disclose in ways that pressure participants to protect the researcher, employer, provider, or desired result.
- Do not expose sensitive identity, disability, distress, financial, health, or behavioral data beyond what the research question requires.
- Do not suppress failed tasks, negative accounts, null results, dissent, subgroup harm, or evidence that contradicts the preferred direction.
- Do not use analytics volume, statistical significance, quote vividness, reviewer authority, or participant confidence as a substitute for validity.
- Do not let research evidence authorize implementation, exposure, publication, policy, or consequential action without separate authority.

## Variation levers

- Use lightweight preregistered question and evidence fields for low-risk formative work and a formal protocol for consequential comparative or causal claims.
- Increase independent coding, blinding, replication, subgroup coverage, and audit depth with subjectivity, consequence, conflict, and organizational pressure.
- Use naturalistic observation for ecological context and controlled tasks for precise comparison while explicitly retaining each method’s limits.
- Use purposive samples for bounded experiential questions and probability or well-characterized samples for population estimates.
- Use qualitative evidence to explain mechanisms and harms while keeping prevalence and causal claims appropriately bounded.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- More disclosure improves reproducibility while increasing privacy, reactivity, and administrative burden.
- Standardization improves comparison while suppressing unexpected context and participant-led meaning.
- Blinding reduces expectation effects while sometimes removing domain context needed for valid interpretation.
- Representative breadth improves transfer while reducing depth available for rare or complex experiences.
- Naturalistic observation improves realism while weakening control and changing behavior through observation.
- Open materials improve scrutiny while risking participant reidentification or exposure of sensitive systems.

## Failure modes

- The research question changes after a favorable pattern appears and the new analysis is presented as planned.
- Only people who completed, purchased, retained, responded, or remained reachable appear in the evidence.
- A moderator explains the intended model until participants reproduce it, then reports comprehension.
- A memorable quote outweighs task failures, contradictory cases, or population coverage.
- Aggregate success hides a subgroup that could not access, understand, refuse, or recover.
- Null, negative, abandoned, and inaccessible tasks disappear from the synthesis.
- One local study becomes a universal principle without replication or transfer testing.

## Anti-patterns

- Recruit the easy users.
- Ask until understood.
- Quote as prevalence.
- Analytics as intent.
- Average as inclusion.
- Unexpected means irrelevant.
- Exploratory means confirmed.
- Null means nothing.
- One study, universal law.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Were the decision, population, artifact revision, hypotheses, counter-hypotheses, measures, exclusions, analysis, stopping rule, and no-change outcome fixed before evidence collection?
- Who was in the frame, recruited, eligible, excluded, nonresponsive, missing, or lost, and who never became observable?
- Are observation, participant account, instrument output, interpretation, preference, and decision recorded separately?
- How might moderator behavior, question order, incentives, disclosure, observation, task artificiality, or audience have changed the evidence?
- Are all planned and exploratory outcomes, tasks, denominators, exclusions, transformations, segments, nulls, and contradictions visible?
- Do distinct evidence types converge, and where they disagree, is the disagreement investigated rather than averaged away?
- Does every recommendation state its evidence type, population, context, artifact, date, uncertainty, contradictions, and transfer limits?
