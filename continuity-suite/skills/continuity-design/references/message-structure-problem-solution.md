# Problem and solution

## Generalized principles

- Begin with a recognizable situation for a named actor, then make the operational, emotional, financial, or risk consequence concrete before introducing the product.
- Explain why the problem persists through a credible cause—fragmented ownership, manual handoffs, missing visibility, repeated work, uncontrolled risk, or incompatible tools—rather than blaming the user.
- Present the solution as a mechanism that changes specific inputs, handoffs, decisions, controls, or outputs; avoid jumping from pain directly to feature inventory or an unearned outcome.
- Connect each claimed benefit to the mechanism and evidence needed to judge it, including actor, baseline, timeframe, scope, and material constraints for consequential claims.
- Preserve favorable existing behavior and acknowledge switching, integration, governance, or learning costs so the proposed solution does not erase what already works.
- End with a proportionate next step that matches the reader's confidence and risk—inspect the workflow, compare approaches, review evidence, try a bounded task, or begin a qualified conversation.

## Variation levers

- Lead with lived frustration for familiar consumer problems and operational evidence for complex B2B problems.
- Use before-and-after structure when workflow fragmentation is visible and causal.
- Use problem-cause-mechanism-evidence when trust or technical complexity is high.
- Show the product in the exact task context described by the problem.
- Scale the call to action with consequence, reversibility, and reader confidence.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Strong pain framing creates urgency while risking exaggeration.
- A concise mechanism improves comprehension while omitting implementation nuance.
- Before-and-after comparison clarifies change while oversimplifying transition cost.
- Outcome evidence builds confidence while becoming misleading when scope or baseline is hidden.
- A direct call to action accelerates conversion while outpacing trust.

## Failure modes

- The problem is generic enough to fit any product.
- The user is blamed for structural friction.
- The solution appears as a feature list with no causal mechanism.
- Benefits have no actor, baseline, timeframe, or constraints.
- Existing favorable behavior and switching cost disappear.
- The call to action asks for commitment before evidence or fit is established.

## Anti-patterns

- Invented crisis.
- You are doing it wrong framing.
- Problem paragraph followed by logo wall.
- Magic automation leap.
- Feature equals outcome.
- Before-and-after with incomparable baselines.
- Fear-based compliance copy.
- Universal solution claim.
- Demo request as the only way to understand the mechanism.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is the problem a recognizable situation for a named actor with concrete consequence?
- Does the narrative explain why the problem persists without blaming the user?
- Is the solution a specific mechanism changing inputs, handoffs, decisions, controls, or outputs?
- Are benefits connected to mechanism, evidence, actor, baseline, timeframe, scope, and constraints?
- Does the narrative preserve favorable behavior and acknowledge transition costs?
- Is the next step proportionate to confidence, consequence, and reversibility?
- Is guidance based on visible messaging evidence rather than claiming unobserved product outcomes?
