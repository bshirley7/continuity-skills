# Continuity Presentation Design Challenge

Design a presentation document that introduces **Continuity**, a control plane for software work shared by humans and agents.

The artifact begins as private responsive HTML and CSS. It must work as a projected presentation and as an independent read-ahead document. It is a strategic product introduction, not a fundraising deck unless verified fundraising evidence is supplied.

## Central idea

**Work survives the handoff. Authority stays human.**

The audience should leave with one clear understanding:

> Continuity carries the goal, evidence, owner, and stopping boundary when the actor, conversation, or day changes.

## Audience

Design for founder-operators, product leaders, engineering leaders, and platform teams evaluating long-running work across humans and agents. They understand repositories, issue trackers, coding agents, documents, and review workflows. They are skeptical of vague autonomy and invented proof.

## Fixed product truth

- Continuity carries goals, evidence, owners, stop conditions, and authority boundaries.
- `healthy` is not `authorized`.
- `review-ready` is not `completed`.
- passed validation is not permission to merge, deploy, publish, or claim business success.
- detailed truth stays project-local; portfolio signal remains owner-safe and summarized.
- design approval never authorizes implementation or external publication.

## Required narrative ingredients

Determine the order and sequence grammar, but make these relationships clear:

1. Important files may remain while the decision context disappears.
2. A commit proves what changed, not why, what was rejected, or who may decide next.
3. Continuity carries goal, evidence, owner, and stop condition together.
4. Work passes through capture, design, approval, execution, and review without collapsing their authority.
5. A healthy current project can remain explicitly unauthorized.
6. Project-local truth and portfolio-safe signal serve different purposes.
7. An overnight handoff should begin the next morning with a decision surface, not reconstruction work.
8. The final request should propose one bounded live handoff rather than imply autonomous adoption.

## Evidence constraints

No customer logos, testimonials, market size, revenue, traction, performance, savings, uptime, or adoption claims are supplied. Do not invent them. Qualify generated or illustrative media visibly. Keep third-party research and reconstructions private and non-shipping.

## Creative challenge

Develop one to three genuinely distinct sequence concepts. Every direction must differ in narrative organization, slide-role system, typography, art direction, composition, and media behavior—not only its cover.

Use image generation or editing as concept development. At least one direction must use owned, supplied, or generated media to form its thesis, composition, and sequence. Extract at least three repeatable non-image rules from that material.

Reject generic investor templates, repeated title-and-body slides, fake metrics, decorative charts, gratuitous quote slides, tiny unreadable type, clipped or obscured copy, illegible data and source lines, purple-gradient AI styling, black-neon terminal reflexes, and imagery used only as wallpaper. Every audience-facing word and every data-bearing mark must be visibly present and readable on its slide at the required viewport; shorten copy or change composition before shrinking it.

## Required outputs

1. Diagnosed brief, collaboration profile, modality, and research mode.
2. Numbered reference decomposition and 12–20 tile private moodboard.
3. Generative concept laboratory with eight to twelve seeds across at least three media.
4. One to three comparable concept boards showing opening, tension, proof, system explanation, quiet or rest, and closure.
5. A sequence contact sheet and slide-role inventory for every direction.
6. Up to three preserved autonomous craft passes with one hypothesis, visual delta, and self-assessment each.
7. Explicit human direction selection or combination.
8. A resolved private responsive deck after selection.
9. Individually verified desktop projection, tablet read-ahead, and mobile read-ahead captures.
10. Keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, accessibility, claim-provenance, capture-integrity, and slide-by-slide copy/data readability evidence.
11. Native AI-slop reports using `translation_fidelity`, `portfolio_diversity`, and `presentation_fidelity`.
12. Final self-critique and exact design approval request.

Stop after exact design approval. Do not implement Continuity, publish the deck, or treat the benchmark as authorization for external use.
