# Conversation lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Establish a visible conversation envelope before sending: identify destination, topic or object, purpose, participants and roles, direct addressees versus observers, workspace or tenant, privacy and external-participant status, retention boundary, and notification reach; keep that envelope visible beside the draft and require fresh review when audience or destination changes.
- Represent conversation as an accessible durable sequence: preserve author or system identity, exact time and ordering, reply parent, attachment and link context, edited or deleted state, delivery failure, and meaningful unread and mention anchors; announce new content without stealing focus and let people return to their prior reading position.
- Keep response scope and common context inspectable through parent preview, quote or reference, thread topic, participants, affected object, recent relevant turns, and an explicit distinction among reply, reply-all, new topic, direct message, forward, reaction, and mention; never rely on visual proximity alone to establish what a response addresses.
- Promote consequential outcomes out of conversational implication into durable objects: record decisions, approvals, requests, promises, action items, owners, due dates, affected scope, status, evidence, and supersession with a backlink to the exact message context; do not treat a reaction, read receipt, silence, generated summary, or informal acknowledgment as agreement or completion.
- Make conversation correction and lifecycle changes legible and proportionate: preserve edited markers and material revision history, deletion tombstones where continuity requires them, forwarding provenance and audience review, pin or summary source links, membership and ownership changes, export and retention state, abuse reporting, and restoration or correction paths without silently rewriting shared history.

## Variation levers

- Use compact linear chat for rapid low-stakes exchange and topic-centered threads when delayed participation, multiple issues, or durable decisions make context recovery important.
- Increase audience review, revision history, retention, moderation, decision capture, and export controls with external participants, regulated records, sensitive content, or consequential commitments.
- Use polite passive updates for routine incoming messages and interruptive presentation only for genuinely time-critical, directly relevant communication.
- Provide personal unread, save, mute, digest, and catch-up controls while preserving shared canonical sequence and decision records.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Rapid messaging lowers coordination cost while producing ambiguous commitments and fragmented decisions.
- Threads preserve local context while hiding related work and splitting attention across parallel discussions.
- Read receipts and presence improve coordination while enabling surveillance and coercive response expectations.
- Editing corrects mistakes while undermining shared memory when material changes are silent.
- Persistent history supports accountability while increasing privacy, retention, discovery, and safety risk.

## Failure modes

- A composer does not clearly identify destination, participants, external status, topic, or notification reach.
- A reply appears near messages but lacks a durable parent, quote, topic, or affected-object relationship.
- Unread state is only a count and cannot restore the first meaningful unread item or prior reading position.
- A reaction, read receipt, silence, or generated recap is treated as consent, approval, ownership, or task completion.
- Editing, deletion, forwarding, summarization, or membership change silently alters the meaning or audience of prior communication.
- A handoff to another channel, person, or automated participant loses transcript, attachments, decisions, open questions, or promised actions.
- New messages, typing, delivery, and error states are unavailable to assistive technology or repeatedly steal focus.

## Anti-patterns

- Send before audience.
- Reply by proximity.
- Unread count without anchor.
- Reaction equals approval.
- Silent history rewrite.
- Forward without provenance.
- Summary replaces source.
- Handoff loses the thread.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Before sending, are destination, topic, purpose, participants, roles, workspace, privacy, external status, retention, and notification reach visible?
- Does the sequence preserve identity, exact order, reply relationships, evidence, change state, delivery failure, unread anchors, accessibility, and reading position?
- Are reply, reply-all, thread, new topic, direct message, forward, reaction, and mention semantically distinct and tied to inspectable context?
- Are decisions, approvals, requests, promises, actions, owners, due dates, status, and evidence promoted into durable linked objects rather than inferred?
- Do edits, deletion, forwarding, summaries, membership, retention, export, reporting, and restoration preserve shared-history integrity?
- Can a channel, participant, or automation handoff retain transcript, evidence, decisions, open questions, and commitments?
