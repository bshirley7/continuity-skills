# Calm clarity

## Generalized principles

- Create calm through controlled attention: establish one dominant status, decision, or starting action before styling secondary content.
- Group secondary information into stable containers with consistent spacing, rhythm, and color semantics.
- Reserve saturated color for meaningful state or action instead of giving promotions and utilities simultaneous emphasis.
- Let empty and completed states reduce visual noise while preserving orientation and a useful next action.
- Keep dense information calm by strengthening scan paths rather than merely removing content.

## Variation levers

- Increase whitespace for singular start states and increase grouping density for monitoring or comparison tasks.
- Use a neutral base with one semantic accent family, expanding the palette only when distinct states require it.
- Choose one dominant card or uncontained focal region depending on whether the primary state belongs to a larger dashboard.
- Reduce navigation and discovery emphasis during completion, recovery, or high-stakes decision moments.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Lower visual noise can improve comprehension while reducing discovery of secondary capabilities.
- Dense data supports expert scanning while challenging a calm first impression.
- Brand expression can create warmth while competing with status and action semantics.
- Reassuring presentation can reduce anxiety while under-signaling genuine risk or urgency.

## Failure modes

- Every card receives its own accent color or promotional treatment.
- A promotional carousel competes with the primary health, financial, or task state.
- Whitespace creates emptiness without explaining orientation or the next action.
- Muted controls lose contrast, affordance, or accessible state distinction.
- Several panels use equal size and contrast despite having unequal importance.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating calm as a pastel palette without resolving hierarchy.
- Hiding important risk, failure, or recovery states to preserve visual serenity.
- Adding decorative gradients, badges, and cards to every region.
- Using low contrast as a substitute for secondary emphasis.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can a user identify the dominant status or next action within a brief scan?
- Do secondary regions share a predictable grouping and spacing system?
- Is saturated color reserved for information or action that merits attention?
- Do empty and completed states preserve orientation and recovery?
- Are risk and accessibility signals still strong enough despite the restrained style?
