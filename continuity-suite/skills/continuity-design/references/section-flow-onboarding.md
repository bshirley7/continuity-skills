# Onboarding

## Generalized principles

- Explain the immediate benefit or necessity before requesting personal data, permissions, or effort.
- Make remaining work legible with named steps, completion state, and a credible time estimate when one is available.
- Keep each screen centered on one material decision and one visually dominant forward action.
- Provide visible recovery or optionality when a question, permission, or interruption is not strictly required at that moment.
- Explain how personalization input changes the experience so setup feels useful rather than extractive.

## Variation levers

- Use a concise benefit statement for low-friction setup and a step preview for high-friction or regulated setup.
- Show progress as named steps when sequence matters, or as a lightweight position indicator when steps are brief and homogeneous.
- Use selectable cards for a small concrete option set and a compact field when the option space is long.
- Offer skip, defer, help, or close according to whether the step is optional, recoverable, or mandatory.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- More rationale can increase trust while slowing a user who already understands the request.
- Precise progress can reduce uncertainty while creating brittle expectations if the process branches.
- Personalization can accelerate relevance while feeling like interrogation when its use is unclear.
- Required legal detail can support informed consent while overwhelming the core decision.

## Failure modes

- The benefit is generic and does not explain why the requested input matters.
- A progress indicator reports position without naming useful work or accounting for branches.
- A permission or sensitive-data request appears before a plain-language rationale.
- A disabled forward action does not make the missing requirement evident.
- Dense legal language displaces the decision summary and recovery options.

## Anti-patterns

- Collecting several unrelated profile answers on one screen.
- Presenting every setup step as mandatory when useful deferral is possible.
- Using celebration, urgency, or social proof to pressure consent.
- Claiming an exact completion time without accounting for verification or review delays.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can a first-time user explain the value or necessity of this step before continuing?
- Can the user tell what remains and whether the estimate is credible?
- Does the screen ask for one material decision rather than several unrelated inputs?
- Are optional, deferable, and mandatory steps distinguished honestly?
- Is the next action clear without hiding consequences or consent details?
