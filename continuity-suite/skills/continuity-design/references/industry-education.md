# Education

## Generalized principles

- Center the learner home on the current learning objective, exact resume point, meaningful progress, due or review work, and the next achievable action before streaks, points, or generic recommendations.
- Separate effort, continuity, mastery, completion, assessment, and credential state so time spent or streak length cannot stand in for learning outcome.
- Keep lesson context complete through course, unit, lesson, sequence, duration, progress, transcript or captions, notes, attachments, assessment, previous, next, and explicit completion state.
- Support comprehension with controllable playback, synchronized alternatives, searchable structure, note capture, review, feedback, and a route from assessment evidence back to the relevant lesson.
- At enrollment, preserve program outcome, format, instructor or authority, start and end timing, cadence, prerequisites, capacity or learner scope, price, discounts, tax, cancellation, and total commitment.
- Distinguish self-enrollment, assigned learning, team enrollment, cohort participation, and required compliance, naming who selected the learner, who can inspect progress, and what completion changes.
- Make instructional analytics actionable by connecting aggregate completion or score to individual learners, assignment state, last activity, due state, evidence, and a proportionate intervention route.
- Use motivation mechanics as adjustable support for an explicit learning goal, never as the sole definition of success or as punishment for interrupted participation.

## Variation levers

- Use linear sequence for prerequisite learning and flexible collections for independent topics.
- Use streaks for voluntary continuity and due dates for accountable assigned work.
- Use transcripts and chapters for media lessons and worked states for interactive practice.
- Use one-time purchase review for bounded programs and lifecycle review for subscriptions.
- Use cohort dashboards for shared pacing and individual drilldown for intervention.
- Use completion checks for participation and assessments for demonstrated learning.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Visible progress motivates learners while oversimplifying mastery.
- Streaks support continuity while penalizing life interruptions.
- Rich lesson controls aid comprehension while increasing player density.
- Flexible pacing improves access while weakening shared accountability.
- Instructor analytics support intervention while increasing surveillance risk.
- Outcome marketing clarifies value while overstating educational certainty.

## Failure modes

- The home promotes streaks or recommendations without an explicit learning objective.
- Time spent, points, completion, and mastery are conflated.
- A lesson lacks sequence, transcript, notes, assessment, or return context.
- Assessment failure has no route back to relevant instruction.
- Enrollment omits schedule, learner scope, price, or cancellation.
- Assigned learning does not identify the assigning authority or visibility.
- Aggregate analytics cannot identify who needs what intervention.
- Motivation mechanics cannot be adjusted or recovered after interruption.

## Anti-patterns

- Calling a daily streak learning progress.
- Using completion percent as mastery.
- Playing lessons in an isolated media viewer.
- Showing quiz scores without feedback or review.
- Selling a course without cadence and time commitment.
- Enrolling team members with no review of dates or reminders.
- Ranking learners without instructional context.
- Using streak loss to manufacture urgency.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does the learner home prioritize objective, resume point, progress, due work, and next action?
- Are effort, continuity, mastery, completion, assessment, and credential state distinct?
- Do lessons expose sequence, alternatives, notes, assessment, navigation, and completion?
- Can learners move from assessment evidence back to relevant instruction?
- Does enrollment show outcome, authority, format, timing, scope, price, and cancellation?
- Are enrollment authority, progress visibility, and completion consequence explicit?
- Can analytics connect aggregates to learner evidence and intervention?
- Are motivation mechanics adjustable, recoverable, and subordinate to learning goals?
