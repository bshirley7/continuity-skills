# Quantitative evidence and uncertainty

## Generalized principles

- Make every quantitative claim self-describing through the question or intended decision, population or object scope, measure definition, numerator and denominator, unit, time and timezone basis, comparison or baseline, source, freshness, exclusions, and whether the result is observed, estimated, modeled, or forecast.
- Choose representation from the analytical relationship rather than visual novelty: use position and length for precise comparison, trends for time, distributions for variation, ranges for uncertainty, tables for exact values, and plain-language summaries plus structured data alternatives so the evidence remains available without color, hover, vision, or fine pointer control.
- Expose uncertainty and data quality when they can change interpretation through sample or coverage, missing and suppressed values, estimation method, range and coverage basis, rounding, revision, source change, and limitations; explain uncertainty in plain language and withhold or qualify comparisons that the evidence cannot support.
- Separate exploratory pattern finding from confirmatory evidence and causal claims: preserve the original question, disclose post-hoc segmentation and multiple comparisons, identify assumptions and transformations, distinguish correlation from mechanism, and require independent validation before a selected pattern governs consequential action.
- Make analysis reproducible and correctable through inspectable query, source and version, metric definition, transformations, filters, comparison basis, generated time, code or method where relevant, owner, revision, annotations, underlying data access, and a visible correction path that reaches prior recipients and decisions.

## Method

- Write the analytical question, intended use, population, measures, dimensions, periods, baseline, denominator, source, exclusions, and exploratory, monitoring, or confirmatory mode before selecting a chart.
- Document data provenance, transformations, missing and suppressed values, sampling or coverage, uncertainty model, revisions, freshness, and comparability limits.
- Choose encodings and coordinated table or text alternatives based on comparison, trend, distribution, composition, sequence, relationship, geography, and uncertainty tasks.
- Specify filter visibility, drill-down scope, annotation, generated interpretation, saved-query identity, access, export provenance, revision, and correction behavior.
- Test accurate interpretation with realistic values, similar magnitudes, zero and missing states, small denominators, wide uncertainty, outliers, long labels, zoom, keyboard, screen reader, reduced color, and alternate formats.
- Measure correct conclusions, scope and denominator comprehension, uncertainty understanding, missed exceptions, accessibility task completion, reproducibility, correction reach, and decisions changed—not only chart engagement.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not alter axes, baselines, units, precision, ordering, bins, aggregation, periods, or aspect ratio to manufacture a visual conclusion.
- Do not hide denominator, sample, missingness, exclusions, uncertainty, revisions, or noncomparable periods in tooltips or distant methodology alone.
- Do not present correlation, selected segments, post-hoc patterns, generated summaries, or forecasts as causal or confirmed findings.
- Do not use averages alone when distribution, subgroup effects, or affected extremes materially change the decision.
- Do not expose row-level or small-group data when filtering, drill-down, export, or cross-linking creates privacy or re-identification risk.
- Do not optimize analytical interfaces for persuasive impact while excluding correctness, uncertainty comprehension, reproducibility, and correction.

## Variation levers

- Increase methodological, uncertainty, provenance, privacy, and revision detail with estimation, consequence, external publication, or small-group analysis.
- Use direct labels for a few series, persistent legends for more, and small multiples when overlap prevents accurate comparison.
- Use uncertainty ranges when they change interpretation and plain-language limitations or no comparison when uncertainty defeats meaningful distinction.
- Use guided metrics and narrative for broad audiences and composable queries with definitions, distributions, diagnostics, and code or method access for experts.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Simplification improves comprehension while omitting uncertainty or variation that changes the conclusion.
- Interactive exploration enables discovery while increasing post-hoc selection and irreproducibility.
- Dense methodological context supports scrutiny while overwhelming readers who need the decision boundary.
- Narrative interpretation aids orientation while steering attention and overstating one explanation.
- Open underlying data supports verification while increasing privacy, misuse, and stale-copy risks.

## Failure modes

- A number or chart lacks population, definition, unit, period, denominator, baseline, source, or freshness.
- Color, area, angle, animation, or three-dimensional effect carries a comparison that position or text could communicate more accurately.
- Uncertainty, missingness, suppression, revision, or noncomparability is absent or available only on hover.
- An exploratory segment or generated summary is presented as a confirmed or causal finding.
- A saved or exported analysis cannot be reconstructed from query, source, definitions, transformations, filters, and generated time.
- A correction changes the current chart but does not identify prior versions, recipients, or decisions that relied on it.

## Anti-patterns

- Metric without a population.
- Truncated-axis drama.
- Color equals evidence.
- Average erases distribution.
- Tooltip-only uncertainty.
- Exploration becomes confirmation.
- Generated causality.
- Export without lineage.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does every claim expose question, scope, definition, numerator, denominator, unit, time, comparison, source, freshness, exclusions, and evidence type?
- Does the representation match the relationship and provide equivalent text and structured data access across modalities?
- Are sample, coverage, missingness, suppression, estimation, uncertainty, rounding, revision, and comparability visible where material?
- Are exploration, monitoring, confirmation, correlation, causality, generated interpretation, and forecast clearly distinguished?
- Can the analysis be reproduced and corrected from query, source, version, transformations, filters, method, owner, annotations, data, and history?
- Do evaluation measures include correct conclusions, denominator and uncertainty comprehension, accessibility, reproducibility, privacy, and correction reach?
