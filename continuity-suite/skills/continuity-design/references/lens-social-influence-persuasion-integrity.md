# Social influence and persuasion integrity

## Generalized principles

- Treat social information as fallible decision evidence, not a command: state what is being counted or claimed, population and time window, source, verification, sampling and ranking method, material incentives or relationships, typicality, uncertainty, and important exclusions; provide access to independent details and do not imply that popularity establishes quality, safety, truth, or suitability.
- Preserve independent judgment before exposing consensus when anchoring or herding could materially distort the choice: let people inspect criteria, evidence, alternatives, and their own priorities first; distinguish descriptive norms from recommendations and requirements, and offer a neutral or non-personalized path in high-consequence or platform-mediated decisions.
- Make every social signal authentic and auditable: prohibit fabricated activity, scarcity, demand, reviews, identities, expertise, affiliations, or consensus; disclose moderation, incentives, sponsorship, material connections, selection and suppression rules, aggregation, recency, duplicates, and whether a person, organization, model, or automated process produced the signal.
- Do not convert reciprocity, liking, belonging, authority, commitment, consistency, or prior effort into obligation: a gift, compliment, community membership, expert cue, small initial action, saved progress, public statement, or previous choice must not make refusal, revision, exit, privacy, or a different decision harder or socially costly.
- Match authority cues to relevant and bounded competence: identify the advisor, role, jurisdiction, evidence basis, conflicts, uncertainty, review date, and limits; separate expertise from celebrity, rank, institutional branding, confidence, and enforcement power, and keep the user able to question, compare, seek another opinion, or decline.
- Use urgency and scarcity only when factual, material, current, attributable, and decision-relevant: explain what is limited, why, when the condition was observed, when it expires, and what happens if the person waits; never fabricate countdowns, low stock, demand, expiring benefits, queues, exclusivity, or social loss to suppress deliberation.
- Evaluate persuasion by informed fit and durable agency rather than conversion: test claim comprehension, independent choice before and after social signals, refusal and reversal, delayed regret, representativeness, subgroup effects, cumulative exposure, complaint and cancellation outcomes, privacy, and whether the selected outcome still serves the person.
- Increase protection when social identity, developmental stage, distress, isolation, stigma, dependency, financial constraint, health, civic consequence, or personalized susceptibility may amplify influence; do not target or adapt pressure using inferred vulnerability, and involve affected groups in testing the safeguards and alternatives.

## Method

- Inventory every review, rating, count, norm, authority cue, testimonial, ranking, scarcity claim, referral, commitment, and reciprocal gesture.
- For each signal, document source, population, window, verification, incentives, selection, aggregation, uncertainty, typicality, ranking, and decision consequence.
- Map the path before and after exposure, including independent evaluation, comparison, refusal, revision, exit, cancellation, and non-personalized alternatives.
- Prototype missing, conflicting, stale, manipulated, sponsored, atypical, minority, outlier, low-sample, and high-pressure social evidence.
- Test comprehension, independent choice, susceptibility across repeated exposure, refusal cost, delayed regret, accessibility, and affected subgroups.
- Monitor false claims, skew, suppression, complaints, reversals, cancellations, harm, and long-term fit rather than optimizing only acceptance.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not fabricate, inflate, selectively time, or ambiguously label reviews, activity, consensus, scarcity, demand, expertise, or endorsement.
- Do not personalize persuasion from vulnerability, dependency, distress, age, disability, financial constraint, isolation, or inferred susceptibility.
- Do not make refusal, privacy, cancellation, dissent, or changing one's mind socially embarrassing, hidden, delayed, or more costly.
- Do not use a prior small action, public commitment, free benefit, compliment, community identity, or saved effort to manufacture obligation.
- Do not present an atypical success, paid relationship, selected testimonial, or aggregate rating without the material context needed to interpret it.
- Do not treat conversion, engagement, virality, compliance, or popularity as proof of informed value.

## Variation levers

- Show aggregate social evidence only after personal criteria in high-consequence or strongly anchorable decisions.
- Increase source, method, uncertainty, and conflict detail with consequence, personalization, sample weakness, and ranking opacity.
- Use neutral factual counts for operational coordination and richer qualitative evidence when suitability depends on context.
- Provide non-personalized, chronological, or criteria-based alternatives where recommendation systems shape exposure.
- Increase stopping points and independent review for children, vulnerable groups, recurring commitments, and delayed costs.
- Prefer representative outcomes and dissenting evidence over polished testimonials or a single exceptional case.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Social evidence reduces search cost while anchoring and herding can suppress independent judgment.
- Community norms coordinate behavior while excluding minorities or normalizing harm.
- Expert advice supports complex decisions while authority cues can exceed relevant competence.
- Visible popularity aids discovery while rankings can become self-reinforcing.
- Commitment supports continuity while creating identity and sunk-cost pressure.
- Urgency can communicate a real constraint while shortening deliberation exactly when stakes are high.

## Failure modes

- A rating appears without sample, recency, selection, incentive, or distribution context.
- Prior votes create self-reinforcing rankings that are presented as independent quality.
- A paid or insider endorsement is mixed into organic reviews without conspicuous context.
- An expert badge has no relevant domain, evidence basis, limits, or review date.
- A free resource or compliment is followed by a request framed as repayment.
- A real-time activity count or countdown is fabricated, recycled, or unrelated to availability.
- Declining an invitation, privacy change, renewal, or community norm is framed as letting others down.
- Persuasion is personalized toward a person inferred to be distressed or susceptible.

## Anti-patterns

- Popularity means truth.
- Consensus before criteria.
- Badge means expertise.
- Testimonial means typical.
- Gift means debt.
- Commitment means no revision.
- Countdown theater.
- Belong or disappoint.
- Target the susceptible.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can a person inspect the source, population, window, verification, incentives, selection, ranking, typicality, uncertainty, and exclusions behind each social claim?
- Can people form and act on their own criteria before consensus, popularity, or advice anchors the decision?
- Are reviews, activity, demand, endorsements, identities, expertise, and scarcity authentic, current, auditable, and clearly labeled?
- Can a person refuse, revise, cancel, protect privacy, or dissent without reciprocity, commitment, identity, authority, or social-cost pressure?
- Are authority cues bounded to relevant competence, evidence, jurisdiction, conflicts, uncertainty, and review date?
- Is urgency tied to a factual material constraint with provenance, observation time, expiry, and consequences of waiting?
- Does evaluation measure informed fit, independent choice, reversal, regret, subgroup harm, and cumulative exposure beyond conversion?
- Are protections strengthened for developmental, social, financial, health, civic, or other vulnerability without targeting susceptibility?
