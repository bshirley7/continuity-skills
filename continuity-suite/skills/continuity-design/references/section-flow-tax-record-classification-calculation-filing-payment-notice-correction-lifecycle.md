# Tax record, classification, calculation, filing, payment, notice, and correction lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Preserve one versioned filing identity: identify taxpayer or liable entity, tax type, jurisdiction and authority, tax period, filing obligation, form and revision, registration or account identifiers, due dates, filing frequency, accounting basis, preparer and representative authority; bind source records, classifications, calculations, review, declaration, submission, payment, notices, amendments and finality to it; and never let a generic tax year, dashboard card or software workspace stand in for the exact obligation.
- Separate identity, residence, citizenship, domicile, beneficial ownership, legal entity, account holder, earner, payer, dependent, guardian and authorized representative: explain why each fact is required, its date and jurisdiction, who can certify or change it and which filings or reports it affects; support multiple legitimate roles; and never infer tax status from address, payment account, nationality, employment relationship or device location alone.
- Build the filing from traceable source evidence: identify issuer, recipient, period, transaction or account, amount, currency, category, tax treatment, import or sync origin, update time and attached document; preserve user edits, duplicates, missing records and conflicts; link every reported figure back to included records; and never let automated import, OCR or prior-year carryover silently become verified truth.
- Make classifications and jurisdiction rules inspectable: state the factual inputs, applicable period, rule or assumption, threshold, rate, exemption, allocation and consequence; distinguish user-supplied facts, software inference, professional judgment and authority determination; expose alternatives and correction; and never present a category, residency result, nexus state, deductible treatment or exemption as certain when material facts are missing or rules conflict.
- Explain every material calculation as a reconstructable chain: show taxable base, included and excluded records, rates, brackets, credits, deductions, withholding, payments, adjustments, rounding, currency conversion, prior balances, penalties or interest, jurisdiction allocation and date; distinguish projected, prepared, filed, assessed and paid amounts; show deltas when inputs change; and never use refund or amount-due prominence to hide assumptions or uncertainty.
- Make review responsibility and filing authority explicit: identify what the taxpayer, preparer, payroll operator, employer, marketplace, intermediary or agent can view, edit, certify, submit, pay, receive and amend; preserve questions and approvals; present the exact declaration and final return before authorization; distinguish software readiness from human review; and never let delegated preparation become unreviewed filing or imply that a provider guarantees legal correctness.
- Represent filing as a state machine: distinguish draft, incomplete, ready for review, authorized, queued, transmitted, received, accepted, accepted with alert, rejected, corrected, superseded, amended and final; expose authority acknowledgments, rejection rules, deadlines and next action; preserve the exact submitted artifact and receipt; and never call a queued transmission filed, an accepted return audited, or a software check authority approval.
- Keep filing, payment, refund and account balance distinct: show payment authority, destination authority, tax period, amount, method, scheduled and settled dates, reference, cancellation boundary, allocation and receipt; distinguish expected refund, claimed refund, approved refund and disbursement; preserve payment plans and offsets; and never imply that filing pays a balance, that payment files a return or that a projected refund is owed.
- Treat notices, corrections and amendments as linked procedural work: identify issuing authority, notice number, period, reason, proposed change, amount, response deadline, rights, required evidence and representative; compare authority data with the filed record; preserve agreement, disagreement, response, amendment, appeal and outcome; and never reduce an official notice to an upload ticket or silently overwrite the return that produced it.
- Preserve private, portable and accessible tax records through finality: provide durable returns, forms, calculations, source links, acknowledgments, payments, correspondence and amendments with retention guidance; minimize identifiers, protect exports and representative access, support language and assistive technology, and allow continued retrieval after service exit; never hold compliance evidence hostage or expose full taxpayer identifiers where a masked reference is sufficient.

## Variation levers

- Increase source-record, reconciliation, approval and segregation controls for payroll and business filings.
- Increase plain-language explanation, guided review and low-cost support for individual self-filing.
- Increase jurisdiction, currency, treaty, residency and intermediary controls for cross-border cases.
- Increase age-aware authority and privacy controls for dependents and guardians.
- Increase offline, accessible, multilingual and representative routes when digital-only filing creates exclusion.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Guided simplicity versus legal and computational completeness.
- Automation versus taxpayer verification and professional judgment.
- Early estimates versus volatile facts and rule changes.
- Identity assurance versus privacy and access.
- Provider convenience versus taxpayer ownership of records.
- Deadline urgency versus review, correction and representation.

## Failure modes

- Address or nationality is treated as tax residence.
- Imported records become verified without reconciliation.
- A classification hides its factual and legal basis.
- A refund estimate appears authority-approved.
- A provider submits without exact taxpayer review.
- Transmission is labeled accepted or complete.
- Filing and payment states collapse.
- A notice loses its deadline, rights or source return.

## Anti-patterns

- Profile-as-tax-status.
- Imported-equals-proven.
- Black-box tax category.
- Refund certainty theater.
- Delegated blind filing.
- Transmit equals file.
- Return-payment collapse.
- Notice as support ticket.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is the exact taxpayer, obligation, jurisdiction, period, form, authority, due date and representative scope preserved?
- Are identity, residence, ownership, earner, payer, dependent and representative roles distinguished?
- Can every reported value be traced to current source evidence and visible edits or conflicts?
- Can people inspect and correct the facts, rules and assumptions behind classifications?
- Can material calculations be reconstructed and are projected, filed, assessed and paid values distinct?
- Does the taxpayer review the exact return and declaration before a clearly scoped submission authorization?
- Are transmission, authority acceptance, rejection, amendment and finality represented separately?
- Are filing, payment, refund and account-balance states kept distinct with durable receipts?
- Do notices preserve deadlines, rights, evidence, response and linkage to the source return?
- Are tax records private, accessible, portable and available through the required retention period?
