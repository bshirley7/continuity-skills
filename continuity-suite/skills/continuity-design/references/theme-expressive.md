# Expressive

## Generalized principles

- Assign expressive elements a job—explain a mental model, motivate progress, identify a content category, or create a focal action—and remove ornament that competes with task comprehension.
- Anchor personality to operational structure through visible progress, checklists, object state, stable navigation, and one unmistakable next action.
- Keep content and artifact surfaces readable by containing strong color, illustration, and dark framing within deliberate regions rather than applying equal intensity everywhere.
- Use consistent image treatment, typography, tags, and navigation to turn varied expressive content into a coherent system.
- For consequential creation and publishing, preserve scope, current state, preview, management controls, and explicit confirmation regardless of visual personality.
- Use voice, character, and illustration to support comprehension and motivation while retaining skip, close, back, and recovery where the journey is optional or interruptible.

## Variation levers

- Use illustration for concepts and photography for content or place.
- Concentrate saturated color in focal actions, tags, selected navigation, and bounded panels.
- Use conversational voice for guided setup and editorial voice for discovery.
- Use dark framing for professional creative tools and light framing for consumer guidance.
- Vary personality intensity by task consequence and content dominance.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Distinctiveness improves recall while increasing competition for attention.
- Illustration supports explanation while consuming space.
- Conversational voice motivates while becoming intrusive in repeated expert workflows.
- Media richness improves discovery while reducing information density.
- Dark or saturated tool frames create identity while overpowering the artifact.

## Failure modes

- Decorative imagery has no explanatory or navigational role.
- Multiple saturated regions compete for primary attention.
- Brand expression obscures progress, state, or recovery.
- Content cards use inconsistent crops and hierarchy.
- Publishing or management state is hidden behind visual flourish.
- Character or voice blocks skipping or efficient repeat use.

## Anti-patterns

- Decoration-first empty states.
- Every surface using the brand accent.
- Oversized display type for operational details.
- Illustrations replacing instructions.
- Unbounded gradients behind dense content.
- Expressive confirmation without scope or consequence.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does each expressive element serve comprehension, motivation, categorization, or action?
- Are progress, object state, navigation, and next action explicit?
- Is the core content or artifact still readable?
- Are imagery, typography, tags, and navigation systematic?
- Are publishing scope and consequence preserved?
- Can users skip, close, recover, or work efficiently on repeat visits?
