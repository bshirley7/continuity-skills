# Memory, learning, and experience continuity

## Generalized principles

- Externalize task memory by preserving entered data, choices, prior steps, current state, next action, dependencies, and consequences in the interface; prefer recognition and visible context to unaided recall, and never require a person to remember information the system already holds unless memory itself is the chosen learning objective.
- Design every interruptible task for safe resumption: save a truthful checkpoint, retain the working context, identify what changed while the person was away, distinguish completed from merely started work, and provide a stable cue that restores the suspended goal and likely next step without forcing reconstruction.
- When durable learning is the explicit user goal, support effortful retrieval, corrective feedback, and appropriately spaced return rather than repeated passive exposure; disclose the learning purpose, adapt difficulty without humiliation, permit review and alternative formats, and do not turn productive difficulty into a barrier for ordinary task completion.
- Match retrieval support to the information and future use: combine meaningful labels, examples, structure, and distinctive but accessible visual cues; do not assume pictures are inherently more memorable, because cue type, distinctiveness, prior knowledge, modality, and the required recall operation change the result.
- Treat sequence and prominence as allocation decisions rather than mnemonic guarantees: repeat or otherwise reinforce essential middle content, keep critical actions available at the moment of use, and test delayed comprehension because primacy, recency, distinctiveness, task type, delay, age, and interference can produce different recall patterns.
- Separate lived quality from remembered evaluation: measure burden, time, errors, recovery, accessibility, and value throughout the journey as well as immediate and delayed recollection; improve painful or difficult moments themselves and never add burden, manipulate an ending, or conceal total cost merely to produce a more favorable memory.
- Evaluate continuity across people, sessions, devices, modalities, and time using realistic interruptions and delayed return: test whether users can identify prior commitments, source and freshness of state, completed and remaining work, changes, ownership, and a safe next action without privileged recall or accidental duplication.

## Method

- Identify what the person must know, recognize, remember, learn, resume, or decide at each point and whether that memory demand is intrinsic.
- Map persisted data, checkpoints, history, drafts, ownership, device boundaries, interruptions, expiry, changes, and recovery paths.
- Separate ordinary task completion from an explicit learning objective before introducing retrieval difficulty, repetition, or testing.
- Prototype uninterrupted use, interruption before and after commitment, delayed return, cross-device return, changed state, missing state, and recovery.
- Test recognition, unaided recall only where relevant, resumption accuracy, duplicate work, delayed comprehension, transfer, and subgroup burden.
- Measure the lived journey and delayed recollection separately, including total time, peak burden, ending, errors, agency, regret, and value.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not create memory demand to increase effort, lock-in, repeated exposure, dependency, or data surrender.
- Do not treat forgetting as user failure when the product can preserve or present the needed context.
- Do not insert quizzes, recall gates, streaks, or spaced prompts into ordinary tasks without a user-chosen learning purpose.
- Do not use remembered-experience effects to justify extra pain, delay, friction, concealment, or a cosmetically improved ending.
- Do not infer comprehension from completion, immediate recognition, satisfaction, or repeated use.
- Do not erase prior state, silently merge revisions, or make stale context appear current.

## Variation levers

- Use persistent step summaries for complex or consequential work and lightweight recent-state cues for short reversible tasks.
- Increase checkpoint detail with interruption likelihood, elapsed time, collaboration, cost, and consequence.
- Use active retrieval and spacing only for explicit learning; use visible answers, autofill, and recognition support for task execution.
- Combine words and visuals when each adds meaning; preserve text and nonvisual alternatives rather than relying on imagery alone.
- Repeat critical content based on risk and delayed comprehension, not a fixed first-or-last placement rule.
- Increase provenance, freshness, diff, and ownership detail when state may change outside the current session.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Visible context reduces memory burden while dense history can overwhelm attention.
- Automatic restoration speeds resumption while stale or cross-context state can cause harmful assumptions.
- Retrieval effort supports learning while impairing access when learning is not the task.
- Distinctive cues aid finding and memory while novelty can distract or lose meaning across cultures and modalities.
- Repetition reinforces essentials while increasing length and habituation.
- A strong ending can support closure while masking the burden of the full journey.

## Failure modes

- A multi-step flow asks for information already supplied on an earlier screen.
- Returning users see a blank editor with no checkpoint, change summary, or next-step cue.
- A learning mechanic is added to an operational task solely to increase engagement.
- An unlabeled illustration replaces the words or state needed to act.
- Critical middle content is assumed remembered because it appeared once.
- A pleasant confirmation screen is used to excuse avoidable friction or harm earlier in the journey.
- Restored state is stale, belongs to another context, or duplicates work without warning.

## Anti-patterns

- Remember what we already know.
- Blank return.
- Quiz as friction.
- Picture means memorable.
- First and last are enough.
- Good ending excuses bad journey.
- Restore without provenance.
- Completion means comprehension.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Which memory demands are intrinsic, and which can be replaced by visible context, saved data, recognition, or assistance?
- Can a person resume after realistic interruption or delay with a truthful checkpoint, changes, source, and safe next action?
- Is effortful retrieval limited to a user-chosen learning objective with feedback, review, alternatives, and no humiliation?
- Do words, examples, structure, and visual cues match the retrieval task and remain accessible without assuming picture superiority?
- Are critical middle and delayed-use details reinforced and tested rather than relying on first-or-last placement?
- Does evaluation distinguish lived burden from remembered experience and prohibit adding harm for a favorable recollection?
- Can people verify freshness, ownership, revisions, completed work, and remaining work across sessions, devices, and collaborators?
