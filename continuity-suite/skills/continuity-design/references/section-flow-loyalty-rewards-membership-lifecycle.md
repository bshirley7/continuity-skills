# Loyalty, rewards, and membership lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Represent the program as three linked but distinct systems: an earned-value ledger, a time-bound status qualification contract, and a redemption marketplace; name which balance or progress measure each surface shows; do not imply that nominal points equal cash, that status progress is spendable, or that an available reward is already entitled.
- Make every balance self-describing: show unit, available amount, pending amount, approximate or guaranteed value where meaningful, as-of time, next expiry, restrictions, and a route to activity; separate earned, promotional, transferred, reversed, redeemed and expired entries; and never collapse unsettled or conditional value into an immediately usable total.
- Explain earning before commitment: identify the qualifying action or spend, rate, threshold, cap, exclusions, partner, activation requirement, posting delay, promotion period, return or cancellation effect, and whether status and spendable value accrue differently; preserve the offer terms with the resulting ledger entry.
- Treat tier progress as a dated measurement, not a motivational ornament: show current level, qualification period, qualifying measure, completed amount, remaining requirement, active benefits, locked benefits, renewal or downgrade date, grace rules, and what happens to benefits already booked or earned; avoid artificial head starts or near-complete visuals that obscure the true denominator.
- Make redemption value and feasibility inspectable before selection: show point price, cash equivalent or comparison basis where supportable, points-plus-cash composition, inventory or capacity, eligible item or service, taxes and fees, minimums, exclusions, expiry, cancellation effect, transferability, confirmation, and remaining balance; retain a clear choice to save value rather than redeem now.
- Give expiry, devaluation and program change durable notice: identify the exact affected balance or benefit, old and new rule or value, effective date, reason, preservation or redemption options, and support path; do not use resetting urgency, hide material changes in generic terms, or retroactively alter a completed earning contract without explicit authority and remedy.
- Provide a correctable, exportable activity ledger: connect each earning, adjustment, transfer, redemption, reversal and expiry to date, source transaction, calculation, status and policy; support missing-value claims, unauthorized-activity review, duplicate correction, and reasoned outcomes; restore proven loss and preserve history across partner handoffs.
- Make entry and exit consequences proportionate: disclose required data, linked accounts, partner sharing, age, device and geography limits; provide accessible non-smartphone alternatives where the program affects ordinary prices or essential access; explain closure, inactivity, transfer and deletion effects on earned value and booked benefits; and never require unrelated tracking or future marketing as the hidden price of using earned value.

## Variation levers

- Use money-equivalent display only when the conversion is stable and actually redeemable.
- Increase qualification-period and downgrade detail for travel and status programs.
- Increase settlement, reversal and dispute detail for payment-linked rewards.
- Increase inventory, expiration and substitution detail for item catalogs.
- Provide offline enrollment and identification when member pricing affects routine essentials.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Simple balances motivate while hiding heterogeneous value and restrictions.
- Progress visualization encourages completion while amplifying sunk-cost pressure.
- Flexible redemption expands choice while making comparison and exchange rates harder.
- Partner ecosystems expand earning while fragmenting responsibility and privacy control.
- Expiry limits liabilities while creating breakage and urgency.
- Personalization improves relevance while increasing data collection and unequal offers.

## Failure modes

- Points appear without usable value, restrictions or expiry.
- Pending and available value are merged.
- A promotion hides exclusions, caps or posting delay until after purchase.
- Tier progress omits the qualification period or downgrade rule.
- A reward appears available but cannot be used for the selected item, date or location.
- Devaluation is announced only through generic terms.
- Refunds or cancellations reverse value without a traceable calculation.
- Missing points require serial contact across partners.
- Account closure silently destroys earned value.
- Member pricing excludes people who cannot use the required device or enrollment path.

## Anti-patterns

- Promotional balance fiction.
- Pending-is-spendable.
- Earn-now-terms-later.
- Timeless tier treadmill.
- Catalog availability mirage.
- Silent devaluation.
- Invisible reversal.
- Partner support carousel.
- Exit-value forfeiture.
- Device-gated fair price.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are ledger, qualification, and redemption represented as distinct contracts?
- Does every balance expose unit, availability, pending state, value basis, age, expiry and activity?
- Are earning rates, thresholds, exclusions, caps, partners, posting times and reversal rules visible before action?
- Does tier progress show period, denominator, active and locked benefits, renewal and downgrade?
- Can people judge redemption value, feasibility, restrictions, fees and remaining balance before confirmation?
- Are expiry and devaluation notices exact, durable, prospective and repairable?
- Can people trace, correct, contest and recover every material ledger change?
- Are enrollment, data use, accessibility, partner sharing and exit consequences proportionate and explicit?
