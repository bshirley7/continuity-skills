# Retention and lifecycle continuity

## Generalized principles

- Make ongoing value inspectable through the user's own durable state: current plan or program, saved work, progress, usage, benefits already received, next meaningful action, and the future state that continuation enables; do not substitute generic promotion for earned continuity.
- Treat return and resumption as restoration rather than restart: preserve object identity, prior choices, completion state, next step, remaining effort, paused or expired status, and a low-friction route back into meaningful work.
- Keep plan identity, price, billing cadence, renewal or trial-conversion date, included scope, usage limits, payment source, invoices, and change routes together so continued commitment can be reviewed before it renews.
- Make pause, downgrade, cancellation, and refusal reachable and understandable; disclose effective date, remaining access, retained progress or credits, lost capabilities, billing consequence, confirmation state, and later reactivation without requiring optional feedback or retention offers.

## Variation levers

- Lead with saved task state for work and learning products, benefit and usage state for memberships, and billing state for subscriptions.
- Offer pause, downgrade, schedule change, or reduced scope only when each option is genuinely available and its future billing and access effects are explicit.
- Use a concise welcome-back recap for short absences and a fuller state review when access, ownership, price, data, or underlying conditions changed.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- A richer continuity recap improves orientation while risking an overloaded return surface.
- Explaining benefits can support an informed decision while becoming pressure when it obscures cancellation.
- Preserving progress supports return while requiring clear retention, privacy, and deletion boundaries.
- Targeted offers can address price or cadence mismatch while exploiting urgency or making refusal harder.

## Failure modes

- The return experience promotes new content but hides saved work and the next unfinished action.
- Progress or streak state is reset, inflated, or detached from meaningful outcomes.
- Renewal timing, billing cadence, usage limits, or trial conversion appears only after commitment.
- Cancellation feedback or a retention offer blocks access to the actual cancellation action.
- The terminal state does not confirm effective date, remaining access, billing status, retained state, or reactivation path.

## Anti-patterns

- Welcome back to a blank slate.
- Promotion instead of continuity.
- Streak as value.
- Renewal by obscurity.
- Mandatory exit interview.
- Discount gauntlet.
- Cancelled but unclear.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does the return surface restore saved state and the next meaningful action rather than reset the journey?
- Can people inspect plan, price, cadence, renewal date, included scope, usage, payment, and invoices before continued commitment?
- Are pause, downgrade, cancellation, and refusal reachable without optional feedback or offers?
- Are effective date, remaining access, retained progress or credits, lost capabilities, billing state, and reactivation explicit?
- Do progress and benefit claims reflect meaningful user outcomes rather than attention alone?
- If conditions changed during absence, does the product explain the change before resumption?
