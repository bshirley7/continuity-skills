# Finance

## Generalized principles

- Explain regulated data requests in plain language by naming what is collected, why it is necessary, and what the user will do next.
- Place destination, amount, fees, total, timing, and the irreversible action together in the final transaction review.
- Make risk guidance specific to the current decision while preserving a concise summary of its financial consequences.
- Distinguish available financial state from trends, discovery, and promotion through hierarchy and stable grouping.
- Expose data freshness and transaction status wherever delayed or aggregated information could change interpretation.

## Variation levers

- Use a brief rationale plus expandable detail for routine verification, and a fuller disclosure when consent or retention terms materially differ.
- Show fee arithmetic inline for variable-cost transfers and a compact labeled summary when costs are fixed or absent.
- Increase confirmation friction according to irreversibility, fraud exposure, recipient novelty, and monetary risk.
- Prioritize available balance for operational banking and net position or category comparison for planning dashboards.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Full disclosure supports consent while making the immediate decision harder to scan.
- Additional confirmation friction can prevent loss while delaying frequent legitimate actions.
- Promotional discovery can improve product adoption while competing with account status and trust.
- Aggregated simplicity can aid comprehension while concealing freshness, pending state, or account-level exceptions.

## Failure modes

- Legal language is presented without a plain-language decision summary.
- The final transaction action is separated from recipient, fee, timing, or total details.
- A generic warning adds anxiety without explaining the relevant risk or recovery path.
- Available, pending, projected, and historical amounts are visually indistinguishable.
- Promotions or discovery modules receive the same emphasis as core financial state.
- Aggregated values omit freshness or synchronization status.

## Anti-patterns

- Using compliance as an unexplained justification for sensitive-data collection.
- Hiding fees behind expandable controls at the final confirmation point.
- Relying on color alone to distinguish positive, negative, pending, or risky state.
- Adding urgency or celebratory motion to an irreversible financial action.
- Presenting an account total without clarifying whether funds are available, pending, or projected.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can the user explain why sensitive information is required and what happens after submission?
- Can the user verify recipient, amount, fees, total, and timing without leaving the final review?
- Does risk guidance describe the current decision rather than repeat a generic warning?
- Are available, pending, projected, and historical values distinguishable without relying only on color?
- Is the freshness or synchronization state visible wherever it changes interpretation?
- Do promotions remain subordinate to account state and high-stakes actions?
