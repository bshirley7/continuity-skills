# Shape and form language

## Generalized principles

- Define a form taxonomy before selecting radii or decoration: distinguish content, container, control, input, navigation, object, status, selection, overlay, evidence, and decorative shapes; assign each family a semantic purpose, interaction behavior, and relationship to the broader visual identity.
- Specify a coherent geometric character through corner profile, curvature, angle, stroke, silhouette, proportion, cutout, and repetition while allowing semantic families to differ enough that controls, content, state, and decoration do not become interchangeable.
- Use shape redundantly with label, position, color, contrast, icon, and programmatic semantics; never make radius, outline, fill, capsule form, or silhouette the only signal for action, selection, status, availability, or consequence.
- Tie signature forms to product subject, behavior, or artifact and bound where they appear; let expressive geometry frame marketing, teaching, creation, or identity moments while preserving familiar and efficient forms for frequent, dense, assistive, or consequential work.
- Design form across state and scale: define default, hover, focus, pressed, selected, disabled, unavailable, loading, error, drag, compact, expanded, nested, and responsive behavior while maintaining target size, focus visibility, content fit, and recognizable object identity.
- Govern combinations and exceptions: document which shapes may nest, touch, overlap, merge, clip media, contain text, carry shadows, or break the dominant geometry; test whether cumulative use creates hierarchy or an undifferentiated field of pills, cards, boxes, and motifs.

## Variation levers

- Use softer continuous geometry for approachable, conversational, or low-arousal expression.
- Use sharper and more orthogonal geometry for precision, density, technical structure, or strong alignment.
- Reserve capsules for compact labels, filters, statuses, or bounded actions rather than every container.
- Use expressive silhouettes in identity and teaching moments while retaining conventional control targets.
- Increase outline and container differentiation when color, depth, or surface contrast is constrained.
- Reduce decorative shape variety in dense, high-frequency, or consequential workflows.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Consistent geometry creates recognition while making unlike roles look equivalent.
- Rounded forms feel approachable while consuming space and weakening dense alignment.
- Sharp geometry feels precise while appearing severe or reducing touch affordance.
- Custom silhouettes create identity while complicating content fit, responsiveness, and accessibility.
- More containers clarify grouping while producing nested-card clutter.
- Fewer boundaries reduce noise while weakening action, state, and grouping cues.

## Failure modes

- One radius and container style is applied to every object regardless of role.
- Controls, content, status, and decoration share indistinguishable shapes.
- Selection or state depends only on outline, fill, or silhouette.
- Signature geometry appears everywhere and competes with task content.
- Nested cards and pills create ambiguous hierarchy and excessive padding.
- Shapes fail with long text, localization, zoom, compact layouts, or missing media.
- Focus, pressed, disabled, drag, error, and loading forms are unspecified.
- Decorative clipping hides evidence or materially relevant image content.

## Anti-patterns

- Radius as brand strategy.
- Pill everything.
- Card inside card inside card.
- One shape for every role.
- Shape-only state.
- Signature silhouette everywhere.
- Decorative clipping of evidence.
- Default-state-only geometry.
- Geometry without responsive rules.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are content, container, control, input, navigation, object, status, selection, overlay, evidence, and decorative form families distinguished?
- Does the geometric character remain coherent without making unlike roles visually equivalent?
- Are action, selection, status, availability, and consequence redundantly encoded beyond shape?
- Are signature forms tied to product truth and bounded away from frequent or consequential work?
- Are interaction states, target size, focus, content fit, nesting, and responsive behavior specified?
- Are permitted nesting, overlap, clipping, media, shadow, and exception combinations governed?
- Does realistic cumulative use create hierarchy rather than pill, card, or box clutter?
