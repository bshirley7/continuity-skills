# Human agency, calibrated reliance, and automation boundaries

## Generalized principles

- Preserve meaningful human agency by distinguishing system proposal, recommendation, decision and action; make purpose, affected people, accountable actor, automation mode, authority, material consequence and refusal path explicit; retain genuine choice, manual alternatives where consequence warrants them, correction, withdrawal, intervention and remedy without using friction, anthropomorphism or urgency to manufacture acceptance.
- Calibrate reliance to capability and current evidence rather than fluency or generalized trust: communicate intended use, known limits, relevant performance and failure conditions, uncertainty, source and data coverage, freshness, distribution shift and verification needs at the decision point; evaluate whether people accept correct outputs and reject incorrect ones instead of treating use, satisfaction or confidence as proof of appropriate reliance.
- Match automation authority and oversight to consequence, reversibility, uncertainty and affected scope: make modes and transitions explicit, bound targets, tools, data, actions, duration and persistence, add risk-adjusted checkpoints and independent review, and ensure any human overseer has sufficient competence, time, information, authority and practical ability to understand, challenge, override, stop or decline the system.
- Make oversight actionable through inspectable state and real control: expose current mode, objective, plan, material inputs, source and tool evidence, outputs, uncertainty, anomalies, errors and changed assumptions; explain intervention effects and whether stop, rollback or compensation reaches queued, in-flight and completed actions; preserve traceability without exposing hidden reasoning, unrelated personal data or surveillance detail.
- Treat correction, feedback, memory and evaluation as separate governed loops: never infer consent to retain, learn from or reuse interactions; preserve provenance, scope, expiry and contestability; test complementary human-system performance, overreliance, underreliance, automation bias, false acceptance and rejection, workload, accessibility, group effects and delayed harms; and hand off exact state, evidence, limitations, unresolved risks and accountable next action.

## Method

- Classify each system output as proposal, recommendation, decision or action and identify purpose, affected parties, accountable actor, consequence, reversibility and automation mode.
- Document capability boundaries, relevant performance and failure conditions, uncertainty, sources, coverage, freshness, distribution shift and required external verification.
- Map every tool, data source, target, action, duration, persistence and memory behavior to explicit authority, checkpoint, revocation and stop semantics.
- Design oversight around evidence, intervention and recovery: state, changes, anomalies, errors, challenge, correction, override, pause, stop, rollback, compensation, appeal and handoff.
- Evaluate correct acceptance, correct rejection, false acceptance, false rejection, overreliance, underreliance, workload, accessibility, group burden, delayed effects and remedy.
- Review whether nominal human control is usable in practice given competence, time, information, incentives, authority and organizational pressure.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not anthropomorphize a system in ways that imply intention, care, understanding, authority or accountability it does not possess.
- Do not claim meaningful human oversight when the person lacks time, competence, evidence, independence, authority or an effective intervention path.
- Do not transfer responsibility to a user for errors caused by invisible inputs, hidden limitations, inaccessible evidence or organizational pressure to accept automation.
- Do not infer broad task or action authority from conversational engagement, account connection, prior acceptance or general terms.
- Do not use attention, engagement, acceptance rate, confidence, satisfaction or absence of complaint as evidence of correctness, welfare or appropriate reliance.
- Do not retain interaction history as persistent memory, training data or cross-task personalization without explicit purpose, scope, control, correction and deletion.
- Do not remove a workable manual or human escalation path where automated error can materially affect rights, safety, livelihood, access or irreversible outcomes.
- Do not convert observability into worker surveillance or expose affected non-users, sensitive inputs or internal reasoning beyond what oversight and remedy require.

## Variation levers

- Use low-friction assistance for reversible low-consequence drafting and stronger checkpoints, verification and independent review for consequential decisions or external actions.
- Use explanations suited to the decision and likely failure mode rather than assuming any explanation improves reliance.
- Use continuous supervision for fast-changing or safety-critical action and periodic checkpoints for bounded asynchronous work with reliable stop semantics.
- Increase affected-person notice, challenge, appeal, audit and remedy as automation shapes access, rights, employment, finance, health, safety or public services.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- More autonomy can reduce workload while weakening predictability and timely intervention.
- More explanation can improve understanding while increasing cognitive burden, false reassurance or exposure of sensitive information.
- Frequent confirmation can preserve agency while creating habituation and acceptance fatigue.
- Detailed monitoring can support oversight while becoming surveillance or distracting from material evidence.
- Personalization can improve relevance while making behavior less predictable and expanding memory, consent and correction obligations.

## Failure modes

- The system's conversational fluency is allowed to imply competence, certainty, intention or accountability.
- A nominal reviewer cannot see material inputs, limitations or consequences and cannot realistically stop the system.
- Confidence, explanation or source count substitutes for task-specific verification.
- Automation mode, authority or persistence changes without clear notice and control.
- A stop control does not cover queued, in-flight or downstream effects.
- User feedback silently becomes persistent memory, training data or broader personalization.
- Evaluation measures adoption or satisfaction but not correct acceptance, correct rejection, workload, group effects or delayed harm.
- Affected people cannot learn that automation was used, challenge an outcome, obtain human review or receive remedy.

## Anti-patterns

- Fluency means competence.
- Human in the loop by label.
- Trust score as safety.
- Explanation guarantees reliance.
- Connection implies consent.
- Stop without effect.
- Feedback becomes memory.
- Adoption means success.
- Manual path removed.
- Observability as surveillance.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are proposal, recommendation, decision and action distinct, with purpose, affected people, accountable actor, mode, authority, consequence, refusal, correction and remedy visible?
- Are capability, limitations, relevant performance, failure conditions, uncertainty, sources, coverage, freshness, distribution shift and verification needs shown where reliance occurs?
- Does automation authority match consequence, reversibility and uncertainty, and can the overseer realistically understand, challenge, override, stop or decline it?
- Are state, objective, plan, material inputs, tools, outputs, anomalies, errors, changed assumptions and intervention effects inspectable without surveillance theater?
- Are correction, feedback, memory, retention, learning and reuse separate, purpose-bound, controllable, contestable and deletable?
- Does evaluation cover appropriate reliance, false acceptance and rejection, workload, accessibility, group effects, delayed harms, affected-person challenge and remedy?
