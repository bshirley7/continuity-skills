# Accessible media and multimodal content

## Generalized principles

- Make every available alternative discoverable before commitment and controllable during use: identify captions, subtitle and spoken languages, narrated visual alternatives, complete multimodal transcripts, sign interpretation, chapters and text equivalents; distinguish human-reviewed, generated, translated, live, delayed, partial and unavailable states; disclose material limits without making people start playback or reveal a disability to learn what is offered.
- Keep media, alternatives and navigation synchronized without trapping attention: preserve speaker labels, meaningful sounds, timestamps, reading order and current segment; support transcript search, select-to-seek, pauseable auto-follow, keyboard and assistive-technology operation; maintain playback position when opening settings or text and avoid moving focus without request.
- Preserve sensory, language and temporal agency with visible per-item controls and understandable preference scope: support caption presentation, language, audio track, volume, mute, speed, autoplay, motion and flash reduction where relevant; honor system and account defaults, allow safe session overrides, explain precedence and persistence, and use an accessible fallback when a preferred track or device capability is absent.
- Treat generated and translated alternatives as fallible published content: retain source-media and language provenance, distinguish correction from destructive media editing, support words, punctuation, speakers, timing and meaningful non-speech cues, identify uncertain or inaudible passages, provide review and publication state, and bind revisions and exports to the matching media version.
- Handle live and partially processed media honestly: show whether captions or transcripts are available, their language, delay, speaker-label limits and recording status; preserve a usable fallback when the service degrades; explain whether a corrected post-event record will follow; and protect participant notice, consent, access, retention and deletion around captured speech.
- Provide an equivalent route to the essential outcome, not merely an attached text dump: carry instructions, decisions, actions, alerts, assessments, links and important visual or auditory context into the appropriate alternative; keep interactive options operable; and test whether a person can understand and complete the task through the chosen combination of modalities.

## Variation levers

- Increase review, accuracy disclosure and human correction with consequence, specialized vocabulary, multiple speakers, live latency and translation.
- Offer both synchronized and independent transcript modes when people need either contextual following or self-paced reading and search.
- Persist preferences at account, device or session scope according to sensitivity and shared-device risk, always retaining a visible per-item override.
- Use integrated narration during production when possible and alternate tracks or versions when essential visual information cannot fit natural pauses.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Automatic alternatives improve coverage and speed while introducing silent errors, timing drift and false confidence.
- Auto-follow preserves synchronization while disrupting reading, focus and assistive-technology navigation.
- Persisted preferences reduce repeated setup while exposing sensitive choices on shared devices or overriding situational needs.
- Rich customization supports individual access while fragmenting controls and making basic alternatives hard to find.
- Live capture enables participation while recording voices, identities and sensitive conversation.

## Failure modes

- Accessibility alternatives are disclosed only after playback begins or inside a deep settings menu.
- A caption badge does not state language, generation, review, delay or completeness.
- Transcript text omits speakers, meaningful sounds, visual context, links or interactive actions.
- Selecting transcript text loses the media position or creates an inaccessible focus jump.
- Generated text is published without review, correction, provenance or version linkage.
- A preference silently follows the user onto a shared device or is overridden without notice.
- Live caption failure disappears without status, fallback or post-event remedy.
- Controls cannot be reached or understood without the sensory channel they modify.
- Audio, motion or autoplay begins before the person can apply a preference.
- The alternative exists but cannot support the essential task or assessment.

## Anti-patterns

- Hidden alternatives.
- Caption badge theater.
- Passive text dump.
- Seek that steals focus.
- Immutable automation.
- Sensitive preference leakage.
- Silent live failure.
- Inaccessible accessibility control.
- Preference after playback.
- Equivalent in name only.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can people discover available alternatives, language, provenance, latency and completeness before and during use without disclosing a disability?
- Do captions and transcripts preserve speakers, meaningful sounds, timing, current position, search, selection, keyboard access and user-controlled following?
- Are sensory, language, speed, autoplay and motion preferences visibly controllable with clear scope, precedence, persistence and fallback?
- Can generated or translated text be reviewed, corrected, versioned, published and exported without confusing text correction with source-media editing?
- Are live availability, delay, failure, consent, retention and post-event correction represented honestly?
- Can the essential information, action and outcome be understood and completed through the chosen combination of modalities?
