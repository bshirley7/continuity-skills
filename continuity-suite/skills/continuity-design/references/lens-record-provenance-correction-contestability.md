# Record provenance, correction, and contestability

## Generalized principles

- Represent consequential data as attributable assertions: preserve the entity described, value and meaning, source or primary record, responsible actor or system, collection or generation activity, observed and effective time, method, derivation and transformation, version, verification scope, and invalidation state so users can judge fitness, reliability, and current authority.
- Separate observation from interpretation and current use: distinguish source value, user assertion, normalized value, inferred or enriched value, verified fact, disputed claim, correction, and downstream copy; explain which version is authoritative for which purpose and never let presentation order, confidence, recency, or repetition silently convert an inference into truth.
- Provide an accessible field-level correction path: let the affected person identify the inaccurate or incomplete item, inspect its source and current uses, propose a corrected value or completion, provide proportionate evidence or context, use an authorized representative where applicable, receive a durable reference and status, and avoid unnecessary fees, retaliation, coercion, or collection of unrelated sensitive material.
- Make review and outcome contestable: pause or visibly qualify consequential reliance on genuinely disputed data when proportionate, conduct a competent and independent investigation, consider the source, purpose, evidence, and person’s statement, preserve reasons and evidence for acceptance or denial, expose timing and escalation, attach unresolved disagreement to the record, and do not require proof that is impossible, irrelevant, or more intrusive than the claim warrants.
- Treat correction as a distributed lifecycle rather than a local edit: preserve the original assertion and linked amendment, identify recipients and derived systems, propagate correction or restriction within a reasonable time, disclose partial or failed updates, notify the person of outcome and affected recipients where appropriate, re-evaluate downstream decisions, and verify that stale copies no longer act as current truth.

## Method

- Inventory consequential record fields, purposes, sources, transformations, versions, recipients, derived systems, decisions, retention rules, and responsible stewards.
- Model source assertions, normalized values, inferences, verification, disputes, corrections, invalidation, and downstream copies as distinct linked states.
- Design field-level provenance disclosure and a correction request that asks only for information proportionate to the disputed claim.
- Specify acknowledgement, status, reviewer competence and independence, evidence handling, response time, reasons, disagreement statement, escalation, and protection from adverse use during review.
- Map propagation to recipients, caches, exports, models, indexes, automations, and prior decisions; show partial failure and verify completion.
- Test missing sources, conflicting authorities, impossible evidence demands, repeated denials, reinserted errors, stale copies, hidden inferences, representative access, and sensitive third-party data.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not present imported, inferred, enriched, aggregated, or repeatedly copied data as verified fact without its source, method, time, scope, and uncertainty.
- Do not erase the original assertion, dispute, correction request, reviewer reasoning, denial, disagreement statement, or propagation history to make the record look clean.
- Do not use correction requests, uncertainty, disagreement, protected traits, or inability to produce disproportionate evidence as negative behavioral, trust, fraud, or risk signals.
- Do not require people to disclose unrelated sensitive data or third-party information to correct one bounded field.
- Do not leave disputed consequential data fully active without visible qualification, proportionate safeguards, or a timely human route.
- Do not claim a correction is complete when recipients, derived data, automated decisions, exports, caches, or models remain stale or unknown.

## Variation levers

- Use compact provenance labels for routine low-consequence data and expanded source, derivation, version, and authority detail for disputed or decision-bearing fields.
- Permit immediate user edits for self-asserted preferences and use reasoned review for externally sourced, regulated, identity-critical, or multi-party records.
- Temporarily restrict use, add a dispute marker, or require human confirmation while a challenge is reviewed according to consequence and reversibility.
- Use automated propagation for deterministic copies and owner-visible reconciliation queues when recipients, transformations, or legal obligations differ.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Detailed provenance supports trust and correction while exposing sensitive sources, relationships, or investigative methods.
- Preserving history supports accountability while conflicting with minimization, retention, and erasure expectations.
- Fast correction limits harm while weak verification can introduce new inaccuracies or affect third parties.
- Pausing disputed data protects people while potentially delaying legitimate service, safety, or compliance work.
- Broad propagation improves consistency while increasing coordination cost and the blast radius of a mistaken correction.

## Failure modes

- A material value has no visible source, time, responsible actor, derivation, version, verification scope, or authority.
- The interface collapses observed, inferred, normalized, disputed, corrected, and verified states into one current value.
- Correction is available only through an inaccessible generic support channel with no durable reference or status.
- The reviewer demands irrelevant or impossible proof, ignores the person’s statement, or provides no reasoned outcome.
- A denied correction removes the challenge instead of preserving a disagreement statement and escalation route.
- A local edit is marked complete while stale recipients, models, exports, caches, or prior decisions continue using the old data.
- The act of disputing a record becomes a hidden adverse signal.

## Anti-patterns

- Source unknown.
- Current equals true.
- Inference becomes fact.
- Correction starts over.
- Dispute as risk signal.
- Denied without reason.
- Updated here only.
- History rewritten.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can a person identify the source, actor or system, time, method, derivation, version, verification scope, invalidation state, and purpose-relative authority of consequential data?
- Are source assertions, user assertions, normalized values, inferences, verified facts, disputes, corrections, and downstream copies visibly distinct?
- Is there an accessible field-level correction route with proportionate evidence, representation, acknowledgement, durable status, timing, and no retaliation?
- Does review consider source, purpose, evidence, and the person’s statement, preserve reasons, support disagreement and escalation, and qualify consequential use during genuine dispute?
- Are the original assertion, linked amendment, recipients, propagation status, failed updates, downstream decisions, and stale copies visible and reconcilable?
- Do privacy, minimization, retention, third-party rights, security, and anti-discrimination safeguards constrain provenance and correction evidence?
