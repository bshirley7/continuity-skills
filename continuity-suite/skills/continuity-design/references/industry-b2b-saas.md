# B2B and SaaS

## Generalized principles

- Structure B2B onboarding around the operating model and accountable outcomes: separate identity, data connection, workspace structure, security, billing, member invitation, customization, and the first assigned or completed unit of real work.
- Distinguish required activation, recommended configuration, role-specific rollout, and optional plan expansion, showing prerequisites, progress, responsible control surface, and the operational capability each step unlocks.
- Design operational homes around current scope, pipeline or system state, exceptions, due work, owners, values, and direct role-relevant actions, connecting summaries to the exact customers, jobs, payments, tasks, or resources that require attention.
- Retire setup prominence as activation completes and return hierarchy to ongoing operations; completed guidance may remain accessible but must not permanently outrank demand, risk, commitments, and due work.
- Make administrative scope explicit across organization, workspace, product, team, and individual levels, showing effective policy, role, authentication, member or seat population, entitlement, save state, and audit history together.
- Tie security controls to affected identities, rollout timing, session behavior, data retention, domain or network boundary, provider, plan availability, and a direct route to inspect or change the governed people.
- Tie billing to current plan and lifecycle, included and trial products, seats, members, guests, viewers, usage or entitlement, invoices, payment method, billing contacts, support, upgrade, and account-exit consequences.
- Anchor collaboration to the exact task, record, document, or resource and keep owner, status, priority, dates, dependencies, evidence, watchers, comments, and attributable history distinct but mutually inspectable.
- Represent assignment, discussion, state change, dependency, approval, notification, subscription, and completion as different mechanisms with accountable actors and lifecycle; activity alone must not imply ownership or completion.
- Use role-based assistance only when it is grounded in current scope and visible evidence, preserves the underlying operational view, names the proposed outcome, and does not obscure exceptions or silently authorize changes.

## Variation levers

- Use an embedded working artifact for collaborative-product onboarding, an administrator checklist for organization rollout, and an in-product setup module for technical activation.
- Use role-specific home views when operational responsibilities differ and a shared organization overview when cross-functional state must reconcile.
- Use pipeline cards for staged work, exception queues for risk, calendars for time commitments, and tables for multi-object comparison.
- Use organization policy for security defaults, workspace overrides only when governance permits, and personal preferences for non-governed behavior.
- Use product-level entitlement cards for suites and a unified plan summary when one subscription governs the whole platform.
- Use record inspectors for structured field work, task detail for dependencies and deliverables, and audit tables for policy or administrative history.
- Use hidden-event collapsing for repetitive history while preserving counts and an explicit expansion path.
- Use assistants as contextual side panels for optional help and direct actions only after the proposed change is reviewed.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Comprehensive onboarding improves configuration while delaying the first meaningful outcome.
- Role-specific dashboards reduce noise while fragmenting the shared operational picture.
- Dense operational homes improve coverage while obscuring the few exceptions that need action.
- Organization-wide security simplifies governance while reducing workspace flexibility.
- Suite-level billing clarifies the portfolio while making product-specific entitlement harder to understand.
- Complete activity history supports accountability while overwhelming current-state review.
- Collapsing repeated events improves scanability while hiding evidence users may need.
- Contextual assistants accelerate work while obscuring data, proposing weakly grounded actions, or blurring authorization.

## Failure modes

- Onboarding completes account setup without data, team, security, billing, or first operational outcome.
- Required activation and optional plan expansion are visually conflated.
- A dashboard shows metrics without scope, exception, owner, due work, or drill-down.
- Completed setup permanently outranks ongoing operational priorities.
- Administrative controls do not identify organization, workspace, product, team, or individual scope.
- Security settings hide affected identities, rollout timing, sessions, retention, boundary, provider, or entitlement.
- Billing omits product inclusion, seats, audiences, lifecycle, invoices, payment, contacts, or exit.
- Comments and activity are detached from the task, record, evidence, dependency, or current state.
- A mention, comment, or event is treated as assignment, approval, or completion.
- An assistant obscures the dashboard or proposes changes without visible evidence and review.

## Anti-patterns

- Calling onboarding complete after creating an account.
- Putting upgrade tasks inside the required activation checklist.
- Using a generic KPI wall as the same home for every role.
- Leaving a completed setup banner above urgent work indefinitely.
- Changing organization policy from a control labeled only settings.
- Offering security toggles without rollout and population context.
- Showing one total bill with no product and seat breakdown.
- Flattening comments, assignments, dependencies, and approvals into one activity feed.
- Closing work because someone reacted or commented.
- Letting an assistant execute administrative changes from a generic prompt.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does onboarding cover identity, data, structure, security, billing, team, customization, and a real assigned or completed outcome?
- Are required activation, recommended configuration, role rollout, and optional expansion distinct?
- Does each operational home show scope, state, exceptions, due work, ownership, value, and direct drill-down?
- Does completed setup yield hierarchy to ongoing demand, risk, commitments, and due work?
- Are organization, workspace, product, team, and individual administrative scopes explicit?
- Do security controls expose identities, rollout, sessions, retention, boundaries, providers, entitlement, and governed people?
- Does billing expose plan, lifecycle, products, seats, audiences, invoices, payment, contacts, support, and exit?
- Are task or record ownership, status, dates, dependencies, evidence, watchers, comments, and history mutually inspectable?
- Are assignment, discussion, state change, dependency, approval, notification, subscription, and completion distinct?
- Is assistance grounded in current evidence, non-obscuring, outcome-specific, reviewable, and non-authorizing?
