# Collaboration

## Generalized principles

- Keep collaboration anchored to the exact artifact, object, passage, task, message, source evidence, or version under discussion, preserving author, time, location, and current state so feedback cannot drift from its subject.
- Represent discussion, assignment, state transition, decision, approval, and permission as distinct record types with their own owners and lifecycle; a comment, reaction, or personal done state must not imply team completion or authorization.
- Make handoffs explicit through one accountable owner, required outcome, due or review condition, dependency and source context, notification scope, and acceptance state, retaining the conversation as evidence rather than as the task itself.
- Bind review comments and approvals to an exact version, expose unresolved and resolved feedback separately, and require a fresh review when the artifact changes materially instead of carrying approval forward automatically.
- Distinguish live presence, recent activity, authorship, edit history, and audience reach; presence shows who is here, while activity and history establish what changed and by whom.
- When instructions, edits, or approvals conflict, pause the consequential change, identify the conflicting authors, objects, and versions, and request an explicit resolution rather than silently applying precedence.
- Convert conversation into durable work or decisions through an explicit linked action that carries owner, status, due condition, and source thread, leaving the original discussion intact and visibly connected.
- Separate named access, group or inherited access, invitation state, collaboration links, public publication, audience delivery, and export, showing role capabilities and the exact external reach before access is granted or a link is copied.
- Give users control over collaboration attention through unread, mention, follow-up, done-later, subscription, and notification states, keeping personal attention state distinct from shared artifact state.
- In AI-assisted collaboration, preserve who supplied feedback, what the system inferred, which edits were proposed or applied, what evidence was checked, and which human decision remains outstanding.

## Variation levers

- Use inline comments for exact passages or objects, side panels for review queues, threaded discussions for bounded conversation, and activity logs for chronological state changes.
- Use live presence for simultaneous editing, seen state for broadcast updates, and named acknowledgement or approval for consequential review.
- Use a single assignee for accountable handoff, collaborators for contributors, watchers for attention, and groups for inherited access.
- Use resolve and reopen for feedback lifecycle, complete and reopen for work lifecycle, and approve and revoke for version-bound authorization.
- Use named invitations for controlled access, group access for managed teams, expiring links for bounded external review, and publication for intentionally public content.
- Use reactions for lightweight acknowledgement, polls for preference gathering, decisions for committed choices, and linked tasks for required action.
- Use version-specific comment filters during review and cross-version history when tracing how feedback changed the artifact.
- Use notification defaults based on ownership and consequence, then allow personal tuning without changing shared state.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- More visible presence supports coordination while increasing surveillance pressure and distraction.
- Inline feedback preserves context while fragmenting discussion across many objects and versions.
- A single owner strengthens accountability while obscuring collective contribution and shared risk.
- Detailed activity history improves auditability while overwhelming the current task and exposing sensitive behavior.
- Broad links reduce access friction while weakening control over external reach, expiration, and future membership.
- Turning messages into tasks preserves action while duplicating context and creating competing records.
- Resolved comments reduce noise while hiding rationale that later reviewers may need.
- AI synthesis accelerates collaboration while blurring authorship, interpretation, and approval.

## Failure modes

- Feedback appears without a stable artifact, object, source, or version anchor.
- A comment, reaction, seen count, or personal done state is treated as assignment, decision, or approval.
- A handoff lacks one owner, required outcome, due or review condition, or source context.
- Approval survives a material version change without renewed review.
- Presence avatars are shown while edit history and authorship remain unavailable.
- Conflicting instructions are silently merged or resolved by recency alone.
- A request remains buried in a thread with no durable linked task or decision.
- A copied link omits role, external reach, inheritance, expiration, or publication consequence.
- Notification state changes shared work state or shared completion without explicit action.
- AI-applied edits omit the originating feedback, interpretation, verification, or outstanding human decision.

## Anti-patterns

- Using @mentions as a substitute for assignment.
- Using reactions as approval evidence.
- Marking a thread resolved when the linked work is still open.
- Showing comments from all versions without identifying which artifact they reference.
- Allowing anyone-with-link access by default.
- Labeling a role guest or editor without explaining its capabilities.
- Treating imported message history as a current decision record.
- Copying discussion into a task without linking back to the source thread.
- Conflating unread, follow-up, done-later, and completed states.
- Letting an assistant choose between contradictory teammate instructions without escalation.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is every feedback item anchored to an exact artifact, object, passage, task, source, or version?
- Are comments, reactions, assignments, state changes, decisions, approvals, and permissions visibly distinct?
- Does every handoff identify owner, required outcome, due or review condition, dependencies, and source context?
- Are unresolved, resolved, and reopened feedback bound to the reviewed version?
- Can users distinguish presence, authorship, recent activity, edit history, and audience reach?
- Do conflicting instructions pause consequential changes and identify what requires resolution?
- Can discussion become a linked task or decision without losing the original thread?
- Does sharing disclose named, group, inherited, link, public, audience, and export scope plus role capabilities?
- Can users manage attention without accidentally changing shared artifact or completion state?
- Does AI-assisted work preserve human feedback, system interpretation, applied edits, verification evidence, and outstanding decisions?
