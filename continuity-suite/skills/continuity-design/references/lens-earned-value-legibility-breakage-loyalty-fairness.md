# Earned-value legibility, breakage, and loyalty fairness

## Generalized principles

- Evaluate value as a user-understandable exchange contract, not a nominal score: distinguish units from money, status, eligibility and inventory; show the range or basis of usable value, material restrictions, fees and conversion variance; preserve the earning terms with the resulting value; and test whether a reasonable person can predict what effort produces and what the balance can actually obtain.
- Protect earned expectations across time: identify which terms governed earning, provide prospective and prominent notice of devaluation, expiry, revocation or partner change, state the exact affected value and effective date, offer a reasonable preservation or redemption window, and provide remedy when the service fails to honor represented conditions; never treat unilateral mutability as informed consent merely because broad terms existed.
- Audit progress mechanics for behavioral distortion: disclose the real denominator, period, qualifying events and resets; distinguish endowed or promotional progress from earned progress; avoid artificial advancement, near-complete framing, streak pressure and expiring urgency that cause effort disproportionate to usable value; and evaluate delayed financial, time and opportunity costs rather than completion alone.
- Treat breakage as a design outcome to govern, not invisible margin: measure earned value that expires, is revoked, cannot be redeemed, is abandoned through friction, or is lost at account closure; segment by value, disability, language, age, device access, geography and program tenure; identify whether reminders and redemption paths genuinely restore agency; and set safeguards when business value depends on people misunderstanding or failing to use what they earned.
- Preserve equitable access to ordinary prices and earned benefits: provide non-smartphone enrollment and identification where practical, avoid unnecessary age, identity, payment or channel barriers, expose accessibility semantics beyond color and animation, make status and expiry announcements programmatically available, and test whether disadvantaged groups bear higher effort, data disclosure or forfeiture for equivalent value.
- Apply purpose and partner boundaries to loyalty data: state what purchase, location, identity, device and behavioral data are collected, why, for how long, and with which program partners; separate necessary account administration from personalization, advertising and model improvement; provide correction and deletion paths; and explain how privacy choices affect earning, redemption, fraud review and retained value without coercive bundling.
- Make correction and restoration first-class rights: maintain an auditable ledger, let people report missing, duplicate, reversed or unauthorized activity, transfer existing evidence across partners, give specific reasons and calculations, provide human review for consequential loss, restore proven stolen or incorrectly removed value, and preserve appeal deadlines while a partner investigates.
- Evaluate exit without manufactured forfeiture: disclose inactivity, downgrade, unlinking, account closure, merger and program termination effects before commitment; provide export, transfer or redemption where promised and operationally feasible; separate deletion of personal data from silent destruction of disputed accounting evidence; and prevent closure friction from becoming a lever to retain tracking or spending.

## Method

- Map ledger, qualification, redemption, partner and account-exit contracts separately.
- Trace one earning event through pending, available, redeemed, reversed, expired and corrected states.
- Calculate usable value across representative redemption choices, restrictions, fees and dates.
- Review every progress and urgency cue for denominator truth, artificial advancement and delayed cost.
- Test access without a smartphone, with assistive technology, and across language, age and geography.
- Audit breakage, devaluation, correction, partner handoffs and closure outcomes by affected group.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not present nominal points as cash without a stable redeemable basis.
- Do not hide exclusions or reversals until after qualifying effort or spend.
- Do not use artificial progress or resetting urgency to manufacture sunk-cost pressure.
- Do not retroactively devalue earned value without authority, notice and remedy.
- Do not make ordinary prices inaccessible solely because a person lacks a smartphone.
- Do not bundle unrelated tracking or marketing into necessary account administration.
- Do not force people to repeat a missing-value case independently to every partner.
- Do not destroy disputed ledger evidence when a person requests account deletion or closure.

## Variation levers

- Increase monetary-equivalence evidence for financial and cashback programs.
- Increase period, downgrade and booked-benefit detail for travel status.
- Increase access-equity review when membership changes routine or essential prices.
- Increase privacy and partner-governance review for card-linked and location-aware rewards.
- Increase restoration and fraud-review controls for transferable or high-value balances.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Stable value supports trust while limiting dynamic inventory and economics.
- Frequent reminders reduce accidental expiry while increasing pressure and interruption.
- Fraud controls protect pooled value while burdening unusual or accessibility-mediated behavior.
- Personalization improves reward relevance while increasing surveillance and unequal offers.
- Easy exit protects agency while complicating fraud evidence, settlement and contractual obligations.
- Public tier recognition motivates some members while exposing status and spending inferences.

## Failure modes

- A large point balance has no comprehensible usable value.
- The program changes value after earning with only generic notice.
- A progress ring hides the measurement period and reset.
- Breakage is celebrated internally but never audited as user loss.
- Member prices require a smartphone even when offline enrollment is feasible.
- Purchase history is reused across partners without intelligible purpose controls.
- Unauthorized loss receives only a generic denial.
- Closing the account silently destroys value and correction evidence.

## Anti-patterns

- Nominal-value theater.
- Retroactive exchange rewrite.
- Denominator-free progress.
- Breakage as success metric.
- Device-gated parity.
- Loyalty surveillance bundle.
- Ledger denial loop.
- Exit forfeiture trap.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can people understand the actual exchange value, restrictions and uncertainty of every reward unit?
- Are earned expectations protected against retroactive devaluation, expiry, revocation and partner change?
- Do progress and urgency cues expose the true denominator, period, resets and delayed costs?
- Is breakage measured as user loss and segmented for unequal effects?
- Can people access prices and benefits without avoidable device, disability, age, language or geography barriers?
- Are loyalty data purposes, partners, retention and optional uses understandable and controllable?
- Can missing, reversed or stolen value be traced, contested and restored without partner repetition?
- Are downgrade, unlinking, deletion and closure consequences visible before action and free of manufactured forfeiture?
