# Choice, comparison, and decision integrity

## Generalized principles

- Define the decision contract before arranging options: identify the person’s goal, available alternatives, material attributes, total cost, risk, timing, scope, uncertainty, default, recommendation, consequence, reversibility, and valid no-action or defer path; present decision-changing information before commitment and retain it through review and confirmation.
- Make option sets comprehensible without manufacturing preference: group and order by a declared user-relevant logic, support comparison on consistent definitions and units, expose exclusions and uncertainty, preserve meaningful alternatives, and reduce avoidable complexity through filters, explanation, or staged comparison rather than arbitrary removal or a strategically inferior decoy.
- Treat defaults and recommendations as accountable decisions: state what is selected, why, for whose benefit, from which evidence, for what duration and scope, and how to change or refuse it with comparable understanding and effort; use neutral active choice when preferences are heterogeneous, stakes are material, or the responsible default cannot be justified.
- Audit framing across words, reference points, order, visual prominence, gains, losses, bundles, partitions, center position, and omitted context: compare equivalent presentations, require the same material facts and consequence under each frame, and never let a favorable description, reference price, installment, percentage, or risk format prevent reconstruction of the underlying outcome.
- Preserve temporal agency around commitment: distinguish real deadlines, expiring inventory, holds, delayed benefits, recurring costs, future risk, cooling-off, renewal, and cancellation; show the exact basis and affected scope of time or scarcity claims, support pause and later return, and do not use fatigue, impatience, sunk effort, or immediate reward to suppress long-term consequences.
- Evaluate decision quality rather than preferred-option uptake: test unaided comprehension, comparison accuracy, preference stability, refusal, deferment, correction, regret, task time, accessibility, and downstream outcome; compare neutral and proposed presentations, segment affected groups, and treat changed choice as a signal requiring explanation rather than proof of benefit.
- Review combinations and repeated exposure as one influence system: inventory defaults, ranking, visual hierarchy, friction, reminders, personalization, framing, scarcity, social signals, and exit across the complete journey; a locally mild device can become coercive when several mechanisms accumulate or when the person is stressed, inexperienced, financially constrained, disabled, or unable to leave.

## Method

- Write the decision contract, user outcome, alternatives, material attributes, default, recommendation, total consequence, reversibility, and no-action path.
- Inventory every influence mechanism across option set, order, comparison, language, reference point, visual prominence, friction, timing, personalization, reminders, and exit.
- Create a neutral baseline and reconstruct every option in consistent units, definitions, periods, denominators, and scopes.
- Prototype accept, refuse, defer, compare, revise, undo, withdraw, cancel, and return-later paths with preserved work.
- Test comprehension and preference before exposure, immediately after choice, and after a reflection period where consequence warrants it.
- Measure errors, inconsistency, refusal, correction, regret, downstream welfare, accessibility, and subgroup burden separately from conversion or completion.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not use a bias or behavioral effect as sufficient reason to increase selection of the organization’s preferred option.
- Do not fabricate or selectively scope scarcity, popularity, authority, savings, reference prices, urgency, defaults, or recommendations.
- Do not hide material cost, risk, renewal, data use, exclusion, or exit behind progressive disclosure or after commitment.
- Do not make refusal, deferment, correction, withdrawal, cancellation, or neutral choice less visible, understandable, or operable.
- Do not target pressure using vulnerability, fatigue, distress, inexperience, disability, financial constraint, or inferred susceptibility.
- Do not interpret completion, consent, retention, or increased spend as evidence of comprehension, preference, or welfare.

## Variation levers

- Use direct active choice for heterogeneous consequential preferences and a reversible justified default for familiar low-consequence settings.
- Use compact comparison for a few commensurable options and progressive comparison tools for larger or multidimensional sets.
- Increase reflection, review, provenance, and neutral explanation with consequence, irreversibility, uncertainty, recurrence, or personalization.
- Provide immediate completion for reversible routine choices and a resumable deliberation path for unfamiliar or high-cost commitments.
- Use recommendations only when their evidence, objective, alternatives, uncertainty, and conflicts can be made inspectable.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Reducing options lowers immediate burden while removing meaningful alternatives or disguising who selected the set.
- Defaults reduce effort while inheriting authority and silently changing outcomes.
- Personalized ranking increases relevance while weakening comparability and exposing people to opaque influence.
- More disclosure improves completeness while creating information overload when structure and comparison are weak.
- Protective friction can prevent harm while becoming obstruction when selectively applied to refusal or exit.
- Neutral presentation supports autonomy while no interface can eliminate every frame or reference point.

## Failure modes

- The option set is simplified by removing the alternative that best serves a minority or high-need group.
- A recommended or middle option appears natural even though its objective, evidence, sponsor, and tradeoffs are hidden.
- Monthly, percentage, or installment framing prevents reconstruction of total cost or downside.
- A real but irrelevant scarcity fact is displayed as if it governed the person’s exact choice.
- Refusal exists but requires more reading, steps, scrolling, repeated confirmation, or lost work.
- An experiment reports uptake without testing comprehension, consistency, regret, or downstream harm.
- Several mild steering mechanisms combine into substantial pressure across the journey.

## Anti-patterns

- Recommended because selected.
- Middle means best.
- More choice means more agency.
- Monthly instead of total.
- Real scarcity, wrong scope.
- Default without an accountable owner.
- Neutral wording with unequal friction.
- Conversion proves preference.
- One nudge at a time review.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does the decision contract expose alternatives, material attributes, total cost, risk, timing, uncertainty, scope, consequence, reversibility, and no-action or defer paths before commitment?
- Can people compare options using consistent definitions, units, periods, denominators, and evidence without a strategically inferior decoy?
- Is every default or recommendation justified by an explicit objective, evidence, beneficiary, duration, scope, and accessible refusal path?
- Can the underlying outcome be reconstructed regardless of gain, loss, installment, percentage, reference-price, visual, or center-position framing?
- Are urgency, scarcity, popularity, authority, and savings claims truthful, person-relevant, precisely scoped, current, and nonresetting?
- Do accept, refuse, defer, revise, withdraw, cancel, and return-later paths preserve comparable understanding, effort, and work?
- Does evaluation measure comprehension, comparison accuracy, consistency, refusal, correction, regret, accessibility, and downstream effects beyond uptake?
- What cumulative pressure emerges when defaults, ranking, framing, friction, reminders, personalization, and social signals are reviewed together?
