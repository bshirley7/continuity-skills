# Learner agency, assessment validity, and credential integrity

## Generalized principles

- Evaluate learning design against meaningful learner agency: clarify purpose and valued outcome, offer multiple ways to engage, perceive, act and express where the construct permits, support goal setting, strategy, reflection and help seeking, preserve choice and refusal within real requirements, and never confuse personalization selected by a system with agency exercised by the learner.
- Treat access as the ability to participate and learn, not account entry: test disability, language, literacy, age, culture, device, bandwidth, time, caregiving and cost across enrollment, content, interaction, assessment, feedback and credential; provide equivalent formats and offline or assisted routes; measure who cannot continue; and never let technology access become an undeclared prerequisite.
- Require a validity argument before consequential assessment: name the intended construct and use, learning standard, population, tasks, scoring, reliability, evidence, limitations and consequences; test whether interface, language, speed, disability, surveillance or technology skill introduces irrelevant variance; use accommodations that preserve the construct; and never infer broad ability from evidence too narrow for the claim.
- Make assessment rules and learner evidence inspectable: disclose format, timing, permitted resources and AI, identity or proctoring, attempts, scoring, threshold, evaluator, feedback, retention and consequence; preserve exact submissions and rubric-linked decisions; support correction, reassessment and appeal; and never use hidden automation or integrity signals as unchallengeable proof.
- Design feedback to improve future performance rather than label the learner: connect evidence to criteria, explain reasoning and next action, distinguish formative from summative judgment, show uncertainty and current state, allow dialogue and revision, protect dignity, and never turn prediction, rank, comparative norm or one error into identity, potential or motivation.
- Keep educators and learners accountable for AI-mediated learning decisions: identify when content, recommendation, feedback, scoring or integrity detection uses AI; show material inputs, limits and responsible human; validate pedagogical fit and bias; require meaningful review for consequential outcomes; preserve learner work and representation; and never automate away professional judgment or authorship.
- Govern learning data as an education record with contextual rights: identify collector, educational purpose, authority, recipients, visibility, retention, correction, export and complaint route; minimize activity, biometric and behavioral data; separate instruction from analytics, research, marketing and model improvement; respect changing parent and learner roles; and never coerce optional surveillance through required learning.
- Evaluate credentials as bounded evidence claims: identify issuer, learner, achievement and learning outcomes, criteria, assessment, evidence, issue date, expiry or renewal, verification and revocation; distinguish attendance, completion and demonstrated competence; make recognition limits understandable; support learner-controlled portability and selective sharing; and never let visual prestige substitute for quality or validity.
- Audit learning systems for outcomes, opportunity and burden beyond engagement: measure access, persistence, meaningful practice, feedback use, assessment validity, correction, mastery, transfer, credential recognition, privacy harm and wellbeing; segment responsibly across relevant populations; include learners who never enroll or leave; examine teacher workload and institutional incentives; and never optimize streaks, minutes, completion or credential volume alone.

## Method

- Trace learner, educator, institution, provider, assessor, issuer and relying party across the complete lifecycle.
- Bind every assessment result and credential claim to exact criteria, evidence, version and intended use.
- Walk disability, low-bandwidth, language, interruption, accommodation and assisted-participation scenarios.
- Test false mastery, scoring error, AI hallucination, integrity false positive and record mismatch.
- Inspect who can see and reuse activity, responses, grades, accommodations and credentials.
- Audit access, learning, burden, correction, recognition, privacy and wellbeing together.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not profile learners beyond a justified educational purpose.
- Do not treat disability accommodation as weaker evidence or unfair advantage.
- Do not use engagement mechanics as proof of learning.
- Do not make opaque AI scoring or cheating detection final.
- Do not disclose grades, accommodations or learning difficulty without authority.
- Do not make required education contingent on optional commercial data use.
- Do not issue credentials whose claim exceeds the evidence.
- Do not optimize completion while hiding exclusion, invalid assessment or distress.

## Variation levers

- Increase validity, identity, security and appeal controls as assessment consequence rises.
- Increase parent, learner and educator role clarity for minors.
- Increase portability and verification for cross-institution or employment credentials.
- Increase offline, assisted and alternative-format support where access is uneven.
- Increase human review and disclosure for AI-generated feedback, scoring or integrity controls.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Personalization versus learner agency and profiling.
- Standardization versus accommodation and contextual fairness.
- Fast automated feedback versus nuance and validity.
- Integrity controls versus privacy and presumption of misconduct.
- Progress visibility versus student confidentiality.
- Credential portability versus linkability and stale evidence.

## Failure modes

- System-selected personalization is presented as learner choice.
- An inaccessible interface changes what an assessment measures.
- A hidden model determines a consequential grade.
- Feedback labels ability instead of supporting repair.
- Integrity detection becomes guilt without review.
- Required learning feeds unrelated analytics or marketing.
- A badge implies competence unsupported by evidence.
- Completion metrics hide who could not participate.

## Anti-patterns

- Algorithmic learner agency.
- Interface-contaminated assessment.
- Black-box grade.
- Ability-label feedback.
- Cheating-detector verdict.
- Education-data land grab.
- Prestige badge inflation.
- Completion-rate learning.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does the experience develop real learner agency rather than system-selected personalization?
- Can people participate and learn across disability, language, device, bandwidth, time and cost constraints?
- Is each consequential assessment valid for its stated construct, population and use?
- Are rules, evidence, scoring, automation, correction and appeal inspectable?
- Does feedback support repair without turning current evidence into identity?
- Are AI roles, limits, validation and responsible human judgment explicit?
- Are education records purpose-bound, minimal, inspectable, correctable and noncommercial by default?
- Do credentials state exact evidence, criteria, issuer, validity and recognition limits?
- Are learning outcomes, opportunity, burden, privacy and wellbeing audited beyond engagement?
