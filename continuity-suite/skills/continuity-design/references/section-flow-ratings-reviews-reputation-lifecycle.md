# Ratings, reviews, and reputation lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Establish eligibility and context before asking for judgment: bind the request to the exact product, version, order, stay, appointment, provider, counterparty or interaction; ask at a time when the experience can reasonably be assessed; explain whether feedback is public, private or operational and how it may affect people; allow skip or later completion; and prevent duplicate, inherited or unrelated reviews from silently attaching to a different object.
- Define every rating scale in task language: label endpoints and important intermediate meanings, distinguish not applicable from neutral or average, separate product, merchant, delivery, provider and platform performance, use category-specific dimensions only when people can judge them, and avoid treating stars, likes, recommendations and satisfaction as interchangeable measures.
- Use progressive composition without steering sentiment: begin with the smallest honest judgment, offer structured reasons and optional prose or media for useful detail, keep positive and negative paths equally operable, disclose identity, display name, audience, edit window and incentive before submission, separate a public review from a private complaint or safety report, and never gate support or resolution on changing or withholding criticism.
- Present aggregates as bounded evidence rather than a trust badge: show the review count and relevant population, distribution, recency, version or transaction context and category breakdown; distinguish verified interaction, incentivized, imported, translated or summarized content; avoid excessive precision and unexplained weighting; and do not let a high average hide a tiny sample, polarized distribution, stale history or materially changed offering.
- Support exploration of the underlying evidence: provide readable reviews, dates and relevant context; offer accessible filtering and sorting by recency, rating, topic, variant or verified experience without making the default deceptively favorable; identify how excerpts or summaries were selected and link them back to source reviews; preserve negative and mixed experiences; and make unavailable, removed or insufficient evidence explicit.
- Keep response, complaint and remedy channels distinct but connected: let the reviewed party respond without editing or suppressing the review, notify the author and permit correction where appropriate, move sensitive account resolution to a private channel without erasing public evidence, prevent responses from exposing personal information or intimidating reviewers, and show whether an issue was acknowledged, resolved, disputed or remains open without rewriting the original account.
- Make moderation symmetric, explainable and appealable: publish eligibility and content rules, check positive and negative submissions with comparable rigor, label pending or removed state, give the author a specific reason and edit or appeal path, let subjects report impersonation, irrelevance, harassment or factual boundary violations without granting a veto over criticism, preserve evidence for review, and distinguish policy enforcement from adjudicating the truth of a disputed experience.
- Govern downstream reputation effects as consequential decisions: document how ratings influence search, badges, prices, eligibility, visibility, deactivation, work or income; use sufficient and comparable evidence, freshness and confidence; detect manipulation and coordinated abuse; limit the effect of isolated or biased ratings; provide notice, explanation, correction and human appeal before serious penalties; and audit outcomes across reviewer and subject groups rather than optimizing only review volume or average score.

## Variation levers

- Increase context, anonymity protection, delayed publication and remedy as reviewer-subject dependence, retaliation risk and livelihood consequence increase.
- Use transaction verification for experience claims while still providing a separate channel for accessibility, safety or pre-transaction concerns.
- Show more distribution, recency and uncertainty as sample size falls, offerings change or ratings drive high-consequence choices.
- Collect private operational detail separately from public review content when disclosure could expose health, financial, safety or personal information.
- Use category-specific dimensions only when they improve prediction and can be judged consistently by the intended reviewer population.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Low-friction prompts increase participation while amplifying momentary emotion and prompt-selection bias.
- Verified transactions improve authenticity while excluding legitimate nonpurchase, safety or accessibility experiences.
- Public identity can improve accountability while increasing retaliation and self-censorship.
- Concise summaries improve scanning while obscuring minority, mixed and recent experiences.
- Provider responses enable recovery while creating a power channel for intimidation or personal-data exposure.
- Permanent history supports trust while making correction, rehabilitation and changed conditions difficult.

## Failure modes

- A prompt asks for a rating without identifying the exact object, version or interaction.
- A star scale has no labels and combines unrelated entities or dimensions.
- Positive feedback is one tap while criticism requires prose, identity or extra screens.
- A public review is silently routed into a private complaint channel.
- An average appears without count, distribution, recency or material context.
- The default sort, excerpts or generated summary systematically hides negative or mixed evidence.
- A provider can suppress criticism or expose reviewer information through a public response.
- A removed review has no reason, correction or appeal path.
- One biased or anomalous rating materially reduces a person's work access without recourse.

## Anti-patterns

- Contextless star request.
- Semantic rating soup.
- Asymmetric positivity funnel.
- Complaint laundering.
- Average as trust badge.
- Curated full picture.
- Response-channel retaliation.
- Opaque review disappearance.
- Reputation cliff.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is the reviewer eligible and the exact object, version, interaction, timing, audience and consequence clear?
- Does each scale have interpretable labels and keep different entities, dimensions and not-applicable states distinct?
- Are positive, negative, public, private, safety and complaint paths equally operable and free of sentiment steering?
- Do aggregates expose count, population, distribution, recency, context, verification, incentives and weighting limits?
- Can readers inspect, filter and trace underlying evidence without defaults or summaries hiding mixed and negative experiences?
- Can subjects respond and resolve issues without suppressing criticism, intimidating reviewers or exposing personal information?
- Are moderation rules symmetric, reasons specific and correction, reporting and appeal available to authors and subjects?
- Are consequential reputation effects explainable, proportionate, bias-audited and subject to correction and human appeal?
