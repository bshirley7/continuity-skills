# Contextual learning

## Generalized principles

- Organize learning around meaningful product outcomes and real objects, not a tour of interface regions; distinguish required setup, recommended improvement, optional education, and work pending on another actor.
- Keep completed, current, remaining, skipped, blocked, and externally completed states visible with direct routes to act, resume, correct, or mark work done without erasing history.
- Teach a capability at the moment it becomes relevant using one bounded concept, a representative example or demonstration, its constraints or access state, and choices to try, learn more, defer, dismiss, or finish.
- Preserve the user's working context and a durable help route; overlays and guides must not replace the product, block unrelated work, or make dismissal equivalent to permanent loss of education.

## Variation levers

- Use a checklist for dependent setup, a contextual panel for one feature, and a learning center for durable self-directed education.
- Show estimated effort only when it reflects the actual user's remaining work.
- Use one concept per step for unfamiliar or consequential capabilities and grouped tasks for expert repeated setup.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Visible progress supports orientation while encouraging completion for its own sake.
- Contextual education increases relevance while interrupting active work.
- Persistent checklists support resumption while consuming valuable workspace.

## Failure modes

- Completion reflects opening a tutorial rather than achieving the product outcome.
- Skipped education cannot be found again.
- A tour points at controls without explaining the user's goal or resulting state.
- Required and optional work share the same visual status.

## Anti-patterns

- Tooltip parade.
- Checklist as engagement score.
- Forced tour before first task.
- Progress without outcome.
- Dismiss means never show help again.
- Learn more that leaves the task without a return path.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does each learning step name a meaningful user outcome?
- Are required, optional, blocked, skipped, and completed states distinct?
- Can users resume, correct, dismiss, and later rediscover guidance?
- Does contextual education preserve the working artifact and unrelated actions?
- Does completion verify a real product state rather than tutorial exposure?
- Are constraints, access, and commercial conditions visible before action?
