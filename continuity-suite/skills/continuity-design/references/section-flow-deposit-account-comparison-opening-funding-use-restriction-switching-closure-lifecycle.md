# Deposit account comparison, opening, funding, use, restriction, switching, and closure lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Preserve one account identity across the lifecycle: identify account type and purpose, legal owner or owners and authority, actual depository institution and visible service provider, currency, ownership category, insurance or protection scope and limits, account agreement version, opening date and current status; bind applications, funding, balances, transactions, fees, interest, restrictions, disputes, changes, switching and closure to it; and never let a brand tile, nickname or masked number obscure who holds the money and under what terms.
- Make comparison about usable value and access, not headline yield: show eligibility, opening deposit, monthly and activity fees, waiver conditions, overdraft behavior, ATM and cash access, transfer limits, branch or support access, rate type and duration, compounding, tiers, bonuses, withdrawal or maturity rules, insurance, required subscription and closure effects; support side-by-side scenarios; and never describe an account as free, high yield or instant while material conditions are visually secondary.
- Separate application, identity verification, eligibility review, approval, agreement, account creation, initial funding, funding settlement, card or access activation and full usability: show owner, next actor, expected timing, missing evidence and available alternatives at every state; preserve a save-and-resume and accessible support route; and never use a celebratory account-opened state when money, credentials or required features remain unavailable.
- Treat ownership and authority as explicit account semantics: distinguish individual, joint, minor or dependent, beneficiary, trustee, beneficial owner, business, employee, delegate and linked external-account roles; explain rights to deposit, withdraw, view, close, dispute and receive records; require proportionate proof and accessible recovery; and never infer authority over an account from device access, relationship, existing customer status or connection permission.
- Represent money availability as an evidence-backed state: distinguish ledger, available, pending, held, restricted, reserved, overdrawn and interest-bearing balances; show each contributing transaction, hold reason category, amount, placed and expected release times, responsible institution and dispute path; update estimates when evidence changes; and never imply that a displayed total can be withdrawn, spent, transferred or insured in full.
- Keep fees, interest and benefit conditions reconstructable over time: show the triggering event, amount or calculation, balance basis, period, rate, tier, compounding, waiver or bonus condition, tax or withholding where relevant, posting date and reversal; link changes to prior and new terms with effective date; provide alerts before avoidable harm; and never let a net balance hide fees, lost interest, expired promotions or conditions the customer could not reasonably monitor.
- Make partner, linked and swept accounts legible as separate systems: identify where credentials, data and money move; distinguish connection from transfer authorization and transfer initiation from settlement; show provider, destination, insurance ownership, limits, delays, revocation and error owner; preserve source and destination receipts; and never aggregate external, investment, cash and deposit balances into one risk-free or immediately available total.
- Treat restrictions as scoped, time-bound and challengeable account states: state which money and functions are affected, distinguish customer freeze, security hold, transaction review, legal restraint, identity issue and provider termination where disclosure is permitted, show requested evidence, reviewer, status, expected update and safe support route, preserve essential access and records where possible, and never use a generic locked label or repeated identity loop as the entire remedy.
- Support errors and unauthorized activity as durable cases linked to exact account events: identify disputed transaction, amount, date, channel, access device and allegation; explain notice window, evidence, provisional credit, investigation, result and appeal or complaint path; reimburse consequential fees when appropriate; retain records after account closure; and never make support contact, card freeze or merchant dispute silently substitute for the applicable account error process.
- Make switching and closure preserve continuity, money and evidence: inventory pending items, recurring deposits and payments, cards, checks, linked services, joint authority, fees, accrued interest, negative or held balances and incoming refunds; show transfer destination, timing, closure or switching date, residual handling, reporting effects and re-entry limits; provide durable statements and confirmation after access ends; and never let closing the interface strand funds, erase disputes or imply every obligation has settled.

## Variation levers

- Increase ownership, authority, approval and role controls for joint, dependent, trust and business accounts.
- Increase provider, custodian, sweep and insurance mapping for embedded or cash-management products.
- Increase cash, branch, accessible-support and alternative-identification routes for financially excluded customers.
- Increase maturity, early-withdrawal and renewal controls for term deposits.
- Increase explanation, continuity and escalation when legal or financial-crime duties limit restriction disclosure.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Fast account opening versus identity assurance and equitable access.
- Simple balances versus holds, settlement and insurance nuance.
- High-yield marketing versus conditional and variable returns.
- Fraud prevention versus timely access to essential funds.
- Partner convenience versus clear institutional responsibility.
- Easy closure versus pending obligations and durable records.

## Failure modes

- A visible brand is mistaken for the deposit holder.
- A headline rate hides tiers, subscriptions or withdrawal conditions.
- Application approval is presented as usable account access.
- Displayed balance is mistaken for available funds.
- Fees or lost interest disappear inside a net balance.
- A restriction gives no scope, timeline or challenge path.
- A dispute is routed to generic support without preserving rights.
- Closure strands money, recurring activity or account evidence.

## Anti-patterns

- Brand-as-bank.
- Headline-yield tunnel.
- Opened-but-unusable.
- Balance-equals-access.
- Net-balance concealment.
- Reasonless lock.
- Support-as-error-process.
- Closure as disappearance.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can people identify the account, owners, actual institution, service provider, protection and governing terms?
- Does comparison expose total cost, usable access, rate conditions, insurance and closure effects?
- Are application, approval, creation, funding, activation and usability represented separately?
- Are ownership, authority and connected-account permissions explicit and proportionate?
- Can people distinguish ledger, available, pending, held, restricted and interest-bearing balances?
- Are fees, interest, bonuses and term changes reconstructable and proactively communicated?
- Are partner, linked, sweep and external-account roles and money movements legible?
- Do restrictions expose scope, evidence, status, timing, support and a challenge path where possible?
- Do disputes preserve applicable rights, investigation state, provisional outcomes and records?
- Does switching or closure protect funds, recurring activity, disputes and durable evidence?
