# Distinctive expression methodology

Use this method when creating or materially reshaping a design, or when producing a visual artifact from an approved direction. It adds a generative bridge between project evidence and the durable design contract. It does not replace accessibility, evidence integrity, prototype integrity, or approval boundaries.

## 1. Derive a subject world

Inspect the subject's real materials, instruments, artifacts, environments, processes, language, and audience conventions. Record only what was inspected or supplied as such. Label plausible but unverified creative interpretations `inferred`.

For each useful cue, record:

- a stable provenance ID;
- `inspected`, `supplied`, or `inferred` classification;
- the observation;
- exact project-relative source references when inspected or supplied;
- the design implication.

Do not mistake a catalog category for the subject world. An industry pack can check fit, but the project itself supplies identity.

## 2. Form the aesthetic proposition

Define:

- an aesthetic thesis explaining the character and why it belongs;
- a signature element that makes the direction recognizable in the artifact;
- one bounded aesthetic risk with its rationale and usability boundary;
- anti-default decisions naming familiar category or model-generated answers that this direction deliberately rejects;
- an expression system for palette, typography, composition, material and imagery, motion, and voice;
- representative target-native compositions;
- uniqueness checks.

Use concrete decisions. Name color roles and values when they are settled. Name type roles and the required character even when a specific available family remains provisional. Describe composition as a spatial rule rather than an adjective. Treat motion as choreography with meaning, not a quantity of effects.

## 3. Spend boldness deliberately

Use one meaningful risk rather than distributing novelty everywhere. The risk may change composition, hierarchy, typography, interaction, imagery, material treatment, or voice. It must advance the subject and remain bounded by comprehension, accessibility, favorable behavior, and recovery.

No risk is a valid outcome only when restraint itself is a project-specific proposition and its precision is specified. A legacy direction that has not made this decision remains visibly provisional.

Allocate an explicit expression budget. Choose exactly one signature dimension from composition, typography, color, motion, imagery, surface and depth, or voice. Supporting dimensions may be present, but keep at least two dimensions quiet. State the field that stays calm, the boundary containing the expressive move, and the order in which treatments should be removed when the artifact becomes noisy.

Restraint is relational, not a universal preference for minimalism. A typographic concept may use a forceful display face, but its readable scale, weight, line height, measure, wrapping behavior, available-font fallback, and breakpoint transformation must be specified. A spatial concept must define repeatable spacing relationships and collision behavior rather than accumulating one-off offsets. Only the signature dimension owns the highest intensity.

## 4. Refine through multiple passes

Run these passes in order. Infer the relevant review perspectives from the project and context; the user does not need to name lenses or design terminology.

1. **Provenance grounding:** connect the concept to inspected, supplied, or honestly inferred subject cues.
2. **Divergent exploration:** compare materially different organizing ideas before converging.
3. **Boundary push:** strengthen the most subject-specific move until it is visible in the concept, not merely described.
4. **Contextual review:** inspect the concept through the perspectives that matter for this audience, modality, workflow, accessibility need, and consequence level. For every material perspective, record why it applies, a concrete finding, the design response, favorable behavior preserved, and an observable verification. A generic claim that hierarchy or usability was reviewed is not evidence of completion.
5. **Restraint edit:** quiet competing dimensions, repair hierarchy and spacing rhythm, and remove treatments that do not reinforce the thesis.
6. **Responsive transformation:** define how the idea changes across target sizes, states, and modalities instead of merely stacking or shrinking.
7. **Artifact critique:** inspect representative rendered output and compare it with the contract.

For every pass, record its status, purpose, changes, preserved strengths, and unresolved issues. Complete the first six before presenting a direction for selection. Leave artifact critique pending until target-native output exists. A completed pass must record an outcome, even when the correct outcome is to preserve a decision.

Contextual perspectives are private routing and review aids, not intake requirements. Infer them from project evidence when they are not explicit. The durable document may name a perspective when that helps implementation understand the finding, but the creative direction must stand on the finding and response rather than catalog terminology. Do not invent a review record merely because a pack was retrieved, and do not mark contextual review complete unless its records would let another person verify the response in a composition or artifact.

## 5. Challenge replaceability

Before building, ask:

- Would the palette, typography, composition, signature, and copy change materially if the subject changed?
- Is the visual metaphor visible in the artifact rather than only explained in prose?
- Did a current category or model-generated default enter without subject evidence?
- Does each structural device encode something true about the content?
- Is the copy native to the audience, or reusable editorial design language?
- Does the narrow composition transform the idea rather than merely stack it?

Revise any interchangeable decision before artifact generation.

## 6. Preserve provenance in `design.md`

Link the distinctive expression to the creative provenance records that support it. Render classifications and sources in the document so implementation can distinguish observed identity from supplied preference and creative inference.

Provenance supports creative judgment; it does not eliminate it. Inferred decisions may be valuable and bold when labeled honestly. They remain candidates for review, not verified statements about users, implementation, or brand history.

## 7. Critique the rendered artifact

Review target-native screenshots before claiming the direction is demonstrated:

- identify whether the signature is visible within five seconds;
- identify where implementation retreated to a default;
- test whether the aesthetic risk still reads as intentional;
- verify the expression system remains coherent across wide and narrow targets;
- remove decoration that does not reinforce the thesis;
- push the signature further when the result is correct but replaceable.
- reduce the loudest treatment one step and verify that identity survives;
- check headline measure, body measure, line height, collisions, edge spacing, and narrow-screen rhythm;
- identify any accidental second signature dimension and quiet it.

Artifact validation proves binding and demonstrated coverage. It does not by itself prove distinctiveness.
