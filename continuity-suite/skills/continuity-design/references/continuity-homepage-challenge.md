# Continuity Homepage Design Challenge

Design a new public-facing homepage for **Continuity**, a control plane for software work shared by humans and agents.

Continuity helps teams carry important context across conversations, product decisions, roadmaps, implementation, validation, and review. An explicit goal can become a bounded plan and working code, while consequential actions remain subject to exact human authority.

The page is a marketing homepage with meaningful product proof. It is not an authenticated dashboard and must not reproduce an existing Continuity interface.

## Central idea

**Work that remembers. Authority that stays human.**

The visitor should leave with one memorable thought:

> This is the first agentic work system I might trust to keep moving without quietly taking control.

## Audience

Design primarily for founder-operators, product and engineering leaders, and small platform teams managing several active software projects. They already use issue trackers, repositories, AI coding tools, and documents, but repeatedly reconstruct context and struggle to distinguish activity from actual progress. They are technically literate and skeptical of vague autonomy claims.

## Questions the homepage must answer

1. What is Continuity?
2. How is it different from an issue tracker, coding agent, or monitoring dashboard?
3. What happens from an explicit goal through implementation and human review?
4. What can Continuity observe, recommend, or execute?
5. Where does human authority remain mandatory?
6. What proof can a visitor inspect before trying it?

## Required narrative ingredients

Determine the order and presentation, but include:

- A strong hero with a clear product thesis and a meaningful product artifact, not a decorative mockup.
- A day-to-night-to-next-morning story showing how work preserves momentum.
- A lifecycle from captured context through planning, building, validation, review, and completion.
- A portfolio-level view that remains intentionally summarized while project evidence stays isolated.
- A clear explanation that `healthy`, `authorized`, `actively building`, `blocked`, `review-ready`, `approved`, and `completed` are different states.
- A trust model showing that information is not recommendation, recommendation is not authorization, authorization is not execution, passed validation is not permission to merge, and review-ready is not completed.
- A human decision surface prioritizing decisions, evidence, and exceptions over raw activity.
- One systems explanation connecting portfolio visibility, project-local controls, agents, and human decisions.
- A specific next step such as “Explore the operating model,” “Try an interactive example,” or “Install locally.”

## Fictional product fixture

This is illustrative demonstration content, not a customer claim.

| Project | State | Evidence | Appropriate next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atlas | Review-ready | 7 of 7 checks passed | Human review |
| Beacon | Healthy, execution off | Doctor passed | No action |
| Current | Blocked | Source evidence is stale | Resolve evidence |
| Harbor | Building | Scope and runtime bound | Inspect progress |
| Ledger | Completed | Merge independently verified | View outcome |

Time-based scenario:

- 4:48 PM: a human defines a bounded goal.
- 5:06 PM: scope, exclusions, and evidence requirements are visible.
- 11:37 PM: implementation passes its configured checks and stops at review-ready.
- 8:12 AM: the owner receives a concise decision surface with evidence and exceptions.

Transform this material into diagrams, timelines, interfaces, editorial storytelling, spatial compositions, or another coherent device.

## Interaction challenge

Include at least one interaction that materially explains the product, such as changing between Today, Overnight, and Morning; inspecting why a project is blocked or review-ready; comparing observed, recommended, and authorized states; following one goal through its evidence trail; or revealing project-local detail behind a sanitized portfolio summary.

The interaction requires keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, and static equivalents.

## Creative freedom

Choose one strong visual thesis and own it. The direction may feel editorial, cinematic, infrastructural, instrument-like, quietly institutional, warm and human, or something unexpected.

Do not assume Continuity must be a dark command center. Do not default to a centered headline followed by interchangeable feature cards. Avoid generic glowing dashboards, decorative automation diagrams, purple gradients, fake terminal text, or motion that does not teach anything. The visual system must emerge from the product idea, not the conventions of “AI software.”

## Truth and safety constraints

- Never imply that a healthy project is authorized to execute.
- Never imply automatic merge, deployment, publication, or business completion.
- Keep raw notes, approvals, identifiers, paths, commands, credentials, and private evidence out of portfolio-level examples.
- Use labels, structure, icons, and explanatory text in addition to color.
- Clearly label stale, unknown, simulated, or fictional information.
- Do not invent customer logos, testimonials, uptime, revenue impact, or performance claims.

## Required outputs

1. A short interpretation of the problem and central design tension.
2. Reference analysis identifying lessons without copying appearance.
3. One to three genuinely different concept directions.
4. Explicit human selection or combination.
5. A resolved private responsive prototype after selection.
6. Desktop at 1440px, tablet at 768px, and mobile at 390px evidence.
7. Interaction, reduced-motion, and accessibility evidence.
8. A compact type, color, spacing, and motion system.
9. Responsive and accessibility notes.
10. A self-critique describing what changed after review and what remains uncertain.

Stop after exact design approval. Design approval never authorizes product implementation.
