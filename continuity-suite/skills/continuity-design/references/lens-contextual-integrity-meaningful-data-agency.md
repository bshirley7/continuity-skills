# Contextual integrity and meaningful data agency

## Generalized principles

- Evaluate every information flow in its actual context: identify the data subject, sender or source, recipient and downstream actors, information type and sensitivity, purpose, expected benefit, authority, collection or inference method, transmission and reuse rules, social or professional norms, power relationship, geography, retention and consequence; treat movement between personal, workplace, health, financial, educational, family and public contexts as a new decision even when one organization controls both.
- Minimize and purpose-bind source and derived data across the full lifecycle: collect, infer, combine, retain, retrieve, disclose and train on only what is necessary and proportionate to a specific declared purpose; separate service delivery, personalization, safety, analytics, support, advertising and model improvement; use privacy-protective defaults, local or disassociated processing and expiry where feasible, and require fresh authority for materially different secondary use.
- Provide meaningful data agency without transferring all privacy labor to individuals: make actors, data, purpose, source, inference, scope, audience, persistence, consequences and defaults understandable at the relevant moment; support access, item-level correction, completion, restriction, objection, withdrawal, deletion, export and portability through usable controls; preserve a protected service path after optional refusal and use organizational safeguards when granular choice cannot make an inappropriate flow acceptable.
- Treat inference, combination and recognition as new data actions: disclose material inferred attributes and confidence, contributing sources, intended decisions and eligible recipients; let people contest, correct, disable or limit the inference and its use; evaluate proxy, group, household, coworker and non-user effects because one person's data or consent cannot authorize profiles, memories or consequences for other people.
- Make retention and deletion operationally complete and verifiable: map primary records, histories, memories, indexes, embeddings, caches, backups, logs, recipient copies, derived profiles, evaluations and trained or adapted data products; show exact requested scope, dependencies, legal or safety exceptions, expected timing and service consequence; propagate correction, restriction and erasure where required, prevent prohibited future use, verify completion, and retain only minimal evidence necessary to prove the action without silently recreating deleted context.

## Method

- Map context, subject, source, sender, recipients, information type, purpose, authority, norms, power, geography, retention and consequence for every flow.
- Separate service delivery, personalization, safety, analytics, support, advertising and model improvement purposes and test necessity, proportionality and compatibility.
- Inventory supplied, observed, inferred, combined, derived, shared and trained-on data across stores, indexes, models, recipients and backups.
- Design contextual notice, protective defaults, access, correction, restriction, objection, withdrawal, deletion, export and portability at usable levels.
- Test third-party, group, household, coworker, child, employee and non-user effects rather than assuming the active user's choice governs everyone.
- Verify propagation, retained exceptions, timing, downstream behavior and non-recreation after correction, restriction or deletion.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not treat a broad privacy notice, continued use, account connection or conversational disclosure as consent for every inference, recipient, purpose or future context.
- Do not infer sensitive traits, vulnerability, emotion, health, finances, relationships or protected characteristics merely because the data is technically available.
- Do not make optional personalization, training, analytics or cross-context reuse a condition of an otherwise viable core service.
- Do not require people to micromanage hundreds of signals to avoid a processing practice that is inappropriate by default.
- Do not let one person authorize collection, memory, training or disclosure about other people represented in shared content.
- Do not retaliate, degrade unrelated service, increase friction or draw adverse inferences when someone refuses, objects, corrects or deletes.
- Do not claim deletion when data remains usable in indexes, derived profiles, recipient copies or future model-improvement pipelines without clear exception and effect.
- Do not retain sensitive context indefinitely because future usefulness is conceivable or deletion is technically inconvenient.

## Variation levers

- Use just-in-time item and purpose controls for optional low-consequence personalization and stronger organizational prohibition for inherently inappropriate flows.
- Use session-only, local, disassociated or expiring context before durable identified memory where continuity needs are limited.
- Increase subject notice, independent review, recipient control, retention limits and deletion verification with sensitivity, inference, power imbalance and shared scope.
- Use summarized privacy state for routine orientation with direct drill-down to exact sources, recipients, purposes, stores and rights.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Persistent memory improves continuity while violating norms when context changes.
- Granular controls improve agency while increasing privacy labor and obscuring systemic responsibility.
- Transparency supports choice while overwhelming people or exposing sensitive third-party information.
- Deletion protects agency while legal holds, security records, backups and shared copies can require bounded exceptions.
- Personalization benefits one person while creating profiles or consequences for households, teams and groups.

## Failure modes

- Data shared for one task silently becomes available in another product, workspace, audience or purpose.
- Service delivery, personalization, safety, analytics, support and model improvement are bundled into one ambiguous choice.
- A privacy control names the feature but not actors, data, inference, purpose, recipients, persistence or consequence.
- The interface offers signal-level controls while retaining an invasive default that no reasonable person can manage.
- An inferred attribute influences behavior but cannot be inspected, disputed, corrected, limited or deleted.
- One person's consent is treated as authority over other people in messages, files, recordings or shared work.
- Deletion removes a visible record but leaves active indexes, profiles, downstream recipients or training use unchanged.
- Refusal or deletion causes unrelated service degradation, repeated pressure or adverse inference.
- Corrected or deleted context is silently recreated from the same source without notice or changed authority.

## Anti-patterns

- Same company means same context.
- One consent for every purpose.
- Control without actors.
- Privacy by micromanagement.
- Inference without rights.
- One person's consent for everyone.
- Delete the visible copy.
- Retaliation for refusal.
- Recreate what was deleted.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are subject, source, sender, recipients, information type, purpose, authority, context norms, power, geography, retention and consequence explicit for each flow?
- Are collection, inference, combination, retention, retrieval, disclosure and training necessary, proportionate and purpose-bound with materially different uses separated?
- Do defaults protect people while access, correction, restriction, objection, withdrawal, deletion, export and portability remain usable without excessive privacy labor?
- Are inferred attributes, contributing sources, confidence, use and recipients inspectable, contestable and correctable?
- Are other people, groups, households, coworkers, children, employees and non-users protected from authority inferred from the active user?
- Does correction or deletion cover records, histories, memories, indexes, embeddings, caches, backups, logs, recipients, profiles and prohibited future use with verification?
