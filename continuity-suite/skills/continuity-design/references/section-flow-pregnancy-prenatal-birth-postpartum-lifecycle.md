# Pregnancy, prenatal care, birth, and postpartum lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Represent pregnancy state as private, sourced and revisable: distinguish possible, self-reported, test-supported and clinically confirmed pregnancy; show dating basis, estimated range, last update and care source; support correction, uncertain dates, multiple gestation and changed clinical estimates; let people control mode, notifications and sharing; and never infer or disclose pregnancy from purchases, cycles, location, search or account relationships without explicit understood purpose and consent.
- Preserve reproductive autonomy at every entry and transition: ask what information or care the person wants without assuming pregnancy intention, continuation, parenting, relationship, gender, family structure or emotional response; provide neutral routes to evidence-based options and qualified care according to context; make defer, change and exit possible; protect confidentiality; and avoid celebratory language, fetal imagery, countdowns or partner sharing as irreversible defaults.
- Make the timeline clinically contextual rather than falsely precise: connect gestational estimate, trimester, maternal changes, development, appointments, tests, decisions and preparation to their source and applicable range; distinguish population information from individualized advice; preserve uncertainty and variation; update downstream content when dating changes; and never imply that comparison objects, exact sizes or milestone dates prove normal development.
- Coordinate prenatal care as linked obligations: show the responsible clinician or team, visit purpose, recommended timing window, location or modality, preparation, records, tests, results, medicines, referrals, coverage and next action; distinguish scheduled, attended, resulted, reviewed and followed up; expose missed or unavailable care with assisted alternatives; and never use content consumption or appointment booking as evidence that prenatal care is complete.
- Make benefit, risk and uncertainty usable for tests, medicines and exposures: identify the exact intervention or product, gestational or lactation context, intended benefit, known evidence, unknowns, alternatives, timing, source and qualified contact; preserve the person's values and decision; avoid simplistic safe or unsafe labels and commercial urgency; support updated guidance and correction; and never recommend starting or stopping treatment solely from generic app content.
- Place maternal warning signs and escalation above engagement mechanics: distinguish common changes, contact-soon, urgent and emergency symptoms during pregnancy and through postpartum; state the symptom, time window, service, channel and fallback; carry pregnancy or recent-pregnancy context across the handoff; believe and preserve the person's concern even when measurements appear normal; confirm connection and response; and never bury urgent care behind a chatbot, article, tracker or fetal-status reassurance.
- Handle pregnancy changes and loss without deletion or forced narrative: support uncertain viability, miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, termination, stillbirth, neonatal loss, adoption and other outcomes with person-chosen language, privacy, care and support; stop celebratory notifications immediately; preserve clinically needed history while allowing suppression of reminders and imagery; explain physical warning signs and follow-up; provide correction and re-entry; and never require the person to repeatedly declare or prove a loss to each feature.
- Continue maternal care after birth or pregnancy end: foreground physical recovery, pain, bleeding, blood pressure or other indicated monitoring, sleep, mental health, medicines, feeding goals and challenges, contraception only through informed choice, chronic-condition handoff, social support and urgent warning signs alongside infant care; identify responsible maternal providers and follow-up through the first year where relevant; and never replace the person's health record with a baby-content funnel.

## Variation levers

- Increase monitoring and specialist coordination for high-risk, multiple and medically complex pregnancies.
- Increase privacy and coercion safeguards where reproductive information may create interpersonal or legal danger.
- Increase loss-sensitive controls after uncertain, ended or adverse outcomes.
- Increase language, transport, accessibility and assisted-care routes for underserved communities.
- Increase postpartum continuity for surgical recovery, hypertension, mental health and chronic conditions.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Personalized timelines aid orientation while overstating biological precision.
- Celebratory engagement supports some people while harming ambivalent, private or bereaved users.
- Partner sharing supports care while enabling coercion or involuntary disclosure.
- Frequent symptom content supports awareness while increasing anxiety and false reassurance.
- Infant-centered content supports caregiving while erasing maternal recovery.
- Risk alerts support safety while reproducing bias or dismissing lived concern.

## Failure modes

- Pregnancy is inferred and exposed without consent.
- An estimated due date becomes an exact developmental truth.
- The app assumes every pregnancy is wanted and joyful.
- A booked visit is counted as completed prenatal care.
- Medicine is labeled simply safe or unsafe.
- Urgent maternal concerns are routed through unmonitored content.
- Loss leaves celebratory reminders active.
- Postpartum mode removes the mother's health from the primary experience.

## Anti-patterns

- Inferred pregnancy mode.
- Countdown certainty.
- Mandatory celebration.
- Booking-as-care.
- Binary medication safety.
- Tracker-before-triage.
- Persistent loss trigger.
- Mother-to-baby displacement.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is pregnancy state sourced, private, uncertain where appropriate and easy to correct or exit?
- Does the experience preserve reproductive autonomy without assumptions about intention or emotion?
- Are timelines contextual and range-based rather than falsely exact?
- Are visits, tests, results, review and follow-up separate accountable states?
- Are test, medicine and exposure benefits, risks and unknowns qualified and decision-supportive?
- Can maternal concerns reach urgent qualified care without engagement barriers?
- Are changed outcomes and loss handled privately, sensitively and completely across the product?
- Does postpartum care continue to foreground the person's health and long-term continuity?
