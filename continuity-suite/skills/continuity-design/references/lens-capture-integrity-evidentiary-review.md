# Capture integrity and evidentiary review

## Generalized principles

- Treat acquisition, transformation, recognition and decision as separate accountable stages: preserve source identity and capture conditions; record crop, enhancement, compression, redaction and extraction; bind every recognized value to its source region and version; distinguish image quality, OCR confidence, document authenticity, person-to-evidence binding, eligibility and final action; and expose the evidence needed to review each claim.
- Design for successful capture without making physical ability or equipment an eligibility gate: provide contextual permission, multimodal framing and quality cues, controllable timing, page and side progress, local review and precise recapture; support file, manual, assisted, in-person or trusted-referee routes according to risk; and test with low vision, blindness, tremor, limited reach, cognitive disability, older devices, poor lighting and constrained connectivity.
- Preserve evidence while making automation correctable: retain an appropriate unaltered source, make transforms reversible, present extracted fields in context, flag uncertainty and cross-field mismatch, prevent automatic completion of consequential values, allow correction without rewriting the source, keep both source and correction lineage, and escalate repeated or ambiguous failure to trained human review.
- Evaluate authenticity and biometric systems by operational harm, not a success animation: measure false acceptance and rejection across supported documents, attacks, devices, environments and demographic groups; validate liveness and manipulation detection without claiming infallibility; separate identity proofing from authentication and eligibility; provide notice, reasons, contestability and a proportionate alternate route; and monitor exclusion and repeated-capture burden.
- Minimize and secure the full visual data exhaust: bound field of view, avoid unrelated people and documents, strip unnecessary location and device metadata, isolate temporary files and previews, validate type and content rather than trusting names, scan uploads safely, restrict recipients and public retrieval, define retention per source, derivative and biometric template, and support access, export, correction and deletion consistent with required audit lineage.
- Require consequence-sensitive review and completion evidence: before financial, legal, health, identity, safety or public action, present exact source pages, extracted values, destination, recipient and commitment; distinguish captured, saved, uploaded, received, validated, under review, accepted and rejected; preserve receipts and correction routes; and ensure a successful technical transfer never masquerades as substantive approval.

## Method

- Map the source object, capture environment, every image transform, extracted value, validation claim, reviewer and downstream decision.
- Identify access needs, device and environmental constraints, fraud threats, privacy exposure, consequence and required alternate routes.
- Design contextual permission, multimodal quality feedback, reversible processing, source-linked review, precise recapture and secure submission.
- Set claim-specific thresholds and human escalation for OCR, document authenticity, liveness, biometric comparison and eligibility.
- Test genuine and manipulated evidence across affected users and operational conditions, then measure exclusion, error, correction, contestability and final outcome.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not make camera access, a modern phone, steady hands, good vision, private space or strong connectivity an unstated prerequisite for service.
- Do not present OCR, barcode recognition, liveness or facial similarity as proof beyond the claim it actually supports.
- Do not silently enhance or crop away faint marks, handwriting, context or security features from the only retained source.
- Do not infer identity, eligibility, honesty, emotion, health or risk from an image without a justified, validated and contestable basis.
- Do not retain unrelated faces, surroundings, location metadata, temporary images or biometric templates by default.
- Do not let repeated automated rejection become an unappealable denial or force endless recapture.
- Do not expose identity, health, financial or legal images through public retrieval, shared previews or weak file validation.
- Do not use a successful upload or clean image as a deceptive approval signal.
- Do not delete required correction or decision lineage when removing a derivative, while also not retaining raw evidence without a defined need.

## Variation levers

- Increase unaltered-source retention, exact field review, trained validation and audit lineage with consequence and authenticity need.
- Use automatic capture when it reduces burden and manual capture when timing, accessibility, evidentiary context or user control dominates.
- Prefer local processing for low-risk extraction and require explicit disclosure and stronger governance when sensitive images or templates leave the device.
- Set false-acceptance, false-rejection and human-review thresholds according to harm, population and available alternate routes rather than one global confidence score.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Aggressive quality gating improves machine recognition while excluding people and devices that cannot satisfy ideal capture conditions.
- Image enhancement improves legibility while altering evidence and concealing uncertainty.
- Keeping originals supports audit and correction while increasing privacy, security and retention risk.
- Automated identity checks accelerate access while scaling demographic error and opaque denial.
- Rich capture guidance reduces failure while increasing cognitive load and obscuring the actual commitment.

## Failure modes

- The first captured image becomes the only retained evidence after irreversible processing.
- OCR output appears as ordinary typed data with no source region or uncertainty.
- A biometric or document threshold rejects access with no reason, appeal or alternate proofing.
- Quality instructions depend only on color, animation or visual alignment.
- The interface repeatedly asks for a better image without identifying the correctable cause.
- A file name or content-type header is treated as proof that an upload is safe.
- A successful transfer animation implies validation or approval.
- Deletion controls do not distinguish local source, uploaded evidence, extracted data and biometric derivative.
- Testing uses ideal documents, lighting and devices but not affected populations or attack artifacts.

## Anti-patterns

- Derivative-only evidence.
- Source-free OCR.
- Threshold denial.
- Visual-only capture guidance.
- Mystery recapture.
- Trust the upload header.
- Transfer equals approval.
- One delete for four records.
- Lab-only validation.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can reviewers distinguish source capture, transforms, recognition, authenticity, identity binding, eligibility and final decision with source-linked evidence?
- Can affected users complete capture or an equivalent route across relevant disabilities, devices, environments and connectivity without exclusion?
- Are originals, transforms, extracted fields, uncertainty, corrections and human escalation preserved and reviewable at the correct grain?
- Were false acceptance, false rejection, manipulation detection and biometric performance tested by group and context with reasons, contestability and alternate routes?
- Are field of view, metadata, temporary files, upload validation, recipients, retrieval, retention, derivatives, export and deletion governed safely?
- Before consequential action, are exact evidence, values, destination and commitment reviewable, with transfer and substantive approval clearly separated?
