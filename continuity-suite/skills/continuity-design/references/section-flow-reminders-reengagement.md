# Reminders and re-engagement

## Generalized principles

- Give notification control at four distinct levels: global pause or disable, event or purpose, delivery channel, and timing or frequency; show which rule takes precedence and confirm the applied state.
- Connect each reminder to a user-recognizable goal, task, threshold, or changed state, and expose skip, dismiss, snooze, pause, reschedule, or disable in proportion to the interruption.
- Keep target, activity, progress, elapsed time, remaining work, streak, reward, and outcome as separate states so attention metrics do not impersonate meaningful achievement.
- Treat social nudges and re-engagement prompts as accountable messages: identify the initiating person or rule, affected goal, delivery route, time sensitivity, and a recipient-controlled response.

## Variation levers

- Use global presets for rapid control and detailed rules for users with differentiated needs.
- Offer fixed reminders for predictable routines and user-configured timing for personal goals.
- Use progress summaries for reflection and event alerts for conditions requiring action.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Granular control improves fit while increasing configuration burden.
- Streaks create continuity while converting missed days into perceived loss.
- Smart notification modes reduce volume while making routing logic less inspectable.

## Failure modes

- Turning off one channel silently leaves another active.
- A pause control has no visible duration or restart state.
- Progress celebrates interaction volume without showing the underlying outcome.
- A reminder request appears before the user has established a relevant goal.

## Anti-patterns

- All-or-nothing notification consent.
- Streak anxiety as retention strategy.
- Unlabeled smart routing.
- Nudge without sender or purpose.
- Snooze without schedule control.
- Reward without meaningful progress.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can users disable or pause all notifications and understand duration?
- Can they control event, channel, timing, and frequency independently?
- Does every reminder identify its goal or triggering state?
- Are progress and outcome distinguished from streak and engagement?
- Are social nudges attributable and recipient-controlled?
- Does saved feedback verify the effective preference?
