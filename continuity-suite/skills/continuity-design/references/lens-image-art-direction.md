# Image art direction

## Generalized principles

- Give every image a declared job before choosing a style: identify the audience, context, message, emotional register, evidence role, expected takeaway, relationship to adjacent words, required authenticity, and whether photography, illustration, diagram, or no image communicates it most truthfully and efficiently.
- Translate brand and message into an art-direction grammar rather than a mood label: specify subject behavior, environment, viewpoint, distance, focal hierarchy, geometry, negative space, light, color, contrast, depth, texture, finish, realism, imperfection, and prohibited treatments with reference examples and reasons.
- Choose subjects and moments for contextual truth: prefer real, specific, plausible activity and environments over generic symbolism; represent people with agency, dignity, diversity, and relevant context; obtain informed permission when required, avoid tokenism and stereotypes, and disclose staged, composite, generated, or materially altered imagery when that distinction matters.
- Compose around one deliberate focal path: control subject scale, viewpoint, horizon, visual weight, foreground and background separation, directional energy, and negative space; crop to strengthen meaning without removing consequential context, and define safe focal and text zones for every required aspect ratio rather than centering by default.
- Use light, color, depth, texture, and post-processing as a coherent material system: define naturalism, contrast range, temperature, saturation, shadow character, grain, sharpness, overlays, cutouts, illustration media, and compositing boundaries; apply treatments consistently enough to form a family while preserving subject truth, skin tone, evidence, and legibility.
- Integrate typography and graphics without sacrificing either image or language: reserve intentional quiet regions, test all crops and responsive placements, use real text when possible, maintain contrast against the full underlying image, avoid covering faces or evidence, and provide a non-image path for essential words, data, or instructions.
- Design the accessible equivalent from the image's purpose and context: use empty alternatives for truly decorative assets, concise contextual descriptions for informative images, function-oriented alternatives for controls, and visible structured descriptions or source data for complex visuals; do not repeat nearby text or mistake file identification for meaning.
- Preserve rights, consent, origin, and edit history as part of the asset: record creator, owner, license, permitted uses, releases, subjects, location sensitivity, source, capture or generation method, material edits, accessibility descriptions, expiration, and required credit in durable metadata and the asset system; treat provenance signals as evidence about history, not proof that a depicted claim is true.
- Build and test an image family, not one ideal frame: specify master quality, color space, formats, compression, aspect-ratio derivatives, focal metadata, safe zones, small-size simplification, dark and light contexts, print behavior, localization, loading behavior, and fallback; review the set for repetition, representational balance, accessibility, performance, and cumulative tone.

## Method

- Write the image brief: audience, context, message, task, emotional register, evidence role, authenticity need, channels, formats, and exclusions.
- Select photography, illustration, diagram, collage, or no image based on the communication job.
- Define the art-direction grammar for subject, environment, viewpoint, framing, light, color, depth, texture, treatment, and prohibited conventions.
- Plan casting, representation, access, consent, rights, locations, props, and disclosure before capture or generation.
- Create composition variants for required aspect ratios with focal and text safe zones.
- Produce an image family across dense, sparse, human, object, environmental, informational, and sensitive contexts.
- Write contextual alternatives and complex descriptions; preserve creator, rights, releases, origin, edits, and accessibility metadata.
- Test crops, overlays, contrast, small sizes, responsive delivery, print, loading, localization, assistive access, cumulative tone, and representational balance.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not stage, crop, retouch, composite, or generate an image in a way that materially falsifies evidence or context.
- Do not use people as decorative proof of diversity, vulnerability, success, or endorsement.
- Do not collect or publish a person's image, identity, location, health, work, or private context without appropriate permission and risk review.
- Do not infer that provenance metadata proves the truth of what an image depicts.
- Do not use stereotyped symbolism, disaster imagery, humiliation, fear, or idealized bodies to manufacture persuasion.
- Do not strip creator credit, rights, accessibility descriptions, consent limits, provenance, or required disclosures during transformation and delivery.

## Variation levers

- Use documentary naturalism when trust depends on real people, places, processes, or outcomes.
- Use controlled studio composition when product form, comparison, craft, or modular adaptation is primary.
- Use illustration when simplification, abstraction, privacy, impossible viewpoints, or conceptual explanation is necessary.
- Increase negative space and compositional restraint when typography or multi-format cropping must coexist with the image.
- Increase texture, unusual viewpoint, color intervention, or mixed media for expressive differentiation only when the message and context support it.
- Reduce visual detail and tonal range as display size shrinks or the image becomes secondary.
- Increase provenance, disclosure, and nonvisual explanation as evidentiary consequence or synthetic alteration increases.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- A distinctive treatment builds recognition while making subjects feel staged or reducing contextual truth.
- Tight cropping strengthens focus while removing place, relationships, or evidence.
- Negative space supports copy while producing formulaic compositions.
- Representation breadth improves inclusion while a checklist approach creates tokenism.
- Consistent post-processing unifies a family while distorting skin tone, material, data, or environment.
- High-resolution masters preserve quality while increasing storage, transfer, and delivery cost.
- Provenance improves traceability while metadata may expose private people or locations.

## Failure modes

- An image is selected because the layout has a slot rather than because it has a communication job.
- Art direction is described only with subjective adjectives and no controllable visual variables.
- Generic stock symbolism substitutes for a real subject, process, place, or outcome.
- One composition is mechanically center-cropped into every format.
- Filters and overlays create consistency by erasing material, environmental, or human truth.
- Text is placed over uncontrolled detail or embedded as inaccessible pixels.
- Alternative text names objects without conveying the image's contextual purpose.
- Rights, consent, origin, edits, and descriptions are kept in a spreadsheet detached from derivatives.
- A hero image looks strong alone while the full family becomes repetitive, exclusive, slow, or tonally incoherent.

## Anti-patterns

- Image slot decoration.
- Mood-board adjectives only.
- Stock metaphor as evidence.
- Center crop everywhere.
- Truth-erasing treatment.
- Typography over visual noise.
- Inventory-style alternative text.
- Metadata detached from assets.
- One-shot art direction.

## Acceptance and review questions

- What exact communication, evidence, emotional, or identity job justifies each image and its chosen medium?
- Is the art-direction grammar controllable across subject, environment, viewpoint, composition, light, color, depth, texture, and treatment?
- Are subjects, moments, representation, consent, staging, generation, and alteration contextually truthful and dignified?
- Does each aspect ratio preserve the intended focal path, consequential context, and safe regions?
- Do color, light, depth, texture, and post-processing form a coherent family without distorting the subject?
- Can typography and graphics remain legible and semantically available across every crop and context?
- Does the accessible equivalent communicate the image's contextual purpose and full complex information where needed?
- Are creator, rights, releases, origin, edits, disclosure, description, and expiration preserved through every derivative?
- Has the complete family been tested for quality, repetition, representation, accessibility, responsive behavior, print, performance, and cumulative tone?
