# Creative direction methodology

Use this method to turn uneven project context into a durable design contract. Exercise creative judgment; do not expose catalog taxonomy or treat the method as a questionnaire.

## 1. Inspect the existing design

Inspect the relevant product documentation, application structure, representative surfaces, shared components, tokens, content, responsive behavior, and existing design records. For document and image targets, inspect the source template, delivery formats, adjacent content, and artifact lifecycle instead of inventing application behavior.

Record:

- evidence actually inspected;
- strengths and favorable behavior to preserve;
- aesthetic, structural, interaction, accessibility, and consistency gaps;
- design debt that affects coherent implementation;
- unknowns and evidence limitations.

Do not call an uninspected area inconsistent or broken. Separate observation from interpretation.

## 2. Reconstruct the problem

Derive a provisional title, intent, audiences, target modalities, constraints, consequences, and non-goals. Use working assumptions to provide immediate value when context is incomplete. Ask at most the concise questions that would materially improve the next revision; do not block the first useful response on formal intake.

Infer useful category references privately from project evidence. Never require the user to name an industry, theme, message structure, lens, or design system. Preserve inferred provenance in private machine state.

## 3. Establish creative directions

Choose the direction count from material ambiguity:

- one when the organizing idea, character, and principal tradeoffs are settled;
- two when one consequential alternative remains;
- three when several coherent approaches would change structure, behavior, or communication.

Each direction must be project-specific and genuinely different in its organizing idea, experience architecture, expression, and tradeoffs. Differences in palette, typeface, or decoration alone do not constitute separate directions.

Define for each direction:

- a concise thesis and principles;
- experience architecture and important states;
- visual and interaction grammar appropriate to the target modality;
- component and pattern direction;
- implementation guidance and bounded variation;
- observable acceptance and drift checks;
- favorable behavior that survives;
- prohibited patterns and explicit tradeoffs.

Use generated fallback directions only to recover a malformed draft. Do not present them as completed creative work.

## 4. Produce the durable contract

The selected or explicitly combined direction becomes `design.md`. Keep it useful to planning, implementation, review, documents, and visual production without turning it into a task plan.

The document must make current-state evidence, design thesis, experience rules, visual system, target-specific decisions, preservation, non-goals, implementation constraints, and validation criteria easy to find. Omit irrelevant modality dimensions rather than fabricating requirements.

## 5. Maintain alignment

- Planning binds the exact approved design ID, revision, and hash and maps affected design sections into acceptance criteria.
- Execution treats preservation, prohibited patterns, and implementation guidance as constraints. New design decisions return to design revision rather than entering silently.
- Testing and review evaluate the artifact against design acceptance and drift checks in addition to functional requirements.
- Reporting identifies design version, implemented surfaces, deviations, and unresolved design debt.

Approval publishes only the exact design document. It never authorizes implementation, external publication, spending, or a goal change.
