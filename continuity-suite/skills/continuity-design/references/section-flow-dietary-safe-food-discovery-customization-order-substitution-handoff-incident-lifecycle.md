# Dietary-safe food discovery, customization, ordering, substitution, handoff, and incident lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Preserve one versioned food identity from discovery through consumption evidence: identify brand or dish, exact variant and size, responsible manufacturer or kitchen and location, seller, recipe or formulation version, ingredient source and date, preparation context, package or menu identifier and last verification; bind dietary labels, customization, substitution, order, handoff, complaint and recall to it; and never let a representative image, familiar name or prior purchase substitute for the current label and preparation record.
- Separate preference, dislike, ethical or religious restriction, intolerance, allergy, celiac requirement, clinical diet and nutritional goal: let people state scope, recipient, severity or consequence where useful without diagnosing them; explain which choices drive discovery only, which require ingredient exclusion, which depend on certification and which need human confirmation or cannot be accommodated; keep absence, unknown and not assessed distinct; and never collapse every constraint into a lifestyle tag or imply medical safety from a taste preference.
- Treat filters and recommendations as bounded retrieval evidence: define each dietary or nutrition label, data source, covered item fields, merchant or platform responsibility, update time, unknown-data behavior and whether customization can invalidate it; show active constraints and why an item matched; exclude or clearly separate unassessed items for safety-sensitive searches; preserve sponsored status; and never call a filtered result safe, suitable or personalized when evidence is incomplete or only category-level.
- Make food composition inspectable at the choice point: show complete ingredients where available, regulated allergen sources, precautionary cross-contact statements, dietary and certification claims with scope, nutrition values with serving and package basis, quantity, preparation and responsible source; distinguish intentional ingredients from possible presence and standardized claims from informal descriptors; provide accessible text beyond icons and color; and never use calories, a health halo, a free-from badge or a short contains list as a substitute for the complete relevant evidence.
- Recompute the exact item after every customization: preserve base recipe, included, removed, substituted and added ingredients, portion and preparation changes; update price, nutrition and known allergen presence; explain what removal cannot control, including derivatives, sauces, shared equipment, oil and cross-contact; require review of final composition and unresolved questions; and never represent removing a visible ingredient or adding a note as an allergen-free preparation guarantee.
- Connect written dietary evidence to a responsible communication path: identify which business can answer ingredient and preparation questions, make the exact item and recipient visible to trained staff, preserve the question and response with time and actor, support language and accessibility needs, state when the business cannot safely accommodate, and carry confirmed requirements into production and handoff; never route a safety question to generic marketplace support or imply that free-text instructions were read and accepted without acknowledgment.
- Treat substitution as a new food decision under inherited constraints: preserve original item and reason unavailable, exact proposed replacement, package identity, quantity, price, ingredients, allergens, precautionary statements, claims and nutrition differences; automatically reject known constraint conflicts, keep unknowns visible, require explicit approval for safety-relevant changes and make refund easy; verify the physical item at selection or handoff; and never infer dietary equivalence from category, brand, appearance, popularity or price.
- Make final review and handoff recipient-specific: group each customized or substituted item with recipient, exact composition, dietary instruction, unresolved caveat, quantity, preparer or picker acknowledgment and packaging identifier; distinguish multi-person meals; prevent unsafe consolidation; provide written allergen information again where applicable; let the recipient inspect package labels before acceptance and report a mismatch; and never let a generic order success state replace item-level safety confirmation.
- Support incident response without pretending the interface provides medical diagnosis: let people report wrong item, undisclosed or unexpected ingredient, label mismatch, cross-contact concern or adverse reaction; preserve food, package, receipt, customization, substitution, preparation, batch and communication evidence; clearly separate emergency action from marketplace support, avoid delaying urgent care, notify responsible parties, correct catalog data and connect affected orders or recalls; and never reduce a possible safety event to a refund-only ticket.
- Keep dietary profiles private, revisable and subordinate to current evidence: collect only what improves the stated task, support multiple named or anonymous recipients, allow temporary and order-specific constraints, show where data is used and shared, prevent health inference and marketing exploitation, let people update or remove it, and distinguish recommendations from clinical advice; use neutral language and preserve food access and cultural context; and never moralize choices, rank bodies or optimize engagement by escalating restrictive eating.

## Variation levers

- Increase package, batch, supplier and label-version controls for manufactured grocery products.
- Increase kitchen, recipe, staff acknowledgment and cross-contact controls for prepared food.
- Increase item-level approval and physical-package verification for shopper-mediated substitutions.
- Increase certification scope and responsible-source evidence for religious, ethical and regulated free-from claims.
- Increase recipient separation, language support and emergency evidence for children, dependent care and severe reactions.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Fast discovery versus complete ingredient and uncertainty evidence.
- Persistent personalization versus dietary and health-data privacy.
- Flexible customization versus preparation consistency and cross-contact control.
- Substitution convenience versus exact constraint-preserving consent.
- Simple icons versus accessible, jurisdiction-specific food semantics.
- Broad precautionary warnings versus useful choice and false reassurance.

## Failure modes

- A preference filter appears to guarantee allergen safety.
- Unknown ingredient data is treated as absence.
- Removing an ingredient appears to remove cross-contact risk.
- Nutrition is shown without serving or customized-item basis.
- Free-text allergy notes have no acknowledgment or responsible recipient.
- A replacement is approved based on category and price alone.
- Multi-person items lose recipient-specific dietary identity at handoff.
- A suspected adverse reaction becomes only a refund workflow.

## Anti-patterns

- Filter-as-safety-certificate.
- Unknown-means-free-from.
- Removal-equals-safe.
- Servingless nutrition.
- Unacknowledged allergy note.
- Category-equivalent substitution.
- Dietary identity collapse.
- Refund-only food incident.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is the exact food, variant, source, formulation, preparation location and evidence date preserved?
- Are preference, ethical restriction, intolerance, allergy, celiac, clinical diet and nutrition goal semantically distinct?
- Do filters expose definitions, data coverage, unknowns, match reasons and limits rather than imply safety?
- Can people inspect ingredients, allergen and cross-contact evidence, claims and serving-based nutrition accessibly?
- Does customization recompute the exact item while stating what ingredient removal cannot control?
- Can a trained responsible business acknowledge questions and requirements for the exact item and recipient?
- Do substitutions inherit constraints, expose differences and require appropriate explicit approval?
- Does review and handoff preserve recipient-specific food identity and item-level confirmation?
- Can people report and preserve evidence for a food-safety incident without delaying urgent care?
- Are dietary profiles private, revisable, multi-person capable and free from moralizing or clinical overreach?
