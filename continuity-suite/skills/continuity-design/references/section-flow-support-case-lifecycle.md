# Support case lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Create a durable case from the person's actual task context: preserve the affected account, order, resource, transaction, or workflow; capture what happened, impact and urgency, timing, attempted fixes, desired outcome, attachments, safe diagnostics, language or accessibility needs, and contact preference without demanding information the service already holds.
- Distinguish help discovery, automated guidance, conversation, submitted request, accepted case, and assigned case; after submission provide a stable case reference, receipt, captured summary, safe correction route, current status, next step, responsible team or owner, response expectation and its basis, and notification controls.
- Preserve one case history across self-service, automation, chat, email, phone, specialist transfer, escalation, and accessibility support: carry forward the narrative, attachments, authenticated context, consent and verification state, prior advice, promised actions, ownership, timestamps, and outstanding questions so the person does not have to repeat or reconstruct the case.
- Use lifecycle states that describe who owes the next action and what evidence exists—received, awaiting information, queued, assigned, investigating, workaround offered, action scheduled, resolved pending verification, closed, and reopened—while exposing material delays, transfers, merged cases, deadlines, and escalation or complaint routes.
- Treat resolution as a verifiable outcome rather than an agent declaration: state the finding and reasons, action or remedy, affected scope, owner, timing, dependencies, evidence of completion, remaining limitations, and how the person can confirm, correct, reject, reopen, appeal, or escalate with the full history retained; request feedback only after the outcome is clear and never as a condition of remedy.

## Variation levers

- Use guided categorization for common issues and free description plus evidence for ambiguous, novel, or high-impact problems.
- Offer asynchronous messaging by default for durable evidence and add live chat, voice, video, or in-person support when urgency, complexity, disability, language, or emotional load warrants it.
- Increase identity verification, authorization, audit, review independence, and written reasoning with financial, health, safety, employment, legal, or account-control consequence.
- Use simple received, in progress, and resolved states for low-complexity support, and richer next-actor states when transfers, dependencies, service levels, or external parties affect progress.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Structured intake improves routing while forcing a novel problem into the wrong category.
- Automation accelerates common recovery while becoming a barrier to a human or formal complaint.
- Detailed diagnostics speed investigation while increasing privacy, security, and cognitive burden.
- Closure targets improve queue hygiene while encouraging premature resolution and hidden reopen demand.
- Channel choice supports access while fragmented systems cause repeated stories and contradictory commitments.

## Failure modes

- Opening a chatbot or sending a message is presented as a submitted and accepted case.
- The person receives no durable reference, captured summary, owner, next step, or response expectation.
- A transfer or channel change discards narrative, evidence, consent, verification, or promised actions.
- Status is only open or closed and does not reveal who owes the next action or why progress is delayed.
- The case is marked resolved when a response was sent, a bot article was shown, or the user became silent.
- Reopening, escalation, complaint, or appeal starts a new case and loses the original evidence and decision history.
- Sensitive diagnostics, credentials, identity evidence, or third-party information are requested without necessity, safe handling, or retention explanation.

## Anti-patterns

- Contact us, then silence.
- Chatbot as locked gate.
- Repeat your story.
- Transfer without custody.
- Open or closed only.
- Reply sent equals resolved.
- Survey before remedy.
- Appeal starts over.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does intake preserve affected context, occurrence, impact, timing, attempts, desired outcome, evidence, safe diagnostics, and access needs without redundant collection?
- Are help, automation, conversation, submission, acceptance, assignment, and resolution represented as distinct states?
- After submission, can the person inspect reference, receipt, summary, status, owner, next step, expectation, and notifications?
- Do channel changes and transfers preserve narrative, evidence, identity and consent state, advice, promises, ownership, timestamps, and open questions?
- Do statuses identify the next actor, evidence, delays, transfers, deadlines, and escalation path?
- Does resolution expose reasons, remedy, scope, owner, timing, proof, limitations, verification, correction, reopening, appeal, and retained history?
