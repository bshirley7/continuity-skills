# Essential utility service enrollment, usage, billing, outage, restriction, restoration, switching, and closure lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Preserve one service identity across the lifecycle: identify premises or service address, account holder and authorized people, retailer, network or carrier and intermediaries, service and meter, line, SIM or device, plan or tariff version, contract and jurisdiction; bind availability, order, installation, activation, usage, bills, payments, outages, cases, restrictions, restoration, switching and closure to it; and never let a provider tile or account number obscure who supplies, operates and supports the service.
- Compare the total service proposition rather than a headline rate: show address eligibility and coverage, capacity or speed, usage allowance, recurring and usage charges, standing charges, taxes and fees, equipment and installation, minimum term, price changes, support, accessibility, interruption commitments, switching and closure costs; support realistic usage scenarios; and never describe service as available, unlimited, fixed-price or free while material qualifications are secondary.
- Separate eligibility and address verification, order acceptance, equipment delivery, installation, device configuration, network registration, activation and usable service: show the responsible party, next action, dependency and expected time at every state; provide accessible alternatives and recovery; and never use payment, shipment, installation or a celebratory connected message as proof that the essential service works at the required premises or device.
- Make usage and metering source-linked: identify service, premises, meter or device, serial where relevant, unit, interval, time zone, actual, smart, customer-read or estimated provenance, collection and posting times, gaps and corrections; preserve raw readings and tariff context behind charts; and never turn an estimate, aggregate, device diagnostic or delayed feed into authoritative consumption without qualification.
- Keep every bill reconstructable: show prior balance, payments and credits, service period, readings or usage, rates, tiers or time bands, standing and equipment charges, taxes, fees, adjustments, estimated versus actual amounts, issue and due dates and resulting balance; link every component to governing terms and corrections; and never let autopay, a monthly amount or net balance replace the issued bill and its evidence.
- Separate billed amount, amount due, payment authorization, processing, settlement, account credit and service effect: identify payment recipient and intermediary, allocation, timing, failure, reversal and receipt; preserve manual, automatic, prepaid and assistance routes without duplicate collection; and never imply that a successful wallet or bank payment has reached the provider, credited the account or prevented restriction until confirmed.
- Represent outages and degradation as evolving evidence: distinguish customer report, device finding, operator detection and third-party report; show affected service and scope, start, known or unknown cause, safety implications, work owner, restoration estimate with confidence, update time, performance indicators and support case; verify restored service and compensation eligibility; and never use a static offline label or optimistic estimate as finality.
- Treat affordability support, prepayment, limits and restrictions as distinct and challengeable states: explain amount and cause, notice history, payment-plan and assistance options, critical-needs or accessibility protections, exact affected services, effective time, evidence and complaint route; use disconnection only as a bounded last-resort state where permitted; show the precise restoration conditions; and never shame, surprise, or repeatedly re-prove a person seeking continuity.
- Make responsibility legible across premises equipment, application, payment intermediary, retailer, carrier, network operator and third parties: route support by diagnosed layer, preserve a durable case with evidence and ownership, hand off without forcing repetition, state what each party can remedy and escalate unresolved disagreement; and never make the customer arbitrate invisible provider boundaries while service remains unavailable.
- Make moving, switching and closure preserve continuity and evidence: coordinate old and new premises or providers, effective and overlap dates, final readings, number or account portability, equipment return, pending payments and disputes, final bill, residual credit, refund, compensation and record access; provide durable confirmation after login ends; and never let a closed interface erase service history, strand credit, interrupt critical connectivity or imply every obligation has settled.

## Variation levers

- Increase safety, critical-needs and outage escalation for energy, water, landline and medically consequential connectivity.
- Increase meter provenance, tariff reconstruction and estimate correction for measured utilities.
- Increase equipment, installation, coverage and performance controls for broadband and mobile service.
- Increase intermediary role, allocation and settlement evidence for embedded bill-payment experiences.
- Increase assisted, non-digital, language and accessible channels where service continuity cannot depend on one device or account.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Fast enrollment versus address, eligibility and installation certainty.
- Simple pricing versus usage, tariff, equipment and tax complexity.
- Readable analytics versus meter and estimate provenance.
- Optimistic restoration estimates versus uncertain field work.
- Collection efficiency versus affordability and essential-service continuity.
- Easy switching versus uninterrupted service, final bills and equipment obligations.

## Failure modes

- Payment is mistaken for service activation or account credit.
- A headline rate hides total recurring and installation cost.
- An estimated reading is presented as authoritative usage.
- A monthly payment replaces bill reconstruction.
- Online status hides degradation or affected scope.
- An outage estimate is presented without confidence or update time.
- Restriction appears without assistance, protections or remedy.
- Switching or closure strands credit, equipment or records.

## Anti-patterns

- Paid-means-live.
- Headline-rate tunnel.
- Estimate-as-meter.
- Autopay-as-bill.
- Green-dot reliability.
- Static restoration promise.
- Disconnection by surprise.
- Closure as disappearance.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can people identify the premises, account, provider, operator, intermediary, service, plan and governing terms?
- Does comparison expose eligibility, capacity, total cost, equipment, changes, support and exit effects?
- Are order, installation, configuration, activation and usable service represented separately?
- Can every usage value be traced to a meter or device, unit, time, provenance and correction state?
- Can every bill be reconstructed from service periods, readings, rates, charges, payments and adjustments?
- Are payment authorization, settlement, account credit and service effect distinct?
- Do outages expose scope, evidence source, cause, estimate confidence, updates, support and verified restoration?
- Do hardship and restriction states expose assistance, protections, challenge and exact restoration conditions?
- Are provider boundaries and support ownership legible without forcing repeated explanation?
- Does switching or closure protect continuity, credits, equipment, disputes and durable records?
