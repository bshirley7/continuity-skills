# Campaign design systems

## Generalized principles

- Start with a measurable outcome and a bounded role for communication: distinguish the organizational or social goal, audience behavior or decision, barriers, communication objective, time horizon, baseline, intended contribution, unacceptable harms, and evidence that would support continuation, change, or stop; do not substitute impressions or asset volume for impact.
- Build the campaign from audience evidence rather than a demographic label: identify needs, motivations, barriers, knowledge, trust, language, ability, culture, channel behavior, decision context, gatekeepers, and journey stage; include underserved groups in research and creative development, and preserve meaningful differences instead of forcing one universal execution.
- Define one evidence-backed proposition and a message architecture: state the primary audience promise or useful truth, the single main message, supporting reasons and proof, material conditions, desired action, objections, and consequence-sensitive tone; separate invariant meaning from optional emphasis so every asset remains recognizable without becoming identical.
- Turn the proposition into a generative creative platform: specify the narrative tension and resolution, verbal hook, visual world, subject and image grammar, typography, color, shape, composition, motion, sound, proof devices, calls to action, disclosure treatment, accessibility equivalents, signature elements, and prohibited shortcuts so a family can grow without losing meaning.
- Design a sequence of audience encounters rather than a pile of placements: assign each journey stage and channel a role such as recognition, explanation, proof, comparison, action, onboarding, reminder, or recovery; control frequency, pacing, handoff, destination continuity, and what a person should know or do after each exposure.
- Adapt to channel behavior while preserving campaign invariants: redesign hierarchy, crop, duration, density, interaction, captioning, alternative text, transcript, language, safe zones, and call to action for each format; never shrink a master asset mechanically, place essential meaning only in inaccessible media, or let platform conventions change the claim or destination.
- Make persuasion inspectable and proportionate: substantiate objective and comparative claims before release, present material qualifications where they affect interpretation, identify endorsements and relationships, show representative rather than exceptional outcomes, distinguish paid or generated content, and avoid urgency, scarcity, obstruction, disguised controls, or visual hierarchy that subverts informed choice.
- Govern the campaign as a versioned asset and decision system: maintain the approved proposition, claims register, proof, audiences, channel roles, creative grammar, master assets, derivatives, localization, accessibility, rights, disclosures, owners, review states, expiry, destinations, measurement tags, and change history; require renewed review when evidence, offer, audience, context, or claim changes.
- Evaluate comprehension, response, outcome, equity, and harm across the sequence: pretest message and action prediction, compare creative and channel contributions, separate outputs from audience outtakes and outcomes, inspect subgroup and accessibility performance, monitor complaints and unintended behavior, and record what changed and why rather than optimizing only the easiest conversion metric.

## Method

- Define the outcome, communication contribution, audience behavior, baseline, time horizon, constraints, harms, and stop conditions.
- Research audience needs, barriers, trust, language, ability, culture, channels, gatekeepers, and journey stages with relevant communities.
- Write the proposition, main message, supporting proof, material conditions, objections, action, and tone.
- Define the creative platform across narrative, verbal, image, typography, color, shape, motion, sound, proof, action, disclosures, and prohibited treatments.
- Map encounter sequence and channel roles from recognition through action, onboarding, reminder, and recovery.
- Build an asset matrix with invariants and channel-specific hierarchy, crop, duration, density, accessibility, localization, and destination behavior.
- Complete claim, endorsement, rights, accessibility, privacy, inclusion, and consequence reviews before release.
- Pretest comprehension and action prediction, then measure outputs, outtakes, outcomes, equity, harms, and channel contribution against the original objective.
- Version the proposition, evidence, creative grammar, assets, approvals, expiry, measurement, learning, and supersession.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not optimize a campaign for clicks, conversion, or reach by obscuring material information or weakening informed choice.
- Do not use false urgency, scarcity, social proof, authority, personalization, progress, or visual imbalance to pressure action.
- Do not imply typical results from exceptional testimonials or use endorsements without genuine experience and material-connection disclosure.
- Do not target vulnerability, distress, disability, financial hardship, children, health concerns, or identity with exploitative creative or frequency.
- Do not translate words while preserving imagery, assumptions, humor, or calls to action that are unsafe or incoherent in the local context.
- Do not let inaccessible formats exclude people from essential information, offers, eligibility, services, or response paths.
- Do not claim campaign effectiveness from activity metrics when the intended outcome, subgroup effects, and harms are unknown.

## Variation levers

- Use one direction when proposition, evidence, audience, journey, and visual world are settled; explore multiple platforms when a material creative or channel tradeoff remains.
- Increase explanation and proof for unfamiliar, costly, consequential, or low-trust decisions.
- Increase distinctiveness and emotional expression for recognition while preserving literal claim and action clarity.
- Shift from broad reach to community, partner, or direct channels when trust, language, or access depends on local context.
- Use shorter, repeated recognition assets early and richer proof or decision assets nearer action.
- Reduce frequency and urgency as vulnerability, annoyance, privacy risk, or decision consequence increases.
- Localize proposition, examples, imagery, pacing, and channel mix when research shows more than linguistic variation.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- A single campaign idea creates coherence while oversimplifying different audience barriers.
- Distinctive creative earns attention while competing with proof, comprehension, and accessibility.
- Channel adaptation improves fit while fragmenting the campaign's meaning and identity.
- Frequency builds memory while creating fatigue, pressure, and disproportionate exposure.
- Emotional narrative motivates action while overstating certainty or exploiting vulnerability.
- Rapid optimization improves a local metric while eroding long-term trust or excluding lower-volume groups.
- Strict consistency speeds production while blocking learning and culturally meaningful adaptation.

## Failure modes

- The campaign begins with a slogan or visual trend before an outcome, audience barrier, or role for communication.
- A demographic segment substitutes for evidence about need, context, trust, ability, and journey.
- Each asset invents a new headline, proof set, tone, or call to action.
- A mood board is called a creative platform but cannot generate coherent new assets.
- Channels repeat the same message at the same depth with no encounter sequence or destination continuity.
- Master assets are resized mechanically and lose hierarchy, meaning, accessibility, or safe framing.
- Claims, endorsements, qualifications, and generated content are visually persuasive but materially ambiguous.
- Asset variants circulate without owners, versions, expiry, rights, accessibility, or evidence lineage.
- Success is reported from reach or conversion without comprehension, outcome, subgroup, or harm evidence.

## Anti-patterns

- Slogan before objective.
- Demographics as insight.
- Message drift by asset.
- Mood board as system.
- Channel repetition without journey.
- Resize as adaptation.
- Persuasive ambiguity.
- Unversioned campaign sprawl.
- Activity metric victory.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is the outcome, audience behavior, communication contribution, baseline, timeframe, harm boundary, and stop condition explicit?
- Do audience and community insights explain needs, barriers, trust, ability, language, channels, and journey rather than only demographics?
- Does one evidence-backed proposition organize the main message, proof, conditions, objection handling, action, and tone?
- Can the creative platform reliably generate a coherent and accessible family across narrative, image, type, color, shape, motion, sound, proof, and action?
- Does every encounter and channel have a distinct role, handoff, frequency, and intended audience state?
- Does each asset adapt to its format while preserving meaning, claim, identity, destination, accessibility, and safe composition?
- Are claims, comparisons, endorsements, results, disclosures, paid status, and generated content truthful, substantiated, representative, and inspectable?
- Are proposition, evidence, assets, localization, rights, approvals, expiry, destinations, measurement, and change history governed together?
- Does evaluation cover comprehension, outtakes, outcomes, equity, accessibility, contribution, unintended behavior, and harms—not only outputs?
