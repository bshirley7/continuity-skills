# Imagery, illustration, and art direction

## Generalized principles

- Assign every image one explicit interface role before selecting its style: identity, object preview, evidence, instruction, status, editorial content, atmosphere or decoration; define the claim it may support, the adjacent information that must carry meaning without it and whether it is interactive; and do not let one attractive asset ambiguously stand in for product state, proof, action and brand at once.
- Use empty-state illustration as emotional and spatial support, not as the state description: name what is empty, its scope, why it may be empty and what the person can do; distinguish not-yet-created, filtered, unavailable, permission-blocked, failed and completed states; preserve a labeled recovery or creation action; and avoid celebratory artwork that implies success when data or setup is missing.
- Make capability imagery demonstrate one concrete concept at the moment it becomes useful: align it with a concise heading, explanation, sequence position and labeled control; show boundaries, inputs or outcomes honestly; let the text-and-control path remain complete when the image is hidden or cannot load; and avoid generic decorative onboarding carousels that delay access without teaching a decision or behavior.
- Create a semantic image grammar for collections: assign aspect ratio, crop behavior, focal point, density, corner and background treatment by content type and decision task; keep the image, title, metadata, status and action unmistakably associated; mark video, gallery, live, generated or unavailable media explicitly; and preserve stable comparison instead of forcing unrelated source assets into visually equal but misleading crops.
- Treat functional and evidentiary imagery as inspectable information: show source, owner or subject, time or freshness, representative-versus-illustrative status, edits or transformations and confidence or verification only when supported; provide detail viewing when crop can hide material facts; never use idealized stock, generated, edited or example imagery as evidence of a real person, product, place, result or security state.
- Portray people and identity with consent, dignity and context: use names, roles and relationship labels where relevant; preserve meaningful attributes and avoid crops that objectify, tokenize or erase assistive devices and context; provide neutral fallbacks without inventing traits; represent varied people and settings without stereotyping; and do not treat a portrait, avatar or resemblance as identity verification.
- Design the complete delivery lifecycle, not only the ideal asset: specify responsive source and art direction, safe crop and focal point, zoom or detail access, light and dark treatment, text-overlay contrast, meaningful alternative, decorative suppression, skeleton, progress, retry, unavailable and removed states, bandwidth-aware loading and layout stability; ensure essential content and controls survive every fallback.
- Govern imagery as versioned content: record rights, license, consent, subject and creator attribution, provenance, manipulation or generation status, source quality, focal point, safe area, alternative description, locale, sensitivity, retention, moderation state and owner; define replacement and removal behavior; and review the system across representative products, cultures, abilities, themes, viewport sizes and failure conditions.

## Variation levers

- Increase literal labels, provenance and inspection as imagery moves from atmosphere toward identity, evidence, health, finance, safety or commerce decisions.
- Use stronger fixed ratios and crop rules for comparison grids; allow more expressive composition in editorial or atmospheric moments where exact comparison is not the task.
- Reduce decorative density and payload with constrained bandwidth, cognitive load, compact layouts or repeated operational use.
- Use photography when specific reality, identity or material evidence matters; use illustration when abstraction, explanation, privacy or a nonliteral emotional tone is more appropriate.
- Permit text overlays only when art direction, safe areas and contrast layers remain reliable across every supplied asset and responsive crop.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Rich imagery creates recognition and emotion while increasing payload, distraction and dependence on visual interpretation.
- Uniform crops improve scanning while hiding distinctive or materially relevant parts of source media.
- Illustration can make difficult states approachable while trivializing harm or obscuring operational cause.
- Photography can establish specificity while raising consent, privacy, representation and provenance obligations.
- Brand consistency creates cohesion while excessive art-direction control can erase authentic user or product variation.

## Failure modes

- An image simultaneously implies evidence, status and action without explicit supporting language.
- A cheerful empty-state illustration masks failure, filtering, missing permission or unavailable data.
- An onboarding image is decorative and the surrounding step teaches no capability or decision.
- Mixed content is cropped into identical cards that hide important differences or source context.
- Text placed over arbitrary imagery becomes unreadable at some crops, themes or viewport sizes.
- A stock, edited, generated or example image is perceived as proof of a real outcome.
- People are represented through stereotypes, tokenism, demeaning crops or invented fallback traits.
- Loading, failure, removal or low-bandwidth behavior collapses layout or removes the only meaningful content.
- No one owns rights, consent, provenance, alternative text, focal points, moderation or replacement.

## Anti-patterns

- One image, many claims.
- Cheerful failure camouflage.
- Decorative tour tax.
- Crop-to-sameness.
- Uncontrolled text overlay.
- Illustration presented as proof.
- Token portrait diversity.
- Happy-path-only image delivery.
- Unowned asset library.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does each image have one explicit role, bounded claim and adjacent nonvisual meaning?
- Do empty states name scope, cause and recovery without illustration overstating the state?
- Does capability imagery teach one concrete behavior while the text-and-control path remains complete without it?
- Are content-type ratios, crops, focal points, metadata and mixed-media cues systematic and decision-appropriate?
- Are functional and evidentiary images inspectable, sourced, fresh, truthfully labeled and free of unsupported proof claims?
- Are people portrayed with consent, dignity, contextual labels, inclusive variation and noninvented fallbacks?
- Do responsive crops, overlays, alternatives, loading, failure and low-bandwidth states preserve meaning and action?
- Are rights, consent, provenance, manipulation, descriptions, locale, sensitivity, retention, moderation and ownership governed?
