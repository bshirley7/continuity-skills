# Error prevention and recovery

## Generalized principles

- Prevent errors before messaging them: ask only necessary questions, use familiar and semantically appropriate controls, state constraints before entry, accept reasonable formats, preserve prior answers, and avoid requiring redundant recall.
- When an error occurs, preserve all valid work and identify the affected field, the rejected value or condition, why it is a problem, and a specific correction; provide an accessible summary linked to inline messages and move attention without destroying context.
- Represent progress through meaningful, stable stages and disclose uncertainty in conditional journeys; support back, save, resume, timeout recovery, and repeated entry without confusing visited, valid, complete, submitted, and accepted states.
- For consequential submission, make the commitment checked, confirmed, or reversible: present a complete review, targeted change routes, retained answers, an action-specific submit label, and proportional confirmation or undo.
- Close the recovery loop with an accessible confirmation and durable record of answers, omitted fields, recipient, time, reference, processing state, next steps, and correction, cancellation, or support routes.

## Method

- Map required information, why it is needed, where users obtain it, conditional dependencies, sensitive fields, and consequences of error or omission.
- Choose controls and accepted formats that prevent invalid states while remaining tolerant of legitimate variation and assistive input.
- Specify draft, saved, valid, complete, reviewed, submitted, received, processing, accepted, rejected, and corrected states separately.
- Design summary and inline errors together, retaining valid input and providing focus, announcement, navigation, and concrete correction.
- Test back, refresh, timeout, resume, branch changes, upload failure, duplicate submission, server rejection, review edits, and post-submit correction.
- Measure completion with error recurrence, work loss, correction time, review changes, abandonment, support demand, duplicate submission, and post-submit correction.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not exploit validation or disabled controls to force optional consent, marketing, or unrelated data collection.
- Do not describe a submission as complete when only a draft, upload, request, or queued process exists.
- Do not clear valid or sensitive work as punishment for one error, timeout, or authentication interruption.
- Do not make correction materially harder than initial entry.
- Do not use confirmation friction indiscriminately; reserve it for consequence, uncertainty, and irreversibility.
- Do not hide rejected, skipped, inferred, or altered answers from the final record.

## Variation levers

- Increase prevention, review, record keeping, and reversibility with consequence and data sensitivity.
- Use immediate validation after meaningful completion of a field, not on every keystroke when the value is necessarily incomplete.
- Use one-question pages for complex branching and grouped pages for short, cohesive, low-risk inputs.
- Use section or task progress when totals vary and numeric progress only when the denominator is stable.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Strict constraints prevent invalid data while rejecting legitimate variation.
- Immediate feedback speeds correction while interrupting thought and assistive output.
- More review reduces errors while adding burden and habituation.
- Autosave protects work while blurring draft and commitment.
- Progress indicators orient users while becoming misleading in conditional journeys.

## Failure modes

- Instructions or constraints appear only after rejection.
- Errors are generic, color-only, unlinked, or erase valid input.
- A disabled action provides no explanation of what remains incomplete.
- Progress counts pages rather than meaningful work or changes denominator silently.
- Review edits restart the remaining journey or lose dependent answers without explanation.
- Confirmation lacks a record, processing state, reference, next step, or correction route.

## Anti-patterns

- Teach by rejection.
- Red means wrong.
- Disabled mystery.
- Progress theater.
- Edit means repeat.
- Success without a record.
- Autosave as commitment.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are constraints, purpose, required status, accepted formats, and examples available before entry?
- Does correction preserve valid work and identify where, what, why, and how to fix each problem?
- Are visited, valid, complete, saved, reviewed, submitted, received, and accepted states distinct?
- Can users go back, resume, recover from timeout, and change branches without unexplained loss?
- Is consequential commitment reviewable, specifically labeled, and checked, confirmed, or reversible?
- Does completion provide an accessible record, status, reference, next steps, and correction or cancellation path?
