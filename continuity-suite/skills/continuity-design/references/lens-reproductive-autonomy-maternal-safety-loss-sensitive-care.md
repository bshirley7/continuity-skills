# Reproductive autonomy, maternal safety, and loss-sensitive care

## Generalized principles

- Evaluate reproductive autonomy before engagement: does the experience provide accurate, neutral and context-appropriate information; allow wanted, unwanted, ambivalent and uncertain pregnancy states; support continuation, termination, adoption and parenting conversations according to applicable care context; preserve privacy and nondirective shared decisions; and avoid assumptions about gender, partnership, fertility goals, morality, joy or grief? Treat access barriers and constrained choices as design evidence, not user preference.
- Require source, uncertainty and time for every pregnancy claim: distinguish self-report, screening, test, ultrasound or clinical assessment; identify dating method and range; separate population milestones from individual findings; update all derived content when evidence changes; show who interpreted tests and what remains unknown; and prevent due dates, fetal comparisons, risk scores or normal ranges from becoming deterministic truth.
- Center maternal safety through pregnancy and postpartum: place the pregnant or recently pregnant person's symptoms, concern and context above fetal-content reassurance or engagement; make urgent warning signs recognizable, actionable and available through the relevant postpartum period; carry pregnancy history into handoff; identify responsive services and fallback; listen even when measurements appear normal; and audit dismissal, delay and unequal treatment as safety failures.
- Evaluate tests, medicines and exposures as benefit-risk decisions rather than labels: identify purpose, exact intervention, gestational or lactation timing, evidence source, known findings, data limitations, alternatives and consequences of treating or not treating; support qualified discussion and the person's values; preserve changes and correction; and prohibit generic safe, unsafe, natural or risk-free claims and app-directed treatment changes.
- Design respectful care as an operational requirement: protect dignity, bodily autonomy, privacy, confidentiality, informed consent, communication, chosen support and freedom from discrimination, neglect, abuse and non-consensual intervention across appointments, exams, labor, birth and postpartum care; make preferences visible without presenting them as guarantees; preserve reasoned changes during emergencies; and provide complaint, correction and remedy routes without threatening access.
- Make loss-sensitive care systemic rather than cosmetic: support diagnostic uncertainty, ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage, termination, stillbirth and neonatal loss with person-chosen language, management options, physical expectations, pain and bleeding guidance, urgent routes, privacy, follow-up and emotional support; stop celebratory content everywhere; retain necessary clinical history without forced reminders; allow future mode changes; and never require repeated disclosure to suppress triggering content.
- Continue person-centered care after birth or pregnancy end: treat postpartum as an ongoing process with physical recovery, chronic conditions, mental health, sleep, social and material support, feeding goals, medicine and lactation decisions, contraception only through informed choice, primary-care transition and urgent warning signs; name maternal-care ownership independently from infant care; and avoid a single routine visit or baby-focused experience as proof of recovery.
- Audit reproductive experiences for safety, autonomy and distribution: measure timely care, ignored concerns, emergency connection, consent, mistreatment, privacy breach, inaccurate inference, option access, loss-trigger suppression, postpartum follow-up, mental-health treatment and patient-reported respect; segment by race, ethnicity, language, disability, gender identity, age, geography, coverage and outcome; include people who leave or conceal data; and never optimize pregnancy-mode retention, content views or birth outcomes alone.

## Method

- Trace possible pregnancy through options, care, outcome and postpartum or post-pregnancy continuity.
- Inventory every inferred, self-reported, tested and clinically confirmed state and derived content.
- Test uncertain dating, changed estimates, multiple gestation, urgent symptoms and unavailable care.
- Walk wanted, unwanted, ambivalent, ended and adverse-outcome journeys without assumed emotion.
- Review tests and medicines for exact benefit, risk, unknowns, timing and decision authority.
- Test consent, privacy, support-person, discrimination and emergency change scenarios.
- Verify physical and mental-health continuity independently from infant content.
- Audit safety, respect, autonomy, access, triggering content and unequal outcomes together.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not infer or disclose pregnancy without explicit understood purpose and consent.
- Do not assume pregnancy intention, continuation, parenting, identity or emotional response.
- Do not present an estimated date or population milestone as individual certainty.
- Do not let fetal content override maternal concern or urgent care.
- Do not reduce medicine decisions to safe or unsafe labels.
- Do not leave celebratory content active after changed or adverse outcomes.
- Do not pressure contraception, feeding or other postpartum choices.
- Do not optimize engagement while hiding mistreatment, delayed care or unequal harm.

## Variation levers

- Increase specialist coordination and escalation for high-risk or complex pregnancy.
- Increase privacy controls when reproductive information creates interpersonal or legal danger.
- Increase loss-sensitive suppression and support after uncertain or adverse outcomes.
- Increase accessibility, language, transport and assisted routes for underserved populations.
- Increase postpartum monitoring and continuity for surgical, hypertensive, mental-health and chronic conditions.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Personalization versus private reproductive inference.
- Gestational orientation versus false developmental certainty.
- Support-person involvement versus autonomy and coercion.
- Risk awareness versus anxiety and dismissal through normalization.
- Infant support versus maternal erasure.
- Standard pathways versus diverse choices and outcomes.

## Failure modes

- Pregnancy mode activates from hidden inference.
- The interface assumes joy and continuation.
- Estimated development appears exact.
- A normal tracker state suppresses maternal concern.
- Medicine receives a binary safety badge.
- Loss triggers remain active across the product.
- Postpartum care becomes infant-only content.
- Average outcomes hide disrespect and unequal mortality.

## Anti-patterns

- Reproductive inference engine.
- Compulsory happy path.
- Gestational certainty theater.
- Fetal reassurance override.
- Binary pregnancy pharmacology.
- Partial loss reset.
- Maternal disappearance.
- Outcome-only maternity scorecard.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does the experience preserve private, informed and nondirective reproductive choice?
- Are pregnancy claims sourced, time-bound and uncertain where appropriate?
- Are maternal concerns and urgent warning signs centered through postpartum?
- Are tests and medicines represented as qualified benefit-risk decisions?
- Are dignity, consent, privacy, support and freedom from mistreatment operational?
- Does loss-sensitive behavior update the entire experience immediately?
- Does postpartum care retain maternal physical, mental and longitudinal health?
- Are respect, safety, autonomy, access and unequal outcomes audited beyond engagement?
