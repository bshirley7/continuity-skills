# Minimal

## Generalized principles

- Reduce visual competition by establishing one dominant task or state, a small number of stable regions, restrained accents, and generous separation while keeping operational meaning explicit.
- Treat state as essential content: preserve progress, selection, validation, save or publish status, success feedback, urgency, and recovery even when the surrounding interface is sparse.
- Use contextual tools and stable edge zones to keep the work surface focused without making important capabilities, collaboration, navigation, or sharing undiscoverable.
- Make empty and low-data states explain what is absent, why it matters, and the next relevant action instead of using whitespace as a substitute for guidance.
- For forms, preserve persistent labels, coherent groups, visible requirements, inline validation, progress, and fixed forward and backward actions; minimal does not mean under-specified.
- Let urgent or consequential information temporarily outrank the normal minimal hierarchy while keeping routine actions compact and recoverable.

## Variation levers

- Use cards for independent summaries and a single work surface for focused creation.
- Use contextual toolbars for selection-specific actions and stable rails for frequent destinations.
- Use one-step forms for bounded decisions and named progress for longer regulated or business flows.
- Use restrained accent color for state and action, reserving stronger alert color for consequences.
- Use generous whitespace at desktop scale and compact grouped regions on mobile.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Whitespace improves focus while reducing information visible per viewport.
- Contextual tools reduce chrome while weakening discoverability.
- Large empty states offer guidance while overemphasizing absence.
- A single primary action clarifies priority while secondary recovery paths may become too quiet.
- Restrained color reduces noise while weakening state differentiation.

## Failure modes

- Sparse screens omit current state or next action.
- Placeholders or empty cards dominate low-data workspaces.
- Form labels disappear after entry or validation lacks actionable detail.
- Contextual controls are the only route to essential actions.
- Urgent operational information is suppressed to preserve aesthetic calm.
- Minimal mobile layouts hide creation, feedback, or recovery.

## Anti-patterns

- Whitespace without hierarchy.
- Icon-only critical actions.
- Placeholder-heavy dashboards.
- Validation only after submission.
- Hidden save or publish state.
- Low-contrast status encoding.
- Removing useful information solely to make a screen look clean.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is the dominant task or state immediately clear?
- Are progress, validation, selection, save, success, urgency, and recovery explicit?
- Can users discover both contextual and frequent tools?
- Do empty states explain absence and next action?
- Are forms grouped, labeled, validated, and navigable?
- Can consequential information override the quiet hierarchy when needed?
