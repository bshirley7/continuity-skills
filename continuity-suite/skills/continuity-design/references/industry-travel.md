# Travel

## Generalized principles

- Keep travel search scope persistent through origin, destination, dates, travelers, rooms, cabin or property type, policy, filters, sort, map area, result count, and the price basis shown.
- Make options comparable through total and unit price, availability, timing, location, transfers, baggage, amenities, rating basis, cancellation, loyalty or policy status, and material restrictions.
- At booking review, preserve the exact itinerary, traveler identity, operator, segment, baggage, ticket relationship, amendability, refundability, fees, add-ons, payment, currency, and final total.
- Separate optional protection from base travel terms, naming the covered event, price, affected traveler or segment, exclusions, claim path, and interaction with carrier or supplier policy.
- Treat delay, cancellation, schedule change, missed connection, and supplier cancellation as distinct states with source, last update, affected segment, time consequence, and accountable recovery party.
- Place alternatives, rebooking, refund, supplier support, platform support, and reference details beside disruption state, preserving what happens to unaffected trip components.
- Build trip continuity around a time-zone-aware sequence of transport, lodging, activities, check-in, documents, local details, and support rather than a disconnected reservation archive.
- Use urgency only for real availability or operational deadlines and keep countdowns, viewer counts, discounts, and policy limits subordinate to decision-critical terms.

## Variation levers

- Use map-plus-list for location-sensitive lodging and aligned rows for schedule-sensitive transport.
- Use per-night or per-person price only alongside total trip commitment.
- Use staged checkout for complex itineraries and compact review for one simple segment.
- Use a strong disruption banner for changed operations and a recovery workspace for alternatives.
- Use supplier-first support when only the operator can act and platform coordination when the platform owns the booking.
- Use local-time timelines for active trips and origin-time context where it prevents confusion.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- More search filters improve fit while hiding the true comparison basis.
- Bundling reduces planning effort while obscuring per-component terms.
- Protection products reduce anxiety while complicating total commitment.
- Urgency can reflect scarce inventory while encouraging rushed decisions.
- Real-time disruption status improves awareness while increasing stress without recovery.
- Unified itineraries improve continuity while depending on incomplete external reservation data.

## Failure modes

- Search results omit scope, price basis, or result count.
- Options cannot be compared on baggage, restrictions, cancellation, or total.
- Checkout hides separate-ticket, operator, fee, add-on, or currency consequences.
- Protection is sold without coverage and exclusion detail.
- A disruption lacks source, update time, affected segment, or time consequence.
- Cancellation provides status but no accountable recovery route.
- Trip itinerary ignores time zones or unaffected reservations.
- Scarcity messaging outranks cancellation and total-price terms.

## Anti-patterns

- Showing from price with no total basis.
- Comparing flight times without transfers or baggage.
- Treating a package as one inseparable policy.
- Preselecting travel protection.
- Using delayed without scheduled and estimated time.
- Sending users to contact support with no booking reference.
- Listing reservations without chronological trip context.
- Using countdowns to suppress policy review.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is search scope, map area, result count, and price basis persistent?
- Can users compare timing, location, baggage, amenities, restrictions, policy, and total?
- Does booking review preserve itinerary, traveler, operator, ticket relation, fees, payment, and currency?
- Are protection coverage, exclusions, price, and claim path explicit?
- Do disruptions expose source, update, segment, timing, and responsible party?
- Are alternatives, refund, rebooking, supplier, platform, and reference routes nearby?
- Does the itinerary preserve time zones, sequence, documents, and unaffected components?
- Is urgency limited to real operational or availability deadlines?
