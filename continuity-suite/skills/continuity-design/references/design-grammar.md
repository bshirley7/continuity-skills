# Design grammar

Every direction must translate intent, evidence, selected catalog packs, and preservation requirements into one coherent design grammar. The grammar is an implementation-facing decision contract, not a mood board, token dump, or authorization to change code.

## Required dimensions

- **Composition:** Content bounds, reading order, grid, alignment, module spans, section pacing, emphasis, and deliberate exceptions.
- **Spacing and density:** Relational spacing, task density, grouping intervals, content measure, and differences between exploratory, routine, expert, and consequential work.
- **Typography:** Type roles, hierarchy, voice, reading measure, data treatment, responsive behavior, localization, and user scaling.
- **Color:** Brand and neutral palette roles, semantic color, contrast, data encoding, themes, environmental conditions, and non-color redundancy.
- **Shape and form:** Geometry by semantic role, corner and stroke character, containers, controls, objects, status, signature forms, and state variants.
- **Surface and depth:** Canvas, panels, boundaries, elevation, overlays, modality, translucency, texture, themes, accessibility fallbacks, and performance constraints.
- **Imagery:** Image role, art direction, bounded claims, crops, responsive delivery, provenance, representation, alternatives, loading, failure, and removal.
- **Iconography:** Semantic inventory, familiar and custom symbols, labels, accessible names, visual family, state redundancy, localization, and governance.
- **Motion:** State continuity, timing, choreography, progress, expressive moments, interruption, reduced motion, performance, and non-motion equivalents.
- **Voice:** Brand voice, operational language, labels, calls to action, uncertainty, errors, success, consequence-sensitive tone, and localization.
- **State language:** Visual and verbal treatment for default, hover, focus, active, selected, disabled, unavailable, pending, complete, stale, synchronized, warning, error, and recovery.
- **Responsive behavior:** Semantic priority, transformation rules, content order, grouping, comparison, navigation, action proximity, state preservation, and modality-specific adaptation.

## Coherence rules

- Every rule must trace to project intent, a selected direction, a preservation requirement, or a reviewed catalog pack.
- Dimensions must reinforce one another. Do not choose typography, color, geometry, motion, imagery, and voice as independent style samples.
- Name bounded variation and prohibited combinations where inconsistent interpretation would be likely.
- Preserve familiar interaction semantics and accessibility even when expression changes.
- Distinguish universal decisions from section-specific or platform-specific exceptions.
- Classify each pack against the requested target as validated, inferred, or not applicable. Apply only modality-independent principles from inferred packs, exclude not-applicable packs, and label the target mixed when direct and inferred evidence are composed.
- Keep unresolved material decisions explicit rather than hiding them behind broad adjectives.

## Approval boundary

The grammar becomes canonical only as part of the exact hash-approved `docs/design/design.md`. Its approval authorizes publication of that document only. Implementation still requires a separately approved and hash-bound goal.
