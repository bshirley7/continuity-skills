# Professional services and corporate consulting

## Generalized principles

- Lead with a distinctive, decision-relevant point of view and immediately clarify the audience, business situation, or transformation it applies to; do not ask a slogan to carry the full proposition.
- Organize expertise through both business problems and recognizable practices or sectors, allowing visitors to enter by need while keeping the firm's delivery structure legible.
- Describe each capability through the business change, mechanism, and boundary it addresses rather than presenting an undifferentiated inventory of consulting deliverables.
- Treat thought leadership as inspectable intellectual evidence: identify the question or audience, format, date, author or expert, evidence basis, and a concise reason the finding matters now.
- Structure client impact around the client context, challenge, intervention, and resulting outcome, attaching metrics to timeframe and comparison basis instead of relying on logos or isolated numbers.
- Use identifiable experts as evidence by pairing names and roles with practice or sector relevance, operating responsibility, selected credentials, authored thinking, and a route to deeper biography where appropriate.
- Separate corporate purpose, sustainability, governance, and impact claims from promotional language and connect them to named priorities, stakeholder scope, accountable ownership, and reportable evidence.
- Offer an exploratory path through practices, sectors, work, and insights before a direct engagement path, then explain what beginning a conversation entails and keep requested information proportionate to visitor readiness.
- Use editorial restraint, typography, documentary imagery, and measured pacing to signal authority, but keep proposition, navigation, evidence, and action legible without depending on animation or aesthetic confidence alone.
- Keep point of view, capability architecture, client proof, intellectual property, expert profiles, and engagement language mutually reinforcing so the corporate story does not fragment into unrelated content systems.

## Variation levers

- Lead with a proprietary point of view when the firm is differentiated and well understood, or feature a timely insight when sector relevance and intellectual authority need to be established first.
- Use a practice-first architecture when buyers know the service they need, an industry-first architecture when sector context changes the work materially, and a problem-first entry when needs cross established practice boundaries.
- Use concise capability groups for senior scanning and reveal detailed deliverables progressively; expose the full taxonomy only when it helps procurement or specialist evaluation.
- Present client impact as comparable outcome cards when measures share a basis, and use narrative case-study modules when context, confidentiality, or system change matters more than a headline number.
- Use an institutional editorial voice for large global firms, a named-expert voice for specialist consultancies, or combine them by attaching experts to an institutional research system.
- Use dated report and article lists for research-heavy firms, curated thematic collections for enduring intellectual property, and a lead-story hierarchy when one current point of view should frame the site.
- Use a compact expert directory when practice discovery is primary and richer biography cards when individual reputation materially influences selection.
- Offer contact by challenge, practice, expert, or office when routing matters; keep one general conversation path when the firm is small enough to triage personally.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- A concise institutional thesis signals confidence while becoming vague when audience, problem, and mechanism remain unstated.
- Broad capability coverage supports complex procurement while making the firm appear interchangeable with competitors.
- Editorial restraint and whitespace communicate seniority while hiding navigation, proof, or action when taken too far.
- Frequent thought leadership demonstrates activity while diluting intellectual authority if articles lack authorship, evidence, or a coherent research agenda.
- Quantified client outcomes support credibility while becoming misleading without baseline, timeframe, attribution, and confidentiality context.
- Prominent partners humanize expertise while weakening institutional continuity if the firm's method and collective capability remain invisible.
- A detailed qualification form improves routing while asking prospective clients to disclose too much before trust or fit is established.
- Awards and recognizable client marks accelerate confidence while substituting reputation for relevance when they are disconnected from the nearby claim.

## Failure modes

- The opening uses transformation, impact, innovation, or future-facing language without naming the audience, business problem, mechanism, or differentiating point of view.
- Capabilities appear as a long service inventory with no problem, outcome, sector, priority, or relationship among practices.
- Industry pages repeat generic corporate copy without demonstrating how sector economics, regulation, operations, or customers change the work.
- Thought-leadership cards omit date, author, format, evidence source, intended audience, or the specific question addressed.
- Client proof consists of logos, awards, isolated metrics, or testimonial fragments without challenge, intervention, timeframe, and outcome context.
- Leadership appears as portraits and titles without practice relevance, credentials, authored work, operating responsibility, or a deeper profile path.
- Purpose and sustainability claims remain aspirational and cannot be traced to priorities, owners, targets, evidence, or reporting scope.
- Every page ends with the same generic contact action regardless of whether the visitor is exploring a problem, evaluating expertise, seeking support, or ready to engage.
- Large type, motion, abstraction, or image-led storytelling makes the firm feel distinctive while obscuring navigation, proposition, proof, or the next meaningful step.
- Insights, capabilities, case studies, expert profiles, and contact language use different taxonomies, preventing visitors from following one coherent decision path.

## Anti-patterns

- Opening with a category claim or abstract slogan that could describe any global consulting firm.
- Using a dense mega-menu or capability wall as a substitute for explaining which problems the firm is equipped to solve.
- Treating a report cover, article image, or executive portrait as sufficient evidence without date, authorship, method, or synopsis.
- Displaying a client-logo wall before clarifying what work the firm performed or what changed for those clients.
- Publishing an outcome percentage without baseline, timeframe, denominator, attribution, or a route to the full case.
- Using awards as the primary proof for a proposition whose relevance still has not been explained.
- Presenting leadership as an ornamental carousel with no practice or sector context.
- Requiring revenue, budget, phone number, and detailed project disclosure before explaining the engagement process or offering a lower-commitment path.
- Hiding the primary navigation or engagement route behind experimental motion and unlabeled controls to signal creative confidence.
- Mixing careers, support, investor, media, and prospective-client inquiries into one undifferentiated contact experience.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does the opening express a distinctive point of view while naming the audience, business situation, or transformation to which it applies?
- Can visitors enter expertise by problem, practice, or sector without encountering duplicate or contradictory taxonomies?
- Does every capability explain the business change, delivery mechanism, and relevant boundary rather than only naming a service?
- Do insights expose topic or audience, format, date, author or expert, evidence basis, and a clear reason the finding matters?
- Does client proof connect context, challenge, intervention, and outcome, with metrics tied to timeframe and comparison basis?
- Do expert profiles show practice or sector relevance, operating responsibility, credentials, authored thinking, and a route to deeper context?
- Are purpose, sustainability, and impact claims connected to named priorities, stakeholders, ownership, targets, and reportable evidence?
- Can a visitor explore fit before contacting the firm, and does the engagement path explain what happens next while requesting proportionate information?
- Does the visual system convey authority without hiding proposition, navigation, evidence, accessibility, or action?
- Do point of view, practices, sectors, insights, cases, experts, and engagement use one coherent language system throughout the site?
