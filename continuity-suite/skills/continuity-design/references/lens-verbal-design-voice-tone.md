# Verbal design, voice, and tone

## Generalized principles

- Define a canonical vocabulary before polishing tone: name each important object, action, role, state, scope, and consequence once; use the same term wherever the same concept appears, distinguish terms when concepts differ, and document prohibited aliases, ambiguous verbs, and domain terms that require explanation.
- Keep voice stable and adapt tone to context: express a consistent product character while changing warmth, urgency, brevity, and formality according to the person's likely state, task frequency, consequence, uncertainty, and need for reassurance; reduce personality as risk, distress, or irreversibility increases.
- Structure language around the person's task: lead with the information or decision needed now, use familiar and concrete words, keep sentences and paragraphs scannable, reveal detail progressively, and place explanation where it supports rather than interrupts action.
- Make labels and calls to action predict behavior: name the action and, when material, its object, scope, timing, cost, sharing, reversibility, or consequence; avoid vague, clever, coercive, or falsely reassuring labels, and keep visible and programmatic names aligned.
- Design state language as part of the state system: state what happened, what is affected, whether work was retained, what the system is doing, and what the person can do next; identify errors in text, avoid blame, connect messages to affected controls, announce important status changes programmatically, and preserve recovery paths.
- Calibrate claims and recommendations to evidence: distinguish observed facts, estimates, predictions, suggestions, and decisions; identify uncertainty, important assumptions, source or freshness when material, and who remains responsible; never use confident language, celebration, or authority cues to overstate safety, completion, consent, or likely outcomes.
- Write for accessibility and translation from the start: provide visible labels and instructions, avoid meaning carried only by position, color, idiom, humor, or cultural shorthand, keep grammatical structures direct, leave room for expansion, and test assistive announcements and localized variants in their actual interface context.
- Govern verbal design as a product system: maintain the vocabulary, voice attributes, tone matrix, message patterns, examples, ownership, review triggers, localization notes, and measurement plan; review language with design, research, accessibility, legal, support, and domain experts according to consequence rather than treating copy as final-stage polish.

## Method

- Inventory product nouns, verbs, roles, states, messages, claims, and repeated terms across the complete journey.
- Create a canonical vocabulary with definitions, preferred terms, prohibited aliases, and domain explanations.
- Define a stable voice and a tone matrix by emotional state, consequence, urgency, frequency, and uncertainty.
- Map each control and message to the person's task, predicted behavior, affected scope, consequence, and next step.
- Write complete happy, empty, loading, validation, error, partial-success, offline, permission, and recovery states.
- Classify claims as fact, estimate, prediction, recommendation, or decision and add material uncertainty, provenance, and responsibility.
- Test scanning, comprehension, action prediction, assistive announcements, translation expansion, sensitive contexts, and recovery.
- Record vocabulary, patterns, examples, owners, review triggers, and measures in the design system.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not use language to disguise cost, sharing, renewal, risk, irreversibility, or loss of control.
- Do not manufacture urgency, scarcity, social proof, progress, authority, or certainty.
- Do not blame, shame, infantilize, flatter, or emotionally pressure people into an action.
- Do not imply that generated, estimated, or recommended output is observed fact or settled professional advice.
- Do not use personality, humor, celebration, or euphemism where it trivializes harm, failure, privacy, health, finance, or security.
- Do not rely on inaccessible labels, unexplained jargon, cultural idioms, or interface position to carry essential meaning.

## Variation levers

- Increase brevity and directness for frequent expert workflows while preserving consequence and recovery information.
- Increase guidance and explanation for unfamiliar, complex, or first-run tasks.
- Reduce warmth, playfulness, metaphor, and promotional language as consequence, distress, or uncertainty increases.
- Use progressive disclosure for policy, technical, or legal detail without hiding the decision-relevant summary.
- Use domain terminology when it is the audience's precise working language and define it when audience knowledge varies.
- Adapt examples, sentence structure, and available space for locale while preserving canonical concepts and action meaning.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Brevity speeds scanning while omitting context needed for a safe decision.
- A distinctive voice builds recognition while becoming distracting or insensitive in consequential moments.
- Consistency reduces learning while domain-specific nuance sometimes requires differentiated terms.
- Plain language improves access while specialized audiences may need exact technical vocabulary.
- Progressive disclosure reduces immediate load while hiding information people need before acting.
- Friendly phrasing reduces friction while softening material risk or system responsibility.

## Failure modes

- The same object or action has several names across navigation, controls, help, and messages.
- A single upbeat tone is used for celebration, errors, permissions, and consequential decisions.
- Headings and openings provide context before revealing the task or decision.
- Buttons use vague labels whose scope or consequence is only explained elsewhere.
- Errors state that something failed without identifying the affected work, retained state, or recovery.
- Recommendations and generated output are phrased as certain facts.
- Idioms, humor, visual position, or color carry meaning that is lost in translation or assistive use.
- Copy is reviewed only at the end and has no vocabulary, owner, pattern library, or measurement.

## Anti-patterns

- Synonym drift.
- One cheerful tone everywhere.
- Context before task.
- Mystery-meat action labels.
- Dead-end error copy.
- Confidence without evidence.
- Untranslatable personality.
- Copy as decoration.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does every important object, action, role, state, scope, and consequence use a canonical and unambiguous term?
- Is the voice stable while tone responds appropriately to emotional state, consequence, urgency, frequency, and uncertainty?
- Can people scan the language and identify the task, decision, or next step before secondary context?
- Do labels and calls to action accurately predict behavior, scope, timing, cost, sharing, reversibility, and consequence where material?
- Do all states explain what happened, what is affected, what was retained, what happens next, and how to recover accessibly?
- Are facts, estimates, predictions, recommendations, and decisions distinguished with material uncertainty and responsibility?
- Does the language remain complete and understandable through assistive technology, translation, expansion, and loss of visual context?
- Are vocabulary, voice, tone, message patterns, ownership, review triggers, and measures governed as part of the design system?
