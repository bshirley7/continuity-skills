# Conversation grounding, turn-taking, and repair

## Generalized principles

- Design for a purpose-relative grounding criterion: before participants act, make the current topic, referenced objects, relevant prior turns, known facts, assumptions, unresolved ambiguity, participant and system capabilities, and evidence of understanding visible enough for the consequence; increase explicit restatement and verification as ambiguity, delay, audience size, automation, or harm rises.
- Make participation and turn structure explicit without forcing rigid alternation: identify speaker or system, direct addressee, side participants, observers, current input state, expected responder, interruption and notification priority, and whether a response is optional, requested, or required; present sequential updates accessibly and avoid stealing focus for routine activity.
- Distinguish receipt, attention, comprehension, acknowledgment, agreement, authorization, commitment, and completion: use lightweight acknowledgment for low-risk continuity, targeted confirmation for material parameters, and explicit scope, actor, action, consequence, and durable evidence for consequential decisions; never infer commitment from presence, read state, reaction, silence, or conversational fluency.
- Treat misunderstanding as a joint repair problem, not user failure: identify the uncertain reference or unsupported action, preserve valid context, offer a concise interpretation or bounded alternatives, accept one-step correction and topic change, escalate support with increasing help rather than repetition, and provide a graceful exit or human route when common ground cannot be reached.
- Preserve grounded context across correction, summary, forwarding, participant change, and human-system handoff: carry the source sequence, participants and roles, explicit decisions and open questions, attachments, confidence and system limits, requested action, consent and privacy boundary, and handoff state; label generated interpretation and let recipients inspect and correct the source before relying on it.

## Method

- Define conversation purpose, consequence, participants and roles, direct audience, objects, shared evidence, required grounding level, privacy boundary, system capabilities, and handoff conditions.
- Map turn initiation, input availability, reply scope, interruption, acknowledgment, clarification, confirmation, refusal, topic change, correction, timeout, exit, and re-entry.
- Specify which states are transient signals and which become durable decisions, actions, commitments, approvals, evidence, or records.
- Design accessible sequencing, polite versus urgent updates, unread restoration, focus behavior, multimodal equivalence, and reduced-interruption controls.
- Test ambiguous references, delayed replies, overlapping topics, changed participants, mistaken recipients, edits, missing context, automation limits, adverse outcomes, interrupted input, and assistive technology.
- Measure correct addressee and referent understanding, repair success, repeated explanation, false agreement, interruption cost, accessibility, handoff continuity, correction reach, and durable commitment accuracy.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not use typing, presence, read receipts, response latency, sentiment, or conversational style to coerce availability, infer consent, score commitment, or penalize workers and customers.
- Do not disguise automation, generated messages, summaries, translation, moderation, or human takeover; preserve actor identity and system limits at the relevant turn.
- Do not forward private conversation, add participants, broaden search or model access, or change retention without audience review, authorization, and visible provenance.
- Do not silently rewrite, delete, reorder, summarize away, or selectively quote conversation in ways that alter a shared decision or accountability record.
- Do not force people to repeat sensitive, traumatic, accessibility, identity, or diagnostic context after transfer when authorized continuity can preserve it.
- Do not optimize engagement, response speed, or apparent conversational fluency while ignoring understanding, refusal, repair, privacy, false commitment, and downstream harm.

## Variation levers

- Use implicit grounding for low-risk reversible social continuity and explicit read-back, confirmation, and evidence for financial, safety, legal, clinical, access, or publication commitments.
- Use linear turn-taking for simple paired exchange and thread, quote, topic, or object anchors as participant count, latency, and topic concurrency increase.
- Use passive polite announcements for routine new content and assertive interruption only when immediate awareness is necessary to prevent material harm.
- Use automated repair for bounded ambiguity and human handoff when intent, authority, emotion, risk, or repeated failure exceeds system capability.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Explicit confirmation reduces consequential errors while making ordinary conversation slow and repetitive.
- Persistent context lowers repair cost while expanding privacy, retention, and inappropriate reuse risk.
- Fast asynchronous participation increases flexibility while weakening shared attention and immediate evidence of understanding.
- Generated summaries accelerate catch-up while collapsing disagreement, uncertainty, minority views, and source nuance.
- Presence and read state improve coordination while creating surveillance and coerced responsiveness.

## Failure modes

- A participant cannot tell who is speaking, addressed, observing, expected to respond, or authorized to act.
- A reference, reply, pronoun, attachment, or action is interpreted without enough visible source context.
- Receipt, reaction, acknowledgment, or fluent response is treated as understanding, consent, approval, or completion.
- Repair repeats the same prompt, discards valid context, blames the user, or forces the conversation to restart.
- Routine incoming content interrupts focus or remains unavailable to screen readers and other assistive technology.
- A summary, correction, forward, or handoff omits source, participants, disagreement, uncertainty, decisions, open questions, or privacy boundary.

## Anti-patterns

- Fluency equals understanding.
- Reaction equals consent.
- Pronoun without referent.
- Reprompt without repair.
- Every update interrupts.
- Summary becomes source.
- Silent participant expansion.
- Handoff starts over.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Is topic, referent, prior context, known fact, assumption, ambiguity, capability, and evidence of understanding sufficient for the consequence?
- Can everyone distinguish speaker, system, addressee, observer, expected responder, input state, interruption priority, and response obligation?
- Are receipt, attention, comprehension, acknowledgment, agreement, authorization, commitment, and completion represented distinctly?
- Does repair preserve valid context, identify uncertainty, offer bounded interpretation, accept correction or topic change, increase useful help, and allow exit or handoff?
- Are sequential updates accessible, appropriately interruptive, recoverable from unread state, and equivalent across modalities?
- Do correction, summary, forwarding, participant change, and handoff preserve source, roles, decisions, disagreement, open questions, limits, privacy, and correction?
