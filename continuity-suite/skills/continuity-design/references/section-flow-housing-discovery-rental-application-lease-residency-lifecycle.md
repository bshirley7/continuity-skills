# Housing discovery, rental application, lease, and residency lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Preserve one source-linked housing opportunity from discovery through tenancy: identify property, building and exact unit separately; show address or bounded location, owner or authorized manager, unit attributes, accessibility claims, occupancy, availability source and freshness, advertised rent, mandatory charges, deposit, utilities, term, move-in window, application deadline and material rules; bind inquiry, tour, application, screening, lease and payment to that record; and never silently substitute a different unit or set of terms.
- Make discovery and comparison truthful before collecting identity: expose search area, filters, ranking basis, sponsored placement, listing source, duplicates, stale or unavailable state, total recurring cost and comparable unit facts; preserve map and list context; label estimates and provider claims; offer accessible-property and accommodation-relevant attributes without stereotyping neighborhoods or residents; and do not use hidden targeting or proxy signals to narrow who sees an opportunity.
- Sequence inquiry, tour, application, screening, approval and lease as distinct commitments: show what each stage reserves or does not reserve, who acts next, expected timing, expiry, competition or waitlist status, fees and refundability, correction and withdrawal; do not imply that viewing, applying, paying a fee, passing screening, receiving conditional approval or paying a holding amount guarantees the unit until the applicable agreement actually does.
- Make every application request purpose-bound and household-aware: distinguish applicant, co-applicant, occupant, minor, guarantor, caregiver, live-in aide, service or assistance animal and emergency contact; explain required versus optional identity, income, rental, credit, criminal, employment and reference evidence; support alternative proof and accommodation; preserve drafts and exact submissions; avoid repeated collection; and never ask for sensitive documents, money or broad profile data before the verified stage requires them.
- Represent screening as inspectable evidence and policy, not an unexplained tenant score: identify provider, report types, data sources, matching basis, criteria selected by the housing provider, fee, consent or permissible purpose, generated time, result and role in rent, deposit, guarantor or denial decisions; separate factual records from predictions; provide report access, correction, applicant context and review; preserve disputed state; and never let stale, duplicate or wrong-person data become irreversible housing loss.
- Treat the lease as an exact, navigable and revisable contract: show parties, unit, term, possession date, base rent, every mandatory recurring and one-time charge, deposit and return conditions, utilities, payment timing, late consequences, occupancy, accessibility accommodation or modification terms, maintenance duties, entry, insurance, pets, renewal, rent change, notice, termination and attachments; expose changes across versions; allow review, questions and correction before signature; and provide every signer a durable copy and receipt.
- Continue the record through move-in and residency: capture agreed condition, keys and access, inventory, existing damage, meter or utility state, deposit ledger, payment allocation, notices, maintenance requests, entry appointments, communications, accommodation requests, household changes and remedies with actor and time; distinguish emergency, habitability, routine and cosmetic work; provide accessible alternate channels; and never erase unresolved evidence when ownership, management or resident accounts change.
- Make renewal, rent change, transfer, termination and move-out explicit transitions: identify current and proposed terms, effective dates, notice and response deadlines, alternatives, required actions, inspection, access, final charges, deposit accounting, forwarding or refund destination, dispute and assistance routes; preserve the original lease and complete ledger; distinguish resident choice, provider nonrenewal, breach, property change and emergency displacement; and never use urgency, inaccessible notice or account lockout to manufacture consent or abandonment.

## Variation levers

- Increase listing-authenticity, source and payment safeguards for peer and open marketplaces.
- Increase waitlist, eligibility, subsidy and assisted-completion support for scarce or regulated housing.
- Increase unit-level accessibility detail and accommodation routes for disability-related needs.
- Increase jurisdiction, document and fee specificity where local housing rules materially differ.
- Increase ledger, notice, maintenance and handoff continuity for long-term managed properties.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Precise location and resident safety versus useful housing comparison.
- Fast reusable applications versus stale or over-broad screening evidence.
- Scarce availability urgency versus adequate review and non-coercive commitment.
- Fraud prevention versus premature collection of identity and financial documents.
- Standard lease automation versus negotiated, accommodated or jurisdiction-specific terms.
- Operational resident portals versus preservation of independent complaint and evidence routes.

## Failure modes

- A different unit or price appears after application.
- Ranking or targeting silently limits opportunity visibility.
- A fee or conditional approval is presented as a reservation.
- The application collects household and sensitive data without purpose.
- A tenant score hides wrong-person or stale evidence.
- Mandatory charges appear only inside a long lease.
- Maintenance and condition evidence disappear during account change.
- Renewal or move-out deadlines rely on inaccessible urgency.

## Anti-patterns

- Unit substitution funnel.
- Invisible housing audience.
- Application-as-hold theater.
- Household data dragnet.
- Black-box tenant score.
- Lease fee ambush.
- Resident record reset.
- Deadline-driven displacement.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does one verified unit and terms record survive from discovery through tenancy?
- Are search scope, ranking, sponsorship, total cost, freshness and opportunity visibility inspectable?
- Are inquiry, tour, application, screening, approval, holding and lease states distinct?
- Is every household and evidence request necessary, explained, accessible and correctable?
- Can applicants inspect and dispute screening evidence before it controls the outcome?
- Are all lease terms, mandatory charges, changes and signatures reviewable and durable?
- Does the resident record preserve condition, payment, maintenance, notice and accommodation evidence?
- Are renewal, termination and move-out transitions timely, accessible, contestable and fully accounted?
