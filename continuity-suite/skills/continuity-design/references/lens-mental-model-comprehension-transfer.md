# Mental models, comprehension, and transfer

## Generalized principles

- Define the minimum accurate system model a person needs to predict outcomes, detect errors, and recover: identify objects, relationships, states, rules, scope, cause and effect, limits, uncertainty, and available control; reveal that model through the work itself and test prediction rather than assuming successful completion proves understanding.
- Bridge prior knowledge deliberately: inventory familiar language, conventions, analogies, platform expectations, and likely misconceptions; preserve useful consistency, make material differences visible, and do not use familiarity or metaphor when it creates a false model of data, authority, permanence, automation, privacy, or recovery.
- Separate intrinsic task complexity from avoidable presentation burden: retain material rules and consequences while reducing split attention, unexplained terminology, memory demands, unnecessary configuration, inconsistent structure, and premature detail; externalize current state, prior choices, examples, dependencies, and remaining work.
- Teach in the context of a meaningful outcome using safe action, representative examples, visible reasoning, immediate feedback, and recoverable practice; keep guidance optional and rediscoverable, and prefer staged support that fades with demonstrated capability over tours that front-load facts before they become relevant.
- Design for transfer, not imitation: vary representative conditions, explain which principle remains invariant and which detail changes, ask people to predict and apply the model in a new case, and measure later independent use, correction, and troubleshooting rather than recall of interface steps or tutorial completion.
- Adapt guidance to prior knowledge, consequence, stress, language, cognitive access needs, and expertise without silently changing the underlying rules: offer summaries, examples, alternate representations, deeper explanation, and expert acceleration while keeping core state, consequence, help, and recovery available to everyone.
- Treat comprehension as evidence that can fail and change: test unaided explanation, prediction, action, error diagnosis, correction, interruption, resumption, and transfer with representative people; record misconceptions and counterexamples, and revise the interface model rather than blaming users for not absorbing instruction.

## Method

- Write the task model, system model, user-visible objects, states, rules, causes, consequences, limits, and recovery paths.
- Research prior knowledge, vocabulary, conventions, analogies, misconceptions, accessibility needs, stress, and expert practice.
- Map what must be perceived, understood, remembered, predicted, acted on, verified, and transferred at each stage.
- Prototype first use, representative practice, error diagnosis, interruption, resumption, changed conditions, and later independent use.
- Compare instruction, example, safe practice, contextual help, and no-guidance conditions when the learning mechanism is uncertain.
- Test explanation, prediction, transfer, correction, confidence calibration, help dependence, accessibility, and delayed performance separately.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not hide material complexity, limits, uncertainty, authority, data use, cost, or consequence in the name of simplicity.
- Do not force training before unrelated access or make tutorial completion a gate when equivalent supported action is possible.
- Do not infer competence, consent, or understanding from completion, confidence, speed, repeated exposure, or memorized steps.
- Do not use a familiar metaphor or anthropomorphic explanation when it materially misrepresents system capability or responsibility.
- Do not make help disappear permanently after dismissal or require disclosure of disability to obtain an accessible explanation.
- Do not treat user error as deficient intelligence when interface structure, language, feedback, or recovery created the misconception.

## Variation levers

- Use direct safe practice for procedural action, worked examples for unfamiliar problem solving, and inspectable models for complex system behavior.
- Offer concise point-of-use guidance plus deeper self-directed explanation rather than one mandatory level for everyone.
- Increase prediction, review, and transfer testing with consequence, novelty, automation, hidden state, or delayed effect.
- Preserve familiar conventions for routine operations while explicitly teaching product-specific differences that alter outcome.
- Fade scaffolding as demonstrated independent capability grows, but keep help, state, and recovery continuously rediscoverable.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Consistency accelerates transfer while preserving an inaccurate prior model or hiding a meaningful difference.
- Simplification reduces burden while omitting the model needed to diagnose failure or exercise control.
- Examples support initial performance while encouraging imitation without transferable understanding.
- Adaptive help improves relevance while becoming unpredictable or concealing unequal underlying behavior.
- Detailed explanation supports accuracy while increasing attention and reading burden.
- Expert acceleration improves efficiency while making novice and accessibility paths harder to maintain.

## Failure modes

- People can follow the demonstrated path but cannot predict what a changed setting, state, or input will do.
- A familiar label or metaphor implies ownership, privacy, permanence, or intelligence the system does not provide.
- A tour explains many controls before the person has a task or context for them.
- Guidance disappears after dismissal and cannot be found during later error or uncertainty.
- The happy path is simplified while recovery exposes an unexplained underlying model.
- Evaluation measures immediate completion but not delayed independent use, transfer, misconception, or correction.
- Advanced and simplified modes silently use different rules or hide consequential state.

## Anti-patterns

- Tour as understanding.
- Familiar therefore accurate.
- Completion equals competence.
- Simplify by omission.
- Tooltip as system model.
- Memorize the path.
- Help once.
- Expert mode changes the rules.
- User error without model diagnosis.

## Acceptance and review questions

- What minimum system model must a person understand to predict outcomes, detect errors, exercise control, and recover?
- Which prior conventions, terms, and analogies help, and which create material misconceptions?
- Has avoidable presentation burden been removed without hiding rules, limits, uncertainty, authority, cost, or consequence?
- Does learning occur through meaningful context, representative examples, safe action, visible reasoning, and recoverable feedback?
- Can people apply the underlying principle to a changed case rather than repeat memorized interface steps?
- Can people obtain appropriate summaries, examples, alternate representations, deeper explanation, and expert acceleration without disclosing disability?
- Does evaluation include explanation, prediction, transfer, error diagnosis, correction, interruption, resumption, accessibility, and delayed independent use?
