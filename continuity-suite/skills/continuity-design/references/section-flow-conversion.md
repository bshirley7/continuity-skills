# Conversion

## Generalized principles

- Preserve the promised outcome and included value at the point where the user chooses a plan, trial, signup, or upgrade.
- State the full commitment model before the action, including price basis, cadence, immediate charge, trial transition, renewal, and material tax or cancellation conditions.
- Compare plans through meaningful audience fit, operating model, capacity, or incremental outcomes instead of an undifferentiated feature inventory.
- Keep selected option, normalized and total price, material differences, and the resulting action in one continuous decision region.
- Provide a credible non-purchase or recovery path such as close, defer, restore, cancel, or change plan without disguising it as failure.
- Treat a trial as a future paid commitment by stating when it ends, what is charged next, how renewal works, and how the user can stop or receive notice.
- Anchor discounts and recommendations to comparable price units and visible plan differences rather than urgency or unexplained popularity.

## Variation levers

- Use side-by-side tiers when users compare operating models or audiences, and stacked options when the primary difference is billing duration.
- Show both total and normalized price when cadence changes materially affect perceived affordability.
- Use trial, direct purchase, contact, or repository actions according to the actual commitment and support model of each option.
- Place detailed terms inline when the entry offer changes automatically, and use an accessible detail layer for stable secondary conditions.
- Offer restore for prior purchases, cancel for existing plans, and close or defer for new commitments according to the user's state.
- Use recommendation labels only when the basis is clear, such as audience fit, included capacity, or comparable savings.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- More comparison detail reduces uncertainty while increasing scanning and cognitive load.
- Normalized pricing aids comparison while total charges remain necessary for informed commitment.
- A highly prominent action can clarify the next step while visually overpowering renewal or cancellation terms.
- Discount framing can make value legible while biasing users toward longer commitments.
- A non-purchase path supports agency while reducing short-term conversion rates.
- Plan recommendations simplify choice while obscuring whether the recommendation serves the user or the seller.
- Inline legal and billing terms improve transparency while making the page feel less lightweight.

## Failure modes

- The conversion action is separated from price, cadence, renewal, or trial-transition information.
- Base price, add-ons, seats, usage, and taxes are fragmented so the likely total cannot be reconstructed.
- A long benefit inventory displaces the selected plan and commitment terms.
- Monthly and annual prices use different units without a comparable total or normalized value.
- A trial is presented as free while the later charge, timing, or cancellation process is visually subordinate.
- Several plans appear different mainly because of color, recommendation badges, or discount emphasis.
- Restore, cancel, close, or defer is absent, hidden, or framed as an error.
- A recommendation label lacks an inspectable reason tied to user fit or plan value.

## Anti-patterns

- Using countdowns, scarcity, or irreversible-language pressure for a recurring subscription decision.
- Preselecting the longest commitment while hiding the current charge or renewal basis.
- Advertising a normalized periodic amount without giving the actual amount charged.
- Showing crossed-out prices or savings without a comparable baseline and cadence.
- Treating trial enrollment as separate from the paid subscription that follows it.
- Adding equal-weight trial, purchase, contact, and upgrade actions without routing by audience or readiness.
- Using a disabled or processing action without preserving the selected option and commitment summary.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can the user connect the selected option to the outcome and included value that motivated conversion?
- Are total price, normalized price, cadence, immediate charge, trial transition, and renewal distinguishable before action?
- Do plan differences express audience fit, operating model, capacity, or incremental outcomes rather than visual decoration?
- Is the selected option still visible where the user commits?
- Can a prior purchaser restore, an existing subscriber cancel or change, and a new user defer or close?
- If a trial is offered, can the user state exactly what happens and what is charged when it ends?
- Are discounts and recommendations based on comparable values and inspectable reasons?
- Would the decision remain understandable without discount badges, crossed-out prices, or urgency language?
