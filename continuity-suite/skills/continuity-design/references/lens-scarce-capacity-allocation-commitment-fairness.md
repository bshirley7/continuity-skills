# Scarce-capacity allocation, commitment, and fairness

## Generalized principles

- Define capacity and availability as scoped operational claims: identify the resource, service, location, provider, time interval and zone, eligibility, dependencies, accessibility characteristics and freshness; distinguish free, limited, tentative, held, requestable, unavailable, overbooked and confirmed states; disclose when a shown slot still requires qualification or participant acceptance; and never convert search visibility or selection into an implied guarantee.
- Use scarcity and time pressure only when they describe a real, person-relevant condition: base remaining-capacity, viewer, popularity and expiry claims on the current search scope and actual hold; state what expires and what does not; prevent resetting countdowns and vague limited labels; keep comparison and policy information available during a valid hold; and do not manufacture urgency to suppress deliberation or conceal alternatives and total cost.
- Select an allocation rule deliberately rather than defaulting to fastest-click wins: evaluate first-come, priority, lottery, batch, queue, invitation and provider-discretion models against demand, harm, urgency, gaming, bot access, connectivity, disability, work schedule and digital literacy; publish the rule and material exceptions; use identity or eligibility controls proportionately; and audit whether intermediaries, automation or privileged groups capture scarce capacity.
- Treat accessible capacity as a specific service guarantee, not a generic filter: describe the exact features needed to judge usability, offer equivalent efficiency, immediacy and convenience, hold and block scarce accessible resources under a documented rule, prevent double-booking across channels, preserve the guaranteed features through third parties and substitutions, and provide assisted or non-digital booking without imposing inferior timing, price or certainty.
- Make waitlists and standby systems intelligible allocations: state whether joining reserves nothing, what qualifies a person, the ordering or matching rule, the limits of position and time estimates, how offers are generated and delivered, offer expiry, response and decline behavior, replacement of existing bookings and exit; preserve list membership when an estimate is unavailable; avoid excessive offer spam; and measure access gains and disparities rather than fill rate alone.
- Represent commitment as a staged and reviewable state change: distinguish browsing, selection, personal hold, submitted request, tentative allocation, participant acceptance, payment authorization and confirmed booking; before the binding action, show exact resource and features, participants, local times and zones, total mandatory price, deposit, cancellation, no-show, refund and substitution terms; support error correction; and make confirmation durable, identifiable and interoperable.
- Allocate disruption risk explicitly and provide proportionate recovery: distinguish user cancellation, missed attendance, provider cancellation, overbooking and significant change; notify through chosen accessible channels; show original and changed commitments; offer suitable alternatives without preselecting away a refund or release option; preserve exact accessibility and dependency needs; restore capacity and waitlist opportunity consistently; and do not shift provider-caused failure into opaque credit, penalty or repeated user effort.
- Govern the allocation system across channels and outcomes: reconcile inventory, holds, requests, confirmations, cancellations and no-shows across first-party, third-party, staff and offline routes; define source-of-truth and stale-state handling; monitor double-booking, bot capture, abandonment, wait, false scarcity, inaccessible substitutions, price and recovery; segment results by relevant access groups; provide explanation and human review for consequential denial or loss; and revise rules when operational efficiency conflicts with equitable access.

## Method

- Define each scarce resource, service, feature, eligibility rule, dependency, time zone, status and source of truth.
- Map browsing, availability, selection, hold, request, acceptance, payment, confirmation, arrival, fulfillment, cancellation, no-show and disruption transitions.
- Choose and publish an allocation rule after assessing demand, urgency, gaming, accessibility, digital inequality and harm.
- Design exact-resource accessibility, waitlist, total-price, policy review, confirmation and assisted-channel parity.
- Test concurrency, stale inventory, holds, bots, time changes, overbooking, provider disruption, substitutions, refunds and recovery.
- Audit allocation, wait, price, certainty, denial, cancellation and recovery outcomes across channels and relevant groups.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not present a slot as guaranteed when qualification, acceptance or inventory locking remains incomplete.
- Do not use a scarcity claim or countdown that is unscoped, unverifiable or resetting.
- Do not default to first-come allocation when bots, connectivity or constant attention determine access to essential capacity.
- Do not require disabled people to call, wait longer or accept less certainty for resources others can book directly.
- Do not describe a room or service as accessible without the specific features needed to assess usability.
- Do not convert a waitlist into marketing consent or imply that an estimate is a promise.
- Do not hide mandatory price, cancellation, no-show or substitution terms until after urgency and effort accumulate.
- Do not substitute a nominally similar resource that breaks accessibility, location, provider or itinerary dependencies.
- Do not foreground vouchers, credits or rebooking while obscuring an available refund or release right.

## Variation levers

- Use exact-resource blocking when features are scarce and substitution can make the service unusable.
- Move from first-come to batch, lottery or priority allocation as demand, bot advantage and essentiality increase.
- Shorten holds when contention is high, but lengthen or suspend timing pressure for accessibility and complex review needs.
- Provide more precise wait estimates only when data quality and volatility support them; otherwise communicate membership and uncertainty.
- Increase independent review, refund prominence and assisted support as disruption cost and dependency increase.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Real-time inventory improves coordination while creating races and stale-state failures.
- Holds give one person review time while temporarily denying capacity to others.
- Priority allocation can improve need-based fairness while increasing verification burden and privacy collection.
- Precise wait estimates reduce uncertainty while becoming misleading under volatile capacity.
- Strict no-show rules protect utilization while disproportionately harming people with unstable transport, care or health conditions.
- Cross-channel access broadens inclusion while increasing inventory reconciliation and double-booking risk.

## Failure modes

- Availability lacks resource, scope, freshness, eligibility or tentative-state context.
- A generic urgency message pressures commitment without a real personal expiry.
- Automated agents capture free appointments before ordinary people can respond.
- Accessible capacity is visible but cannot be blocked or guaranteed.
- A waitlist position is displayed as if it were a stable time promise.
- Payment authorization is mistaken for participant acceptance and confirmation.
- Provider disruption changes an exact resource and hides refund or human support.
- Online and staff channels allocate the same inventory without reconciliation.
- Efficiency metrics improve while access disparities, false denials or unusable substitutions grow.

## Anti-patterns

- Search result as guarantee.
- Synthetic scarcity.
- Fastest bot wins.
- Accessible in name only.
- Queue estimate theater.
- Payment-confirmation collapse.
- Forced substitute recovery.
- Split-brain inventory.
- Utilization without equity.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are capacity, resource, service, features, eligibility, dependencies, time, zone, freshness and status explicit?
- Are scarcity, popularity and expiry claims real, scoped, nonresetting and compatible with deliberation and full price review?
- Was the allocation rule chosen and audited against demand, harm, bots, connectivity, disability, schedule and digital literacy?
- Can people reserve exact accessible features with equal timing, price, certainty and assisted-channel support?
- Do waitlists explain qualification, ordering, estimates, offers, expiry, response, replacement, exit and uncertainty?
- Are selection, hold, request, acceptance, authorization and confirmation staged with complete commitment review?
- Do disruption and recovery preserve original context, accessibility, alternatives, refunds, capacity restoration and support?
- Are inventory and outcomes governed across online, offline, staff and third-party channels with disparity and error audits?
