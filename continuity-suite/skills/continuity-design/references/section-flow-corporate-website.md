# Corporate website architecture

## Generalized principles

- Model the corporate website as a multi-audience information system: identify the primary business audience, then provide explicit routes for customers, partners, investors, media, candidates, communities, and existing users without giving every audience equal top-level weight.
- Keep a stable institutional hierarchy across header, homepage, footer, search, and subsites, using the same language for products or capabilities, solutions, industries, resources, company, and audience-specific destinations.
- Use the homepage to establish the institutional proposition and prioritize a small number of next destinations, then connect capability or product routes, audience or industry routes, current evidence, and corporate context instead of reproducing the full site map.
- Allow investor relations, newsroom, careers, impact, and other stakeholder systems to use local navigation and task-specific content while preserving corporate identity, global context, and a clear return to the parent site.
- Treat the footer as a recovery map and architecture checksum: group destinations by meaning and audience, distinguish legal, support, status, social, and external routes, and prioritize common recovery paths over exhaustive duplication.
- Give investor audiences a governed disclosure system with dated news, events, filings, financials, governance, stock information, direct contact, and subscriptions that let readers select the update types they need.
- Connect the employer proposition to practical candidate evidence, teams or locations, benefits and inclusion, candidate questions, and a searchable role inventory rather than separating culture storytelling from current openings.
- Make newsroom, sustainability, governance, and impact content inspectable through date, reporting period, category, metric definition, accountable contact, archive continuity, and a route to the underlying report or release.
- Preserve global and local context through visible region or language state, local entity or office identity, address and contact detail, and a safe route between local and global content.
- Place proof and engagement actions in the audience context they support, distinguishing exploration, direct conversion, account access, support, media contact, investor requests, applications, and office contact rather than using one generic corporate call to action.

## Variation levers

- Use one integrated domain when audiences share the same brand and taxonomy, or a dedicated subsite when investor, careers, newsroom, support, or regional tasks require specialized navigation, governance, and publishing workflows.
- Use product or capability as the primary route when the offer is concrete, audience or industry routes when context changes the offer materially, and institutional routes when mission, research, or stakeholder information is the primary purpose.
- Use a compact header with expandable groups for broad corporate systems, or a simple visible header for focused units and microsites with few destinations.
- Expose a deep recovery footer when the site supports many stable destinations, and use progressive disclosure when duplicating the full architecture would overwhelm scanning or mobile use.
- Give current reports, releases, roles, or insights homepage prominence when timeliness matters, and use evergreen capability or institutional pathways when the site changes less frequently.
- Use local navigation bars for stakeholder subsites, section-level tabs for peer content types, and breadcrumbs or parent links for ancestry and cross-site return.
- Use regional selectors when content, regulation, language, or contacts vary materially, and use a simple office directory when the global content remains shared.
- Use one contextual engagement path per audience task, with lower-commitment exploration available before sales, application, investor, media, or support contact.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Comprehensive audience coverage supports corporate legitimacy while making the global navigation difficult to scan.
- Independent stakeholder subsites support specialized tasks while fragmenting taxonomy, branding, analytics, accessibility, and cross-site recovery.
- Product conversion actions serve commercial goals while competing with investor, media, careers, support, and institutional audiences.
- A large recovery footer exposes the whole system while becoming an unprioritized dumping ground for legacy destinations.
- Global consistency strengthens the institution while weakening local relevance, legal accuracy, language, and contact utility.
- Current reports, news, and campaigns demonstrate activity while displacing stable explanations of the organization and its offer.
- Impact metrics support accountability while becoming promotional when periods, methods, ownership, and underlying reports are absent.
- Distinctive brand expression supports recognition while reducing accessibility and information scent when navigation depends on motion, abstraction, or hidden controls.

## Failure modes

- The global header treats products, audiences, corporate stakeholders, account actions, support, and conversion as one undifferentiated destination list.
- Header, homepage, footer, and subsites use different labels or groupings for the same products, capabilities, industries, resources, or company topics.
- The homepage becomes a compressed site map and gives no clear priority to the institutional proposition or the visitor's likely next destination.
- Investor, newsroom, careers, impact, or regional content loses corporate identity or provides no reliable route back to the parent site.
- The footer duplicates every destination without distinguishing recovery, legal, support, status, social, stakeholder, and external links.
- Investor content lacks dates, filings, events, governance, financials, contact, update preferences, or accountable disclosure structure.
- Careers content presents culture imagery without practical candidate questions, team or location context, benefits, inclusion, current roles, or job search.
- Impact and newsroom claims omit period, category, metric basis, report source, contact, archive, or current regional scope.
- Office or regional pages list place names without local entity, address, contact, language, jurisdiction, or a route to global context.
- The same generic contact action is used for sales, support, media, investors, candidates, partners, and local offices.

## Anti-patterns

- Adding a global navigation item for every internal department, content type, campaign, and audience request.
- Using Company as an unstructured container for about, leadership, careers, investors, newsroom, sustainability, offices, legal, and support.
- Repeating the entire mega-menu in the homepage body instead of choosing a small set of prioritized pathways.
- Launching a visually distinct investor or careers subsite with no shared identity, current parent link, or consistent destination language.
- Treating the footer as a warehouse for every legacy URL and campaign rather than a tested recovery system.
- Displaying investor logos as proof without giving actual investors a governed route to filings, events, financials, and contact.
- Showing an employer lifestyle gallery with no route to teams, locations, candidate expectations, or searchable open roles.
- Publishing sustainability totals without reporting period, calculation method, accountable owner, or the underlying report.
- Using a map or country flag as a substitute for local entity, language, address, jurisdiction, and contact context.
- Sending sales, support, press, investor, candidate, partner, and community inquiries through one generic form.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Does the corporate hierarchy identify the primary business audience while providing explicit, appropriately weighted routes for other stakeholders?
- Do header, homepage, footer, search, and subsites use one stable language system for products or capabilities, solutions, industries, resources, company, and audiences?
- Does the homepage establish the institution and prioritize a small number of next destinations instead of reproducing the complete site map?
- Can users move into and back from investor, newsroom, careers, impact, support, and regional systems without losing corporate identity or context?
- Does the footer function as a grouped recovery map with legal, support, status, social, stakeholder, and external routes clearly distinguished?
- Does investor relations expose dated disclosures, events, filings, financials, governance, stock context, contact, and selectable update subscriptions?
- Does the careers system connect employer proposition and candidate evidence to teams, locations, practical questions, benefits, inclusion, searchable roles, and application?
- Do newsroom, sustainability, governance, and impact pages expose period, category, metric basis, ownership, contact, archives, and underlying reports?
- Do global and local routes identify current region or language, local entity or office, address and contact details, and a safe return to global context?
- Are sales, account, support, media, investor, candidate, partner, and office actions distinct and proportionate to the current audience task?
