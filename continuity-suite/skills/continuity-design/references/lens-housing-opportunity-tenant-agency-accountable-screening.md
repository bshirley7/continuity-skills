# Housing opportunity, tenant agency, and accountable screening

## Generalized principles

- Evaluate opportunity visibility before application conversion: identify who can see each housing advertisement and listing, which geography, audience, filters, ranking, sponsorship, recommendation or delivery system shaped exposure, what protected characteristics or proxies may be implicated, and whether equivalent people receive materially different opportunities; monitor delivered audiences and search outcomes, not only advertiser settings; preserve broad discoverability and user-controlled criteria; and never treat engagement optimization as a neutral housing-allocation mechanism.
- Require property, provider and price truth before urgency: verify or qualify owner and manager authority, exact unit, availability, listing source, duplicate or copied content, material property and accessibility claims, advertised rent, every mandatory recurring and one-time charge, deposit, utilities, fee purpose and refundability; show changes and total cost before inquiry data, application fees or holding money; provide safe reporting and independent contact routes; and never use scarcity, below-market price or irreversible payment to substitute for verification.
- Evaluate screening criteria for housing relevance, necessity and less-exclusionary alternatives: document the legitimate tenancy interest, exact criterion, lookback, threshold, context, population, evidence of relationship to tenancy obligations and disparate effects; distinguish records, allegations, outcomes and predictions; avoid blanket exclusions and overbroad proxies; support individualized context where required or appropriate; and reassess when data quality, housing conditions, policy or observed impact changes.
- Protect applicant agency in data and fee collection: state criteria, report types, providers, purpose, recipients, retention, application and screening fees, refundability, reuse, decision role and likely timing before collection or payment; request only stage-necessary information; support alternative evidence and assisted completion; separate required application data from marketing, unrelated profiling or future use; preserve drafts, review and withdrawal; and do not call participation voluntary when housing access depends on it.
- Make accommodation and accessibility part of equal housing opportunity: provide perceivable, operable, understandable and robust discovery, application, payment, notice and resident channels; disclose relevant process and property information; offer a confidential route to request effective changes to rules, policies, practices, services or communication; distinguish accommodation from modification and from screening exceptions; preserve interactive resolution and alternatives; and never penalize, expose or delay an applicant merely for requesting equal access.
- Give screening evidence and adverse terms a usable correction path: identify the housing provider's decision separately from the reporting company, report and score source, generated date, matching identifiers, key factors, criteria, and whether the information affected denial, rent, deposit, guarantor, waitlist or renewal; provide durable notice, free-report route, accessible dispute, applicant statement and responsible contact; preserve disputed status and time-sensitive housing context; and never make wrong-person, duplicate, outdated or incomplete records effectively irreversible.
- Evaluate lease and residency terms as an ongoing power relationship, not a one-time checkbox: make exact rent and all charges, deposit handling, utilities, payment allocation, occupancy, entry, maintenance, accommodation, renewal, rent change, termination, notice, move-out and dispute terms navigable before signature and throughout tenancy; preserve versions, receipts, condition and communications; offer accessible independent help; and prevent account suspension, management transfer or document replacement from erasing tenant evidence or remedy.
- Audit access, burden, conditional terms and displacement across the complete housing funnel: measure who receives ads, views, inquires, tours, starts, pays, abandons, requests accommodation, completes, is screened, receives conditional terms, is denied, disputes, signs, renews or exits; compare timing, error, total fees, deposit, rent, guarantor and remedy outcomes across relevant groups and intersections while protecting privacy; include discouraged and unserved people; test less-discriminatory alternatives; and never use approval rate or occupancy alone as proof of fairness.

## Method

- Trace opportunity delivery through search, listing, inquiry, application, screening, lease, residency and exit.
- Separate property facts, provider claims, estimates, reports, scores, policies and decisions.
- Inventory every applicant data request, fee, recipient, retention period and downstream use.
- Test wrong-person records, stale listings, unit substitution, hidden fees and copied-listing fraud.
- Walk screen-reader, keyboard, low-bandwidth, language, time-pressure and accommodation scenarios.
- Compare approval, conditional terms and remedy for equivalent applicants across pathways.
- Verify that screening disputes can matter before a time-sensitive opportunity disappears.
- Audit exposure, burden, terms, errors, remedies, tenancy continuity and displacement together.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not optimize housing ad delivery or ranking without monitoring unequal opportunity.
- Do not collect sensitive identity or money before provider and unit legitimacy are established.
- Do not use blanket screening rules or opaque scores as substitutes for housing-relevant judgment.
- Do not penalize disability accommodation, alternative evidence or assisted completion.
- Do not hide mandatory charges until after application fees or lease commitment.
- Do not let a reporting company become the unaccountable author of the housing decision.
- Do not erase condition, payment, notice or maintenance evidence during account transitions.
- Do not optimize occupancy or time-to-lease while hiding exclusion, error or displacement.

## Variation levers

- Increase advertising and ranking audits where platforms personalize or target housing opportunities.
- Increase authenticity, payment and identity safeguards for peer-to-peer and fragmented listings.
- Increase individualized review and correction when screening includes criminal, eviction or predictive data.
- Increase accommodation, language and assisted routes for public, subsidized or otherwise scarce housing.
- Increase ledger, notice and independent-remedy continuity as tenancy duration and provider power increase.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Personalized discovery versus equal visibility of housing opportunities.
- Fast screening versus relevance, accuracy and individualized context.
- Fraud prevention versus premature sensitive-data collection.
- Standard policies versus disability accommodation and alternative evidence.
- Time-sensitive availability versus meaningful correction and review.
- Fairness monitoring versus privacy and risk from demographic data.

## Failure modes

- Delivery optimization hides housing from protected groups.
- A copied listing collects identity or deposits.
- An overbroad criterion excludes without tenancy relevance.
- Application pressure converts optional data into coerced disclosure.
- An inaccessible process becomes a screening instrument.
- Wrong-person records trigger higher terms before correction.
- A checkbox hides changing obligations throughout tenancy.
- Approval averages conceal unequal visibility, fees and conditional terms.

## Anti-patterns

- Engagement-gated housing opportunity.
- Deposit-before-verification.
- Tenant-score essentialism.
- Consent-by-housing-need.
- Accommodation friction test.
- Adverse-term opacity.
- Lease-checkbox governance.
- Occupancy-as-fairness metric.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can equivalent people discover equivalent housing opportunities across targeting and ranking?
- Are provider, unit, availability, total cost and payment legitimacy established before urgency?
- Is every screening criterion relevant, necessary, evidence-based and tested for less-exclusionary alternatives?
- Are applicant data and fees purpose-bound, proportionate, reviewable and non-coercive?
- Can disabled people access the complete process and obtain effective accommodation without penalty?
- Can screening evidence and conditional terms be inspected and corrected before housing is lost?
- Are lease and resident obligations, records, notices and remedies continuously available?
- Are visibility, burden, conditional terms, errors, remedies and displacement audited beyond approval rates?
