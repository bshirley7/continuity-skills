# Consent and privacy controls

## Generalized principles

- Separate consent by specific purpose, data category, recipient, duration, and consequence; distinguish required processing from optional personalization, advertising, improvement, sharing, and support access.
- Present accept, refuse, essential-only, and customize as recognizable choices with comparable clarity and effort; disclose material effects of refusal without framing privacy protection as punishment.
- Make consent durable and revisitable through visible current state, later settings, withdrawal, export, deletion, saved confirmation, and any processing or data that necessarily remains after a request.
- For sensitive or delegated access, name the data source, receiving actor, purpose, scope, duration, authority, security handling, current grant state, expiry, and revoke path before access begins.

## Variation levers

- Use a concise first layer for purpose and choice, with inspectable details for categories and recipients.
- Use per-purpose controls for optional processing and grouped essential processing only when separation is not meaningful.
- Add positive friction for sensitive or delegated access when it supports comprehension rather than obstruction.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Granular controls improve agency while increasing decision burden.
- Explaining refusal consequences supports informed choice while creating pressure.
- Layered disclosure reduces initial density while risking hidden material information.

## Failure modes

- Accept is prominent while refusal is hidden behind configuration.
- One toggle combines unrelated purposes or recipients.
- Withdrawal changes the control but does not confirm effective processing state.
- Deletion language obscures operational data that must remain.

## Anti-patterns

- Consent by continuation.
- Accept all or hunt through settings.
- Privacy theater toggle.
- Refusal as degraded styling.
- Support access without expiry.
- Delete data without retained-data disclosure.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are purpose, data, recipient, duration, and consequence specific?
- Can users accept, refuse, choose essentials, or customize with comparable clarity?
- Are refusal effects factual and proportionate?
- Can consent be reviewed and withdrawn later?
- Do export and deletion confirm scope and retained data?
- Is delegated access scoped, time-bound, visible, and revocable?
