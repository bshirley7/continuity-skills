# Complex forms and review

## Generalized principles

- Structure the form around the user's information-gathering and decision sequence: group cohesive questions, state why sensitive or unusual data is needed, distinguish required and optional input, and reveal conditional questions only when prior answers make them relevant.
- Keep progress truthful and recoverable through stable section names, current and completed state, save status, safe back navigation, resumable drafts, retained answers, and an honest distinction between section completion, validation, review, submission, and downstream acceptance.
- Prevent errors with appropriate controls, examples, constraints, tolerant formats, document requirements, and contextual help; when errors remain, retain valid input and identify what happened, where, why, and how to fix it at both summary and field level.
- Before consequential submission, provide a complete, scannable review grouped by the original sections, mark missing or skipped answers, offer precise edit routes that return to review, and label the final action by its actual commitment.
- After submission, provide durable evidence of what was sent, when, to whom or which system, current processing state, reference identity, next steps, expected timing, correction or withdrawal routes, and access to a safe record.

## Variation levers

- Use one question per page for complex branching or high cognitive load and grouped pages for short, tightly related, low-risk inputs.
- Use simple step counts only when the total is stable; use named sections or task lists when branching changes length or work can be completed nonlinearly.
- Increase review, confirmation, record keeping, and correction windows as legal, financial, employment, health, identity, or irreversible consequence rises.
- Autosave ordinary drafts while making file upload, verification, submission, and external processing states explicit.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Smaller steps reduce local load while obscuring total effort and relationships among answers.
- Conditional disclosure reduces clutter while making overall scope and progress harder to predict.
- Early validation prevents accumulation while interrupting entry when triggered too aggressively.
- Autosave reduces loss while creating ambiguity about draft, submitted, and accepted state.
- Comprehensive review improves confidence while becoming difficult to scan in very long transactions.

## Failure modes

- The form asks for information before explaining purpose, preparation, or requirements.
- Progress implies a stable total even though conditional paths can expand it.
- Back navigation, timeout, or validation clears valid answers or uploaded evidence.
- Errors rely on color, generic wording, or a disabled submit button with no correction path.
- Editing from review forces the user through every subsequent page again.
- A success message confirms receipt without submission record, processing state, timing, or next steps.

## Anti-patterns

- Questionnaire as database schema.
- False percent complete.
- Validation amnesia.
- Disabled with no explanation.
- Edit means restart.
- Submitted into a void.
- Autosaved, therefore submitted.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Are questions grouped by user reasoning, with purpose, requirements, and conditional relevance visible?
- Are progress, saved draft, validation, review, submission, and acceptance clearly distinct?
- Do back, resume, timeout, validation, and upload failure preserve valid work?
- Can every error be found, understood, corrected, and announced without relying on color?
- Does review show all relevant answers and allow targeted editing that returns to review?
- Does completion provide a record, recipient, time, status, reference, next step, and correction route?
