# Sustainable engagement, wellbeing, and autonomy

## Generalized principles

- Evaluate engagement by the quality and distribution of outcomes, not time or retention alone: identify meaningful benefit, active versus passive use, content and interaction type, continuous duration, time of day, sleep, movement, distress, social connection, work and learning demands, accessibility, vulnerability and opportunity displaced; preserve uncertainty and individual variation; and never infer addiction, health or moral worth from one usage total.
- Design for finite, comprehensible and self-directed engagement: provide natural stopping points, visible progress and time, non-personalized or lower-intensity alternatives, notification and recommendation control, quiet periods, session intentions, pause and exit; remove infinite progression, autoplay, urgency, variable rewards and social penalties where they undermine reflective choice; and ensure a boundary remains usable during fatigue, distress or reduced self-regulation.
- Protect sleep, recovery, movement, social connection and non-digital life without treating technology as inherently harmful: match prompts and limits to context, allow beneficial communication, education, creativity, accessibility and support, preserve emergency paths, avoid shame, and make rest or temporary absence safe from streak loss, ranking decline, financial pressure, workplace penalty or relationship punishment.
- Address structural causes before transferring responsibility to the individual: assess recommender objectives, notification generation, business incentives, workload, staffing, response norms, ranking, monetization and default availability; use organizational and product-level controls before relying on reminders or willpower; give workers and users meaningful voice; and do not market a wellbeing control while the surrounding system is optimized to defeat it.
- Treat wellbeing interventions as testable and revisable: state the hypothesized benefit and affected groups, measure intended and unintended outcomes over an appropriate period, examine displacement, workarounds, inequity, accessibility and delayed effects, protect sensitive health and usage data, support correction and withdrawal, enable independent assessment, and stop or redesign interventions that increase shame, exclusion, surveillance, isolation or hidden harm.

## Method

- Map how the product creates, extends and resumes engagement across content, recommendations, notifications, social feedback, scarcity, workload and commercial incentives.
- Define meaningful benefit and potential effects on sleep, movement, distress, connection, work, learning, accessibility and displaced activity for relevant groups.
- Test whether natural stopping, lower-intensity modes, pause, exit and recovery remain understandable and usable under fatigue or reduced self-regulation.
- Apply structural product and organizational controls before adding individual reminders, goals or self-discipline prompts.
- Evaluate outcomes and distribution over time, protect sensitive evidence and revise or stop mechanisms that undermine wellbeing or autonomy.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not diagnose addiction, mental illness or wellbeing from time spent, session count or one behavioral signal.
- Do not use wellbeing settings as evidence that infinite scroll, autoplay, variable rewards, manipulative notifications or exploitative workload are acceptable.
- Do not shame people for continued use, override, relapse, disability, caregiving, work demands or use of digital support.
- Do not make sleep, rest, absence or boundaries cause irreversible streak, status, income, access or relationship loss.
- Do not expose usage, focus, distress, sleep or self-exclusion data to employers, family, advertisers or peers beyond a specific understood purpose.
- Do not suppress emergency, safety, care or direct human communication under a generic focus rule without a clear alternative.
- Do not frame individual restraint as the remedy for organizational overload, understaffing or expected constant availability.
- Do not optimize a wellbeing intervention only for reduced use when it increases isolation, exclusion, unsafe migration or loss of beneficial access.
- Do not use personalized vulnerability, distress or fatigue to time re-entry, commerce or engagement prompts.

## Variation levers

- Use stronger structural limits and independent evaluation as vulnerability, compulsivity, financial consequence, sleep disruption or continuous exposure risk increases.
- Use natural task or content completion when possible and time-based interruption when progression otherwise lacks a meaningful end.
- Allow individual, family, team and organization boundaries only within their legitimate authority and make conflicts and effective precedence visible.
- Adapt time representation, prompts, friction and alternatives for disability, neurodivergence, shift work, caregiving, crisis and variable schedules.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Strong limits protect prior intent while blocking urgent, beneficial or accessibility-supporting use.
- Personalized support can improve relevance while inferring sensitive vulnerability and timing intervention manipulatively.
- Social accountability can support boundaries while creating surveillance, stigma and dependence.
- Reduced engagement may improve recovery while weakening social support, livelihood, education or civic access.
- Natural stopping points reduce compulsion while business and workplace incentives reward continuous availability.

## Failure modes

- Success is defined only as lower time, higher retention or fewer sessions.
- A wellbeing prompt appears after a long session but the primary action immediately resumes personalized content.
- The user can set a limit while autoplay, infinite progression, notifications and streak penalties remain unchanged.
- A dashboard moralizes use without distinguishing purpose, context, benefit or displaced activity.
- Quiet time makes safety, caregiving or direct communication inaccessible.
- A workplace asks employees to set focus boundaries while preserving impossible workload and response norms.
- Sensitive usage or distress signals become targeting, performance or household-surveillance data.
- Evaluation ignores disabled, neurodivergent, young, shift-working, low-income or socially isolated groups.
- A pause causes loss of rank, earnings, access, progress or relationships that pressures immediate return.
- The intervention reduces measured time while increasing distress, isolation, workarounds or migration to less safe services.

## Anti-patterns

- Time is diagnosis.
- Reminder legitimizes capture.
- Limit beside infinity.
- Moralized dashboard.
- Focus blocks safety.
- Self-care fixes overload.
- Wellbeing data for targeting.
- Average user wellbeing.
- Rest causes loss.
- Less use, hidden harm.

## Acceptance and review questions

- What meaningful benefit is supported, and what do active versus passive use, content, duration, time, sleep, movement, distress, connection, workload and displacement show without diagnosing from time alone?
- Does the experience provide finite progression, natural stopping, visible time, lower-intensity alternatives, notification and recommendation control, pause and exit?
- Can people rest or become unavailable without shame, irreversible loss, social penalty, financial pressure or blocked safety communication?
- Which recommender, notification, workload, business and social mechanisms create unwanted engagement, and were structural controls applied before self-discipline prompts?
- Are vulnerable and differently situated groups included, and are usage and wellbeing data protected from targeting, employment and household surveillance?
- Did evaluation examine both benefit and harm over time, including displacement, workarounds, isolation, exclusion and delayed effects?
