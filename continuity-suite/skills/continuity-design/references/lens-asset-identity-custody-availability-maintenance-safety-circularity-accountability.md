# Asset identity, custody, availability, maintenance, safety, circularity, and accountability

## Generalized principles

- Evaluate asset identity as a versioned evidence graph: can people distinguish definition, model and variant from lot, batch and serialized instance; identify owner, title, manufacturer, supplier, location, custodian, condition, serviceability, risk class, financial class and current state; trace each lifecycle event and correction; and prevent a SKU, label, person, room, ledger line or imported row from becoming identity by convenience?
- Audit quantity and availability as time-bounded, unit-safe claims: define unit and conversion, exact item and location, cutoff, expected and observed source, on-hand, reserved, committed, in-transit, quarantined, damaged and available states, uncertainty and reconciliation; preserve counts, adjustments and prior values; and never let one editable total, financial balance or successful scan stand in for physical truth.
- Evaluate custody as accepted responsibility with bounded privacy: distinguish ownership, location, possession, authorized use, stewardship and liability; record exact handoff, condition, time, parties, route, due or return terms and exceptions; provide correction and dispute; minimize telemetry and access; and never infer custody or fault from an assignee field, address, badge scan, device signal or last-known location.
- Test availability against fitness, safety and accessibility: can people identify intended use and user needs, condition, inspection, calibration, training, compatibility, accommodations, restrictions, recall and quarantine; can they refuse or report unsafe or unsuitable equipment without penalty; and does the system prevent stock, assignment, utilization or accounting value from overriding serviceability?
- Audit maintenance as planned risk control and accountable work: bind the exact asset, trigger, hazard, competent person, procedure, energy isolation, parts, findings, action, downtime, test, record, next interval and residual restriction; preserve worker reports and recurring failure; separate work complete from independent or authorized return to service; and never treat a due-date reminder, checked task or technician status as safety evidence.
- Evaluate exception containment across identity and distribution: for loss, theft, damage, defect or recall, identify affected instances, lots, locations, custodians and downstream transfers; expose risk, stop-use or quarantine action, notification, recovery or search, remedy, authority and closure; preserve disagreement and false positives; and never make an exception disappear through adjustment, replacement, write-off or archive.
- Test inventory and asset controls for accessible, recoverable operation: provide programmatic labels and state, keyboard and non-camera alternatives, clear units and instructions, duplicate and range checks, review before consequential changes, status announcements, offline-safe queues, bounded bulk scope, undo or correction and preserved audit history; and never make color, spatial position, scanner success or pointer-only interaction the sole control.
- Evaluate end-of-life as authorized circular disposition: determine continued need, repairability, redeployment, transfer, donation, resale, parts recovery, sanitization, recycling, hazardous treatment and destruction; preserve title, condition, residual and social value, data sensitivity, recipient or processor, chain of custody and verified result; and never let depreciation, age, write-off or archive prove that reuse was considered or disposal completed safely.
- Audit data-bearing asset disposition independently from physical disposal: classify media and information sensitivity, select risk-appropriate sanitization, preserve authorization and method, verify the result and exceptions, control transport and custody, bind any reuse or destruction certificate to the exact media, and never infer secure erase from reset, deletion, recycling pickup, device archive or generic vendor assurance.
- Evaluate lifecycle performance as value, resilience and burden rather than maximum utilization: combine mission or user outcome, availability, downtime, safety, inspection and repair quality, loss, accessibility, worker effort and control, equitable allocation, useful-life extension, reuse, recovery and environmental effect; segment harms and benefits; and never reward lean stock, high utilization, low book value or rapid disposal when they externalize risk or maintenance debt.

## Method

- Trace one exact item, lot or serialized asset from need and acquisition through catalog, receipt, acceptance, storage, allocation, custody, use, maintenance, transfer, incident, retirement and final disposition.
- Separate owner, title holder, supplier, custodian, user, location manager, inventory counter, maintenance technician, safety verifier, security authority, disposal authority, recipient and processor roles.
- Reconstruct identity, quantity, availability, condition, serviceability, value, custody and disposal claims from source events, units, time, authority and current uncertainty.
- Walk ordinary stock, distributed warehouse, shared tool, employee device, safety-critical machine, data-bearing media, recalled lot, lost asset, accessibility accommodation, repairable item and hazardous end-of-life scenarios.
- Test duplicate IDs, unit conversion, partial receipt, blind count, stale location, refused handoff, offline scanning, failed inspection, recurring repair, recall propagation, missing sanitization evidence and disputed disposal.
- Compare dashboard health and accounting totals with physical truth, safe availability, equitable access, maintenance burden, resilience, worker agency and verified circular outcome.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not turn asset location, device telemetry, scan history or assignment records into worker surveillance or performance scoring.
- Do not expose disability, health, accommodation or sensitive job information to explain equipment assignment or priority.
- Do not automate custody, loss liability, unsafe-use, write-off or disposal decisions without inspectable evidence, correction and accountable review.
- Do not make barcode, camera, color, drag, pointer precision or a particular device the only way to count, receive, inspect or transfer assets.
- Do not permit a stock, available, in-use, repaired or green state to override unresolved safety, recall, quarantine or inspection evidence.
- Do not pressure workers to accept damaged, unsuitable or inaccessible equipment or personally absorb system maintenance and replacement burden.
- Do not erase missing, damaged, stolen, recalled or disputed assets through quantity adjustment, archive or reassignment.
- Do not claim reuse, sanitization, recycling or compliant disposal without exact chain-of-custody and completion evidence.

## Variation levers

- Increase traceability, lot, expiry, environmental-condition and recall controls for regulated or perishable inventory.
- Increase privacy, security, sanitization and remote-custody controls for workforce devices and data-bearing media.
- Increase competence, isolation, inspection, calibration and independent return-to-service controls for safety-critical equipment.
- Increase count frequency, location precision, replenishment, reservation and transfer controls for distributed stock.
- Increase title, public value, reuse priority, disposal authority and audit controls for public or grant-funded assets.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Precise traceability versus privacy and worker surveillance.
- Fast stock updates versus reconstructable physical truth.
- High availability versus quarantine, resilience and safety.
- Central control versus local reporting and worker agency.
- Rapid return to service versus competent verification.
- Lowest short-term cost versus repair, reuse and circular value.

## Failure modes

- Asset identity collapses into a SKU, person or ledger line.
- An editable total hides unit, location and count provenance.
- Assignment or telemetry becomes presumed custody and fault.
- In-stock or high-utilization status overrides fitness and accessibility.
- Maintenance completion lacks isolation, test or return authority.
- Recall, loss or damage disappears through adjustment or archive.
- Scanning and dense tables exclude alternative operation.
- Write-off is mistaken for sanitization, recycling or disposal.

## Anti-patterns

- Convenience identity.
- Magic quantity.
- Surveillance custody.
- Available means safe.
- Maintenance checkbox.
- Exception erasure.
- Scanner-only control.
- Archive certificate.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can one asset be traced without confusing definition, lot, serial instance, owner, location, custodian, condition or state?
- Are quantity and availability claims bound to unit, location, time, source, allocation state and reconciliation evidence?
- Does custody require accepted handoff while minimizing surveillance and supporting dispute?
- Can people identify and refuse unsafe, unsuitable, inaccessible, recalled or uninspected equipment?
- Does maintenance preserve competence, isolation, work, findings, test, downtime and return-to-service authority?
- Do loss, damage, theft and recall propagate containment and remedy without erasing history?
- Can consequential inventory work be completed and corrected without camera, color, drag or pointer-only interaction?
- Does end-of-life prioritize repair, reuse and transfer while preserving title, recipient, processor and verified outcome?
- Is media sanitization risk-based, authorized, verified and bound to the exact data-bearing asset?
- Do metrics reveal safety, accessibility, resilience, burden, useful-life extension and circular recovery alongside cost?
