# Project planning, task execution, dependency, delivery, and learning lifecycle

## Generalized principles

- Preserve one versioned project identity across the lifecycle: identify intended outcome and beneficiaries, sponsor and accountable lead, team and stakeholders, scope and exclusions, assumptions, constraints, success measures, delivery approach, baseline dates, budget or capacity and current revision; bind milestones, tasks, dependencies, risks, decisions, changes, evidence, delivery, closure and learning to it; and never let a board, folder, status color or task total become the project definition.
- Plan from outcome to evidence before accumulating tasks: define the user or operational change sought, baseline, observable success, deliverables, acceptance method, non-goals and uncertainty; decompose only enough to expose sequencing, ownership and risk; keep discovery and later planning revisable; and never treat a complete-looking backlog as proof that the work is necessary, feasible or correctly framed.
- Make every work item self-describing and outcome-linked: show project and milestone, exact objective or acceptance contribution, owner and collaborators, status semantics, priority basis, estimate and confidence, start and target dates, dependencies, evidence, decisions and current blocker; preserve history and source context across views; and never let title, assignee, due date and checkbox be the whole task contract.
- Represent dependencies as accountable relationships: identify predecessor and successor, dependency type, responsible teams, needed artifact or decision, promised and forecast dates, confidence, lag, impact, handoff evidence and escalation path; distinguish external, approval, resource and technical constraints; recalculate affected plans when evidence changes; and never use a connector line or blocked badge without explaining cause and recovery.
- Treat estimates and schedules as revisable forecasts with provenance: show estimator, method, assumptions, range or confidence, calendar and availability, dependency logic, baseline, current forecast, actuals and reason for change; separate target, commitment and prediction; surface critical or near-critical effects; and never punish transparent re-estimation or silently rewrite history to make delivery look on time.
- Make workload a sustainable capacity model rather than a task count: combine time-bounded availability, work estimates and uncertainty, skill and role fit, meetings and support load, interruptions, dependency coordination, leave, accessibility and accommodations, unassigned work and work in progress; let people challenge and rebalance assignments; and never convert overload into an individual productivity defect or expose sensitive personal context unnecessarily.
- Keep blocked, at-risk, paused and cancelled states explanatory and actionable: show trigger, affected outcome and work, evidence, owner, dependency, duration, decision needed, options, escalation, next review and residual consequence; preserve work and history; distinguish deliberate stop from failure and waiting from active effort; and never hide systemic constraint behind overdue status or repeated reassignment.
- Report progress as changed evidence against outcome and baseline: show delivered and accepted results, work added or removed, milestone and schedule movement, active risk, decisions, capacity and quality signals, forecast and confidence, help needed and next meaningful action; preserve prior reports and corrections; and never substitute task completion percentage, activity volume or a green label for credible outcome progress.
- Separate done, delivered, reviewed, accepted, released, adopted and outcome-realized: bind each transition to exact artifact, criteria, reviewer or beneficiary, evidence, date, environment and unresolved exception; support rejection, correction and partial acceptance; close remaining access, financial, operational and records obligations; and never let a checked task, merged change, sent file or payment prove project completion.
- Turn reflection into versioned learning and owned change: create psychologically safer individual and group input, separate observation from interpretation, identify contributing conditions rather than blame, connect findings to exact events and measures, record decisions and experiments, assign bounded actions and review dates, and verify whether future work changed; preserve unresolved tensions; and never treat a retrospective meeting, sentiment board or archived project as learning completed.

## Variation levers

- Increase baseline, critical-path, cost, assurance and change-control evidence for large predictive projects.
- Increase experiment, user evidence, short feedback and adaptive-scope controls for iterative product work.
- Increase client approval, commercial dependency, deliverable acceptance and payment-state controls for external services.
- Increase safety, quality, incident, regulatory and independent-verification controls for high-consequence delivery.
- Increase cross-team dependency, portfolio capacity and outcome tradeoff controls for programmes and shared platforms.

## Tensions and tradeoffs

- Complete upfront planning versus learning-driven revision.
- Simple task views versus outcome, evidence and dependency context.
- Visible accountability versus surveillance and blame.
- Stable commitments versus honest forecast uncertainty.
- High utilization versus sustainable flow and recovery capacity.
- Fast closure versus acceptance, adoption and durable learning.

## Failure modes

- A board or backlog becomes the project definition.
- Tasks lack outcome, acceptance or evidence context.
- Dependency lines omit owners, impact and recovery.
- Forecast dates are presented as certainty or silently rewritten.
- Task counts classify overloaded people as available.
- Blocked work becomes an individual overdue problem.
- Completion percentage replaces credible outcome reporting.
- A retrospective produces notes but no verified change.

## Anti-patterns

- Backlog as strategy.
- Title-owner-date task.
- Connector-line dependency.
- Deadline theater.
- Utilization as capacity.
- Red badge blame.
- Green dashboard project.
- Retrospective archive.

## Acceptance and review questions

- Can people identify outcome, beneficiaries, ownership, scope, assumptions, success measures, baseline and current project revision?
- Does planning connect outcome, evidence, deliverables, acceptance, non-goals and uncertainty before task volume?
- Does each work item expose purpose, owner, status, estimate, dates, dependencies, evidence and blocker across views?
- Are dependency type, parties, artifact, dates, confidence, impact, handoff and escalation inspectable?
- Are targets, commitments, forecasts, assumptions, actuals and schedule changes represented separately?
- Does workload include time, uncertainty, role fit, support load, interruption, leave, accommodations and unassigned work?
- Do blocked, at-risk, paused and cancelled states expose cause, consequence, owner, options and next review?
- Does progress report changed evidence, quality, risk, decisions and forecast against outcome and baseline?
- Are done, delivered, reviewed, accepted, released, adopted and outcome-realized distinct?
- Do retrospective findings create owned experiments whose effect is reviewed in later work?
